> Due diligence services are independent investigations of a business's financial, tax and compliance position carried out before an acquisition, investment or lender decision. Due diligence services from a chartered accountancy firm verify reported earnings, quantify hidden liabilities and give buyers and investors evidence to negotiate price and contract protections.

## What Do Due Diligence Services Cover?

| Type of review | What we examine | Typical trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Financial due diligence | Quality of earnings, revenue recognition, margins, working capital normalisation, capex and cash conversion | Acquisitions, minority investments |
| Tax due diligence | Income tax, GST, TDS and other exposure, open assessments, ITC risk, deferred tax positions | M&A, restructuring |
| Secretarial and compliance review | Statutory registers, ROC filings, board minutes, licences, labour and FEMA compliances | Acquisitions, inbound investment |
| Vendor due diligence | Seller-initiated review packaged for buyers, shortening deal timelines | Pre-sale preparation |
| Red-flag review | Rapid targeted review of the highest-risk issues only | Fast-moving deals, early stage |

## When Is Due Diligence Required?

Transactions fail on information asymmetry more often than on price. Buy-side due diligence is commissioned by acquirers, private equity and strategic investors before signing; sell-side reviews by founders preparing for a stake sale. Lenders may require diligence for large acquisition financing, joint-venture partners for entry decisions, and family businesses for succession planning. In venture rounds, quality of earnings analysis has become standard once a company crosses meaningful revenue scale — our [startup funding and valuation support](/startup-funding-angel-vc) team coordinates the investor-side work.

## Our Buy-Side and Sell-Side Due Diligence Process

1. **Scoping** — agree materiality, entity perimeter, tax years covered and deal timetable.
2. **Data room review** — structured request lists; we track gaps and management representations.
3. **Analysis** — revenue and cost testing, normalisation adjustments, tax exposure build-up, working capital analysis.
4. **Reporting** — findings ranked by deal impact, with quantified exposure ranges and recommended indemnities or price adjustments.
5. **Deal support** — inputs to the share purchase agreement schedules, completion accounts and post-closing covenants.

Sell-side engagements run on the same framework, concluding in a vendor report that answers buyer questions before they are asked.

## Key Issues We Uncover: Quality of Earnings and Tax Exposure

Quality of earnings work separates recurring profitability from one-offs: revenue recognised early or on estimates, related-party sales, subsidy and grant accounting, and unrecorded liabilities. Tax due diligence frequently surfaces unreconciled GST turnover, ineligible input tax credit, TDS defaults, unexplained credits that invite scrutiny under Section 68, and contingent indirect-tax demands. Secretarial lapses — missed filings, unsigned minutes, lapsed licences — matter because they transfer to the acquirer on completion. Each finding is quantified so it can be argued into the price, not discovered after it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is financial due diligence?

Financial due diligence is an independent review of a target's reported financials — revenue quality, margins, working capital, debt and contingent liabilities — so buyers and investors can price the deal on verified numbers rather than management claims.

### How is due diligence different from a statutory audit?

A statutory audit opines on whether annual financial statements show a true and fair view under the Companies Act. Due diligence is transaction-driven: it examines sustainability of earnings, hidden liabilities and tax exposure to support a price and contract.

### How long does due diligence take?

A focused red-flag review typically runs two to three weeks from receipt of the data room; full-scope financial and tax due diligence for a group entity commonly takes four to six weeks depending on data availability and entity count.

### What documents are required for due diligence?

Audited financials and management accounts, GST and TDS returns, tax computation and assessment records, bank statements, debt and litigation schedules, statutory registers, contracts and related-party transaction details, shared through a structured data room.

### Do startups raising venture capital need due diligence?

Yes — most institutional investors commission a quality of earnings or vendor due diligence review before term sheets harden. A seller-initiated review surfaces issues early, protects valuation and speeds up closing.
