> DIR-3 KYC filing is the annual verification every director and designated partner must complete for their Director Identification Number with the {corporate_affairs_authority} by 30 September each year. Missing it deactivates the DIN; alongside Section 90 beneficial ownership filings, it keeps a company's management and ownership records clean.

## Who Must File DIR-3 KYC and By When

Every individual holding a DIN — whether or not they currently serve on any board — must complete annual KYC by 30 September. The form is filed in two ways: DIR-3 KYC, with a digital signature and an OTP to the mobile number and email registered against the DIN, for those filing for the first time or after changes in particulars; and DIR-3 KYC-WEB, a no-fee online confirmation for those who completed KYC last year with no changes. Directors whose DINs were deactivated for earlier defaults, even disqualified directors, remain within the net. A pending filing also blocks everything downstream — new appointments, DSCs linked to the DIN and any RoC form attesting to the director's details.

## DIN Deactivation: Consequences and Reactivation

Non-filing marks the DIN as deactivated. The effects are immediate: no new appointments, no signing of company forms, and a non-compliant director visible in public records. Reactivation is mechanical — file the pending DIR-3 KYC with a flat Rs 5,000 fee — but where several years and companies are involved, sequencing matters, especially if defaults have also triggered Section 164(2) disqualification or are pushing the company toward [strike off](/company-strike-off). ## Significant Beneficial Owners Under Section 90

Section 90 of the Companies Act, 2013 requires every company to look through its shareholding and register individuals who hold — directly or indirectly — at least 10 percent of shares, voting rights or dividend rights, or exercise significant influence or control, as significant beneficial owners (SBOs); the threshold is 25 percent for investing companies. The mechanics are simple but strictly timed:

- The SBO files a declaration in Form BEN-1 with the company within 30 days of acquiring significant beneficial ownership or of any change.
- The company files Form BEN-2 with the RoC within 30 days of receiving the declaration, and maintains a register of SBOs.
- Where ownership is obscured through chains or trusts, the company can issue notice in Form BEN-3 to seek the required information.

The teeth are in Section 90(6): rights attached to an unregistered SBO's interest are not enforceable against the company or its members. Non-compliance attracts penalties on the company and every officer in default, escalating for continuing failure. Demat rules reinforce the same transparency — see [dematerialisation of shares](/dematerialisation-shares).

## DIR-12: Reporting Director Changes on Time

Every appointment, cessation or change in a director's designation must reach the RoC in Form DIR-12 within 30 days under Section 170. A missed DIR-12 leaves MCA master data contradicting the register of members and board minutes — a defect banks, investors and auditors flag in diligence, and one that desynchronises the annual forms. The incoming director's DIN must be KYC-compliant before filing, which is why the two services travel together.

## LLP Filings: Form 11 and Partner KYC

LLPs mirror the regime. Every designated partner with a DIN owes the same annual KYC, partner changes are reported in Form 4, and the annual return in Form 11 — due by 30 May — carries full partner details. Inactive partner KYC therefore blocks Form 11 itself. The key filings at a glance:

| Form | Purpose | Due date |
|---|---|---|
| DIR-3 KYC / DIR-3 KYC-WEB | Annual KYC for every DIN holder | 30 September each year |
| BEN-1 | SBO declaration to the company | Within 30 days of the change |
| BEN-2 | Company's SBO filing with the RoC | Within 30 days of receiving BEN-1 |
| DIR-12 | Director appointment, cessation, designation change | Within 30 days of the event |
| LLP Form 11 | LLP annual return with partner details | 30 May each year |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the deadline for DIR-3 KYC?

30 September every year for all DIN holders. After the due date a flat fee of Rs 5,000 applies, and continued non-filing leads to DIN deactivation until the form is filed with the fee.

### What happens if my DIN is deactivated?

A deactivated DIN blocks the director's appointments and company filings. Filing the pending DIR-3 KYC with the Rs 5,000 fee reactivates it, typically within a few working days.

### Who is a significant beneficial owner?

An individual holding, directly or indirectly, at least 10 percent of a company's shares, voting rights or dividend rights, or one who exercises significant influence or control over the company.

### Is BEN-2 filing mandatory for all companies?

Yes, wherever a significant beneficial owner is identified. The company files BEN-2 within 30 days of receiving the BEN-1 declaration and maintains a register of significant beneficial owners.

### Do designated partners of LLPs need DIR-3 KYC?

Yes. Every designated partner holding a DIN must complete annual KYC, and the LLP's Form 11 annual return carries partner details, so the two records must stay consistent.
