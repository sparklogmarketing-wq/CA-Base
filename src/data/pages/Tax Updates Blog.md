> Income tax updates India refers to the ongoing stream of amendments, circulars, notifications and judicial developments issued under the Income-tax Act 1961 by the {direct_tax_authority} and Parliament through annual Finance Acts. Tracking these changes — rate revisions, regime defaults, rebate thresholds and compliance deadlines — is essential for accurate filing and effective tax planning.

Every Finance Act resets part of the compliance calendar: slabs move, rebate thresholds shift, deadlines get extended and reporting widens. This column is {brand}'s evergreen briefing on the direct tax changes that matter most, explained through mechanics rather than headlines.

## The New Tax Regime Is Now the Default

Under section 115BAC, the concessional regime has moved from opt-in to the default choice in the return forms, now with a standard deduction for salary income. Taxpayers with large home-loan interest or heavy 80C commitments may still favour the old regime; those with simpler income profiles usually save more under the new slabs.

## Section 87A Rebate: Thresholds and Fine Print

The section 87A rebate now removes tax liability up to a materially higher income threshold under the new regime. Two points decide outcomes: the rebate applies only against income taxed at normal slab rates, excluding capital gains; and the old regime retains lower ceilings of Rs 12,500, and Rs 5,000 for resident senior citizens. Rebate miscalculations are a common trigger for demand notices.

## The Capital Gains Restructure: What Changed

The capital gains tax changes announced in the July 2024 Budget rationalised holding periods, taxed long-term gains on listed equity at 12.5 percent beyond a Rs 1.25 lakh annual exemption, and set short-term gains on listed equity at 20 percent, with transitional indexation relief on immovable property acquired before the amendment date. The reach extends to reinvestment routes such as [Section 54 and 54F exemptions](/capital-gains-section-54-54f).

## ITR-U: The Extended Window for Updated Returns

Section 139(8A) allows an updated return where income was missed or understated. The window runs to 24 months from the end of the assessment year, with additional tax rising with delay. The route cannot generate refunds or reduce declared liability, making it most valuable for genuinely omitted income caught during AIS reconciliation before the department flags it.

## Faceless Assessment and the New Compliance Machinery

Faceless assessment under section 144 allocates scrutiny through the National Faceless Assessment Centre, with exceptions for serious fraud and search matters; appeals run on the same architecture. Responses are uploaded and time-stamped online, and a missed portal notice can harden into an ex parte assessment — the terrain of our [scrutiny and notice support](/income-tax-scrutiny-notices) and [AIS, SFT and Form 26AS guide](/ais-sft-guide).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the most important income tax updates for salaried employees?

The new regime is now the ITR default, with revised slabs and a standard deduction. The section 87A rebate threshold has been enhanced, and TDS credits and AIS reconciliation should be reviewed before filing.

### Does the section 87A rebate apply to capital gains?

No. The rebate applies only against income taxed at normal slab rates. Capital gains and other special-rate income are excluded, so gains remain taxable even when other income is fully rebated.

### How long is the ITR-U window now?

An updated return under section 139(8A) can be filed within 24 months of the end of the assessment year, with additional tax rising with delay. It cannot generate refunds or increase losses.

### What did the capital gains restructuring change?

Holding periods were rationalised, long-term gains on listed equity above the annual exemption are taxed at 12.5 percent, short-term gains at 20 percent, with transitional indexation relief on certain properties.

### Is faceless assessment mandatory for all taxpayers?

Assessments under section 144 are largely faceless through the National Faceless Assessment Centre, with exceptions for serious fraud and search cases. Appeals are faceless too, so monitoring the e-proceedings portal is essential.
