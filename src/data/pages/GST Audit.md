> A GST audit is an examination of the records, returns and other documents of a registered person to verify the correctness of turnover declared, taxes paid, input tax credit claimed and refunds taken. It spans departmental audit under section 65, scrutiny of returns, and professional reconciliation of annual returns with books of account.

A GST audit rarely begins with a surprise: it begins months earlier, in data mismatches that officers can see and taxpayers ignore. {brand} works on both sides of the exercise — preparing clients so audits close quickly, and defending them when findings arrive.

## Types of GST Audit in India

| Audit type | Legal basis | Conducted by | Typical trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Departmental audit | Section 65, CGST Act 2017 | Tax authorities | Risk-based selection under the {indirect_tax_authority} audit policy |
| Scrutiny of returns | Section 61 | Proper officer | Mismatches in returns and 2B data (ASMT-10) |
| Annual reconciliation | GSTR-9C | Registered person with professional certification where applicable | Turnover above Rs 2 crore, subject to current-year rules |
| Special audit | Section 66 | Chartered accountant or cost accountant nominated by the officer | Complexity of accounts during any proceeding |

## Departmental Audit Under Section 65

The departmental audit under section 65 is the most structured intervention. The taxpayer receives advance notice of at least fifteen working days; officers examine records at the business premises or electronically; and the audit is normally completed within three months, extendable up to six with approval. Auditors reconcile turnover declared in GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B against e-invoice data, e-way bills, banking and ITC claims. Where the audit reveals unpaid tax, the matter moves to the demand machinery — a show cause notice through the DRC series — so what you do in the first fifteen days of notice shapes the entire outcome.

## Scrutiny and Reconciliation: ASMT-10 and GSTR-9C

Most businesses meet GST audit pressures first through an [ASMT-10 scrutiny notice](/gst-advisory-notices) under section 61, which flags variances — GSTR-1 versus 3B gaps, credit claimed beyond GSTR-2B, anomalous HSN data — and allows thirty days to explain through an ASMT-11 reply. A reasoned reply typically concludes the scrutiny through ASMT-12. At year end, the GSTR-9C reconciliation statement forces a formal bridge between financial statements and the annual return; even where its filing threshold has shifted with rule changes, we prepare the reconciliation internally because it is precisely the exercise departmental auditors repeat themselves.

## Records You Must Be Able to Produce

Section 36 requires records to be preserved for seventy-two months from the due date of filing the annual return for the relevant period. In practice, audit requests centre on a short list: sales and purchase registers tied to e-invoices, ITC reconciliation sheets against GSTR-2B, e-way bill logs, export documentation including LUTs and shipping bills, credit and debit note sequences, and bank statements. Disorganised records convert a routine audit into an adverse one, because officers estimate where they cannot verify.

## Responding to Audit Findings and Demands

Findings accepted at audit stage are settled with tax and interest before a notice issues. Where they harden into a DRC-01 show cause notice, the response window, personal hearing under section 75 and the appeal route under section 107 — three months from the DRC-07 order, with a 10 percent pre-deposit — come into play. Every stage rewards documentation prepared before the auditor walked in, which is why audit defence begins in the return-filing cycle, not at the notice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a departmental GST audit under section 65?

It is an examination of your records, returns and documents by tax authorities to verify turnover declared, tax paid and credit claimed. It starts with at least fifteen working days' notice and is normally completed within three months, extendable up to six.

### Who gets selected for a departmental GST audit?

Selection is risk-based under the audit policy of the {indirect_tax_authority} — typically higher-turnover taxpayers, large refund claimants and businesses with recurring data mismatches between returns, e-invoices and e-way bills.

### Is GSTR-9C still required?

The GSTR-9C reconciliation statement historically applied to taxpayers above Rs 2 crore turnover, but its applicability has changed with successive rule amendments. Confirm the requirement for the specific year with us before planning the filing.

### How should I respond to an ASMT-10 scrutiny notice?

File a reply in FORM ASMT-11 within thirty days, with reconciliations between GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, e-invoices and 2B explaining the flagged variances. A reasoned reply usually closes scrutiny through ASMT-12 without escalation.

### How far back can GST officers examine records?

Broadly, notices for older periods were limited to three years under section 73 or five years under section 74; for tax periods from FY 2024-25, section 74A provides windows of 42 or 54 months depending on the allegations.
