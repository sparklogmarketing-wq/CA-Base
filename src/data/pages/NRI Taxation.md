> NRI taxation in India covers the tax treatment of income that non-resident Indians earn or hold in the country — NRO interest, rent, capital gains and dividends — along with residential status, TDS under Section 195, DTAA relief, ITR disclosure and repatriation compliance under FEMA, 1999 and related RBI rules.

## How Is Residential Status Determined?

Everything in NRI taxation flows from residential status under Section 6 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 — it decides which income is taxable, which forms apply and whether foreign assets must be disclosed.

| Test | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| Basic condition 1 | Physical stay in {country} of 182 days or more in the financial year |
| Basic condition 2 | Stay of 60 days or more in the year plus 365 days or more in the preceding four years |
| Additional condition 1 | Resident in at least 2 of the preceding 10 years |
| Additional condition 2 | Stay of 729 days or more in the preceding 7 years |

Fail both basic conditions and you are a non-resident; pass a basic condition but fail either additional condition and you become resident but not ordinarily resident (RNOR) — foreign income stays outside Indian tax and Schedule FA does not apply. Pass everything and you are ordinarily resident. Section 6(1A) also deems Indian citizens with India-sourced income above Rs 15 lakh resident (as RNOR) even with minimal stay, so departure-year planning needs care in both directions.

## What Income Is Taxable for NRIs?

Non-residents are taxed only on income received, accrued or deemed to accrue in {country}:

| Income | Tax treatment |
| --- | --- |
| NRE account interest | Exempt under Section 10(15)(fa) |
| FCNR deposit interest | Exempt under Section 10(4)(ii) |
| NRO interest | Taxable; TDS under Section 195 at 30% plus surcharge and cess; DTAA rates may reduce it |
| Rent from Indian property | Taxable with a 30% standard deduction under Section 24(a) |
| Capital gains on Indian assets | Taxable at applicable rates; Sections 54, 54F and 54EC exemptions available |

The NRE and FCNR interest exemptions hold only while you remain non-resident or RNOR. Dividends and capital gains stay taxable wherever the money lands, and treaty protection depends on the fine print of the applicable DTAA.

## TDS on NRI Income: Section 195 and Lower Deduction Certificates

Section 195 obliges every payer — a bank, a tenant, a property buyer — to deduct tax before remitting income to a non-resident. The sharpest edge appears in property sales, where buyers routinely deduct on the full consideration rather than the gain. Remedies are procedural:

- **Lower deduction certificate:** Apply on Form 13 so deduction is limited to the estimated capital gain.
- **Buyer-side computations:** Where the gain is ascertainable, deduction under Section 195(2) can be restricted to the gain element.
- **Refund route:** Where excess is already deducted, [ITR filing](/itr-filing) recovers it after reconciling Form 26AS and AIS credits.

Repatriation is the FEMA mirror image: funds remitted abroad need Form 15CB certification from a chartered accountant plus Form 15CA, and NRO repatriation is capped at USD 1 million per financial year.

## DTAA Relief and Foreign Tax Credit

Where the same income is taxed twice, the applicable DTAA decides the outcome: treaty rates can compress the 30% NRO interest deduction, and treaty tie-breaker rules — not the passport — determine residence. Invoking a treaty needs a Tax Residency Certificate from the foreign authority, the electronically filed Form 10F and, where relevant, the treaty's no PE declaration. Income taxed abroad but taxable in {country} earns foreign tax credit under Section 90/91 read with the prescribed rules — our [DTAA and foreign tax credit](/dtaa-foreign-tax-credit) guide covers the mechanics.

## ITR Filing, 26AS and AIS for NRIs

NRI income tax return filing is where compliance holds together or breaks. TDS deducted under Section 195 appears in Form 26AS; high-value transactions surface in the Annual Information Statement (AIS). Mismatches against your return are the standard notice trigger. Returns are due by 31 July, or 31 October where a tax audit applies, and timely filing preserves loss carry-forwards and refund claims.

## Schedule FA: The Returning NRI Question

Ordinarily resident taxpayers must disclose foreign bank accounts, financial interests, immovable property and signing authority in Schedule FA, with Black Money Act, 2015 penalties for non-disclosure. Returning NRIs get breathing space: through the RNOR years, foreign income stays non-taxable and Schedule FA does not apply — but once ROR status begins, disclosure is compulsory and foreign retirement accounts such as 401(k) balances need deliberate treatment. Structuring the return year is where [individual tax planning](/individual-tax-planning) earns its fee.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is NRE interest taxable in India?

No. Interest on NRE accounts is exempt under Section 10(15)(fa) and interest on FCNR deposits under Section 10(4)(ii) while you are a non-resident. The exemption continues through RNOR years after your return, but lapses once you become ordinarily resident.

### How can NRIs reduce TDS on a property sale?

The buyer must deduct tax under Section 195, but the seller can apply for a lower deduction certificate using Form 13 so TDS matches the actual capital gain. Filing the return then secures any refund of excess deduction.

### Do NRIs need to file an income tax return?

Yes, when Indian income exceeds the basic exemption limit, when tax has been deducted in excess, or when losses must be carried forward. Filing by the due date preserves loss carry-forward and refund claims for NRO interest or property transactions.

### What is RNOR status?

Resident but not ordinarily resident. You satisfy a basic residency condition but fail at least one additional condition under Section 6. Foreign income is not taxable and Schedule FA disclosure does not apply, which makes the RNOR window valuable for returning NRIs.

### How much can NRIs repatriate from NRO accounts?

NRE balances are fully repatriable. From NRO accounts, up to USD 1 million per financial year can be remitted abroad after taxes, supported by Form 15CB certification from a chartered accountant and Form 15CA, with bank documentation.

### What happens if foreign assets are not disclosed?

Ordinarily resident taxpayers must report foreign assets in Schedule FA of the ITR. Non-disclosure attracts penalties under the Black Money Act, 2015, besides reassessment risk. Updated returns and voluntary disclosure can mitigate where omissions were genuine.
