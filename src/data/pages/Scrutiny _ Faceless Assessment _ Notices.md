> An income tax scrutiny notice is a formal communication from the Income Tax Department — issued under sections 142(1) or 143(2) — placing your return of income under detailed examination. It demands documented responses within fixed timelines and, under section 144B, concludes through India's faceless assessment framework without any officer interface.

## What Triggers an Income Tax Scrutiny Notice?

Receiving an income tax scrutiny notice is rarely random. The {direct_tax_authority} runs analytics over Form 26AS, the Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Statement of Financial Transactions, and flags mismatches between reported income and observed behaviour. High-value property registrations, credit-card spends, mutual fund redemptions, cash deposits and inbound remittances all feed the selection engine. Refund-heavy returns, recurring business losses, referrals from survey or search operations and specific information from other government databases also trigger examination.

Every notice issued today carries a Document Identification Number and lands in the registered account on the e-filing portal. Always verify authenticity through the portal before responding — spoofed notice emails are common.

## Common Income Tax Notices and Response Windows

Notices under the Income-tax Act, 1961 serve different purposes, and each carries its own clock:

| Notice | What it covers | Typical response window |
|---|---|---|
| Section 139(9) | Defective return — incomplete or inconsistent filing | 15 days from intimation |
| Section 142(1) | Inquiry, questionnaire or production of books and documents | Dates fixed in the notice |
| Section 143(2) | Return selected for detailed scrutiny | Dates fixed in the notice |
| Section 148 | Income escaping assessment (reassessment) | Return in response within the period specified in the notice |
| Section 245 | Refund proposed to be set off against an outstanding demand | Object within 30 days on the e-filing portal |
| Section 156 | Notice of demand raised by an assessment order | Pay or appeal within 30 days |
| DRC-01 / DRC-07 | GST-side demand cycle — notice, hearing summary and demand order | As stated in the notice (GST law) |

A notice under section 143(2) means your return has been picked for scrutiny assessment under section 143(3); a section 148 notice asserts income has escaped assessment and requires you to file a return in response. Demands that survive assessment follow the section 156 route, while matching GST-side demands travel through the DRC series.

## How the Faceless Assessment Procedure Works Under Section 144B

Section 144B mandates an electronic procedure for scrutiny, reassessment and related assessments. The National Faceless Assessment Centre (NFAC) allocates each case through a dynamic jurisdiction chain, so no officer you can identify conducts the assessment. The assessment unit prepares a draft order after considering your responses uploaded through e-proceedings; where a variation beyond the prescribed threshold is proposed, you receive a further show-cause opportunity. Cases involving international or specified domestic transactions are routed to the Dispute Resolution Panel under section 144C. Every communication, notice and order must carry a DIN, and the assessment must be completed within the prescribed time limits. The regime removes interface risk but rewards precise, evidence-backed written submissions.

## Meeting Response Timelines Without Losing Rights

Defective-return notices under section 139(9) must be cured within 15 days; a section 245 intimation should be objected to within 30 days if you dispute the demand; compliance dates for notices under sections 142(1) and 143(2) are fixed in each notice. Where records genuinely need more time, seek an adjournment through the e-proceedings tab with reasons — never let a date lapse silently. Before responding, reconcile your books against AIS, Form 26AS and TDS returns so the reply is internally consistent; inconsistencies invite follow-up questionnaires.

## Consequences of Letting a Notice Lapse

Non-compliance hands the officer a free hand: a best-judgment assessment under section 144 on estimated income, penalties under section 270A, interest under sections 234A, 234B and 234C, and recovery action on the raised demand. In egregious cases of wilful default, prosecution provisions apply. Most of this is avoidable with a timely, documented income tax notice reply.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why did I receive an income tax scrutiny notice?

Common triggers are mismatches between your ITR and AIS or Form 26AS, high-value transactions, large refund claims, repeated losses or specific information received from other government agencies under computer-aided selection.

### How much time do I get to respond to a notice?

Defective return notices under section 139(9) allow 15 days; section 245 responses are generally due within 30 days; scrutiny and inquiry notices carry the dates fixed in the notice itself.

### What is faceless assessment under section 144B?

It is the compulsory e-assessment regime in which the National Faceless Assessment Centre routes your case through a dynamic jurisdiction chain, eliminating physical meetings and moving every notice, response and order onto the e-filing portal.

### Can a chartered accountant reply on my behalf?

Yes. An authorised representative can verify records, draft submissions and upload responses through the e-proceedings tab, so your physical presence is not required in a faceless assessment.

### What happens if I ignore a scrutiny notice?

The assessing officer can complete a best-judgment assessment under section 144 on estimated income, levy penalties under section 270A plus interest, and in serious cases initiate prosecution.

### Is a 143(1) intimation the same as scrutiny?

No. Section 143(1) is an automated adjustment intimation issued after return processing. Scrutiny begins only when a separate notice under section 143(2) is served within the statutory window.
