> GST issues by industry arise because one law applies very differently across business models — input tax credit blocks, classification disputes, e-commerce TCS, refund delays and reverse charge traps hit IT and SaaS, restaurants, real estate, textiles, exports and transport businesses in distinct, sector-specific ways that generic advice often misses.

A restaurant, a SaaS startup and a property developer can all file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B on time and still carry serious GST risk — because the risk sits in how their revenue model interacts with rate, place-of-supply and credit rules. ## Input Tax Credit: The Most Common Block

Credit denial is the dominant dispute across sectors. Under section 16(2), ITC survives only if the supplier has filed returns and paid tax, the invoice appears in your GSTR-2B, and payment is made within 180 days. Section 17(5) then blocks credit outright on construction for own account, specified vehicles, club memberships and personal consumption. Reverse charge self-invoicing under [GST ITC and RCM](/gst-itc-rcm) rules adds its own discipline requirements. Vendors who go non-compliant transmit that risk straight to their customers.

## Refund Delays for Exporters and Inverted Duty Cases

Exporters choose between zero-rating with a Letter of Undertaking and refund of unutilised ITC, or paying IGST and claiming a refund against shipping bills. Both routes punish sloppy documentation. Inverted duty structures — where input tax exceeds output tax — build up working capital in the credit ledger instead. ## Sector Notes: Where Each Model Hurts

- **IT and SaaS** — classification of cross-border services drives everything: export conditions, place-of-supply analysis and OIDAR obligations for Indian users. See our [IT, SaaS and e-commerce industry guide](/it-saas-ecommerce).
- **Restaurants** — the 5% no-ITC rate and the restaurant composition scheme cap planning options, while platform sales raise section 9(5) questions about who is liable.
- **Real estate** — GST on real estate projects runs at 5% without ITC for most residential units, with commercial property at 18% with ITC; promoters collect TCS from buyers and joint development agreements raise valuation and reverse-charge questions.
- **Textiles** — job-work movements under ITC-04, inverted-duty refund build-up and the garment rate threshold (5% up to Rs 2,500, 18% above, post-September 2025) keep classification scrutiny high.
- **E-commerce** — platform TCS at 0.5%, section 9(5) categories and multi-state stock transfers shape both compliance cost and pricing; detail in our [GST TDS, TCS and e-commerce guide](/gst-tds-tcs-ecommerce).
- **GTA and logistics** — the 5% rate applies under either reverse charge (registered recipients) or forward charge with ITC, and the election cannot flip freely; e-way bills above the Rs 50,000 threshold drive checkpoint disputes. See [GST e-invoicing and e-way bills](/gst-e-invoicing-eway-bill).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do restaurants pay GST at 5% without input tax credit?

Standalone restaurant service is taxed at 5% with no ITC, whether under the regular scheme or the restaurant composition scheme. Where food is delivered through an e-commerce platform, the operator may be liable for the GST under section 9(5).

### Can exporters recover GST paid on inputs?

Yes. Zero-rated supplies are supported through a Letter of Undertaking with refunds of unutilised input tax credit, or through refunds of IGST paid on exports. Timely shipping bills and refund applications are critical.

### Why is my input tax credit blocked even with a valid invoice?

Section 16(2) requires the supplier to have filed returns and paid tax, and section 17(5) blocks credit on items such as construction and certain vehicle purchases. Payment to the supplier within 180 days also matters.

### Is GST different for e-commerce sellers?

Yes. E-commerce operators collect TCS under section 52 — now 0.5% on net taxable supplies — and may be liable for GST on certain platform sales under section 9(5). Multi-state warehousing can also create registration obligations.

### What GST issues do IT and SaaS companies face most?

Export classification of services, place-of-supply analysis for overseas customers, OIDAR liability for Indian users and ITC treatment of shared overheads and employee reimbursements are the recurring pressure points.
