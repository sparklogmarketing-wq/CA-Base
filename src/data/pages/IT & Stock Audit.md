## What Do IT and Stock Audit Services Include?

Inventory is where physical reality and accounting records diverge most often, and ERP systems are where the divergence hides. Our combined engagement covers both sides:

- **Physical verification** â€” wall-to-wall or sample-based counting, cut-off testing around receipts and dispatches, third-party and in-transit stock checks

Because the same team reviews the floor and the system, discrepancies are traced to their source â€” counting error, unrecorded scrap, ERP access abuse or deliberate misstatement.

## When Is a Stock Audit Required?

A bank stock audit is the most common trigger: working-capital lenders use audited inventory and receivable figures to compute drawing power against cash credit limits, and many sanction letters require periodic third-party verification along with quarterly stock statements. Other triggers include statutory audit support where inventory is material, insurance claim substantiation after fire or flood, NBFC and fintech collateral reviews, e-commerce marketplace reconciliation across fulfilment centres, and pre-sale diligence where stock drives valuation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a bank stock audit?

A stock audit mandated by lenders for working-capital borrowers. The auditor physically verifies inventory, tests valuation and margins, and reports the figures banks use to compute drawing power and review quarterly stock statements.

### How is inventory valued in a stock audit?

Under AS 2 or Ind AS 2, inventory is carried at the lower of cost and net realisable value. The audit tests the costing method, obsolescence provisions, damaged stock and consistency of valuation between periods.

### What is covered in an IT general controls review?

User access and segregation of duties, change management, IT operations and backups, and data integrity in the ERP or accounting system â€” the controls that keep transaction records reliable for audits and lenders.

### Is an audit trail in accounting software mandatory?

For companies, yes. Section 128(5) of the Companies Act 2013 requires books of account to carry an audit trail of every transaction with an edit log, effective from April 1, 2023, and auditors report on it.

### How often should stock audits be done?

Lenders typically require quarterly stock audits for working-capital accounts; businesses themselves benefit from at least an annual full verification plus cycle counts of high-value items during the year.

### Can stock audit and statutory audit be combined?

Yes. Coordinated planning lets one physical verification support both the statutory audit's inventory assertions and the lender's stock report, reducing disruption at year end.
