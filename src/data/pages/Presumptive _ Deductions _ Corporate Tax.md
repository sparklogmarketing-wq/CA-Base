> Presumptive taxation in India allows eligible small businesses, professionals, transporters and specified non-residents to declare income on a deemed-profit basis under sections 44AD, 44ADA, 44AE, 44B, 44BB and 44BBB of the Income-tax Act 1961, without detailed books of account, while companies plan tax through concessional regimes such as 115BAA and 115BAB.

Compliance weight falls hardest on smaller enterprises and professionals — which is precisely what the presumptive chapters were designed to relieve. At the other end of the scale, companies choose between the default rate and concessional regimes, trading deductions for lower rates. ## Presumptive Taxation in India: Sections 44AD to 44BBB

| Section | Applies to | Deemed income | Key limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 44AD | Resident individuals, HUFs and partnership firms (not LLPs) in eligible businesses | 6% of digital turnover, 8% of cash turnover (or actual profit, if higher) | Turnover up to Rs 2 crore; Rs 3 crore where 95%+ of receipts are digital |
| 44ADA | Resident professionals in specified professions (legal, medical, engineering, architectural, accountancy, technical consultancy, film professions) | 50% of gross receipts | Receipts up to Rs 50 lakh; Rs 75 lakh where 95%+ digital |
| 44AE | Owners of goods carriages (up to 10 vehicles) | Rs 7,500 per month or part for light vehicles; Rs 1,000 per tonne of gross vehicle weight per month for heavy vehicles | Per vehicle, per month |
| 44B | Non-residents operating ships (carriage of passengers, mail, goods) | 7.5% of gross receipts | No book-maintenance requirement |
| 44BB | Non-residents in exploration of mineral oils | 10% of gross receipts | No book-maintenance requirement |
| 44BBB | Foreign companies in civil construction or turnkey power projects | 10% of gross receipts | No book-maintenance requirement |

## When the Presumptive Schemes Help — and Their Conditions

Declaring income at the prescribed deemed rates generally removes the tax audit requirement under section 44AB, which is the single biggest compliance saving for growing businesses. The conditions matter, though:

- Under 44AD, if actual profit is claimed below the deemed rate, or total income exceeds the basic exemption limit, the taxpayer must maintain books and is exposed to audit
- The 44AD scheme is not available to businesses of professionals, agency businesses or commission/brokerage income
- Under 44ADA, no further deduction for expenses is allowed against the deemed 50%; claims beyond it require books and trigger audit exposure
- GST turnover and income-tax turnover must reconcile — mismatches are an easy notice trigger

## Corporate Tax Rates and Concessional Regimes

| Regime | Rate (plus surcharge and cess) | Effective rate | Who can opt |
|---|---|---|---|
| Default | 30% | ~34.9% (with 10% surcharge where applicable, 4% cess) | All domestic companies; 25% where turnover does not exceed Rs 400 crore |
| Section 115BAA | 22% | ~25.17% | Domestic companies foregoing specified deductions (including 80JJAA) and related loss set-offs |
| Section 115BAB | 15% | ~17.16% | New domestic manufacturing companies incorporated on or after 1 October 2019 that commenced manufacture by 31 March 2024, subject to conditions |

The concessional regimes reduce the rate sharply but withdraw incentives — depreciation on additional assets at enhanced rates, set-off of specified losses, and deductions such as 80JJAA. The opt is permanent for the company, so it should follow a projection, not a headline rate.

## Corporate Deductions Worth Reviewing

- Section 80JJAA — 30% of additional employee cost for three assessment years, where a company increases its workforce by 100% or employs 10 additional workers (subject to emolument and PF/ESI conditions); unavailable to 115BAA and 115BAB optees
- Section 115JB MAT — where tax on regular income falls below 15% of book profit, MAT applies; MAT credit carries forward for 15 assessment years; 115BAA/115BAB companies are exempt from MAT
- Ordinary-course deductions — depreciation, CSR spend is not deductible, and related-party costs must survive transfer pricing scrutiny

## Choosing Between Presumptive and Regular Taxation

Presumptive schemes trade deduction detail for simplicity — ideal where margins are healthy and record-keeping costs are real. Regular books win where actual margins are thin, where losses need to be established for carry-forward, or where lenders and investors require audited accounts. Many of our clients graduate from 44AD to regular books as funding or [tax audit](/tax-audit) obligations arrive; planning that transition a year ahead avoids surprises.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who can opt for section 44AD presumptive taxation?

Resident individuals, HUFs and partnership firms (not LLPs) running eligible businesses with turnover up to Rs 2 crore — extended to Rs 3 crore where at least 95% of receipts are digital. Deemed profit is 8% of cash and 6% of digital turnover.

### What is the limit under section 44ADA?

Professionals can declare 50% of gross receipts up to Rs 50 lakh as income, rising to Rs 75 lakh where 95% or more of receipts are digital. No separate expense deduction is allowed against the deemed income.

### What is the tax rate under section 115BAA?

Domestic companies opting for 115BAA pay 22% plus 10% surcharge and 4% cess — an effective rate of about 25.17% — in exchange for foregoing specified deductions and incentives such as 80JJAA.

### When does MAT apply to a company?

Companies computing taxable income below 15% of book profit pay MAT under section 115JB. MAT credit can be carried forward for 15 assessment years. Companies under 115BAA or 115BAB are exempt from MAT.

### Does presumptive taxation remove the tax audit requirement?

Declaring income at the prescribed deemed rates generally exempts the taxpayer from audit under section 44AB. Books of account become necessary where actual profit is claimed below the deemed rate or total income exceeds the basic exemption limit.
