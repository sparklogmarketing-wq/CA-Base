> ROC compliance services cover the recurring statutory filings a company or LLP makes with the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act, 2013 and the LLP Act, 2008 — annual returns, financial statements, director KYC, deposit returns and event-based filings — plus the board meetings and statutory registers that keep the entity in good standing.

## What ROC Compliance Services Cover

Every company carries permanent obligations to the Registrar of Companies: annual financial statements and returns, reporting changes in directors and share capital, statutory registers, and meetings held on time. Professional ROC compliance services bundle all of this into one managed calendar, so deadlines are met by design rather than through late-fee notices. Event-based filings — DIR-12, SH-7, PAS-3 and MGT-14 — keep MCA master data accurate for banks, investors and auditors.

## Annual ROC Filing Calendar for Companies and LLPs

Missed dates trigger most penalties, so every engagement runs on a fixed annual ROC filing calendar:

| Filing | Form | Applies to | Due date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial statements | AOC-4 | All companies | Within 30 days of the AGM |
| Annual return | MGT-7; MGT-7A for small companies and OPCs | All companies | Within 60 days of the AGM |
| Director KYC | DIR-3 KYC / DIR-3 KYC-WEB | Every DIN holder | 30 September each year |
| Return of deposits and exempt deposits | DPT-3 | Companies | 30 June each year |
| Payments to MSMEs outstanding beyond 45 days | MSME Form 1 | Companies exceeding prescribed limits | 30 April / 31 October (half-yearly) |
| Statement of account and solvency | LLP Form 8 | LLPs | 30 October each year |
| Annual return | LLP Form 11 | LLPs | 30 May each year |

One person companies are exempt from the AGM and file within 30 days of board approval; small companies and OPCs use the simplified MGT-7A and AOC-4(A). LLPs run on a separate cycle — our [LLP registration and compliance](/llp-registration) practice handles both.

## AGM and EGM Rules Under the Companies Act, 2013

Section 96 requires the first AGM within nine months of the company's first financial year-end, and every subsequent AGM within six months of year-end, with a gap of not more than 15 months between two meetings. Notice requires at least 21 clear days, shorter only with consent of members holding 95 percent of voting rights; a private company's quorum is two members personally present. Ordinary business such as financial statements, dividends and auditor appointment is transacted at the AGM, while special business needs an explanatory statement under Section 102. EGMs under Section 100 may be convened by the board or on members' requisition of at least 10 percent of paid-up capital, and filable resolutions go to the RoC in MGT-14 within 30 days under Section 117.

## Secretarial Audit and Form MR-3 Applicability

Section 204 mandates a secretarial audit by a practising company secretary for every listed company and every public company with paid-up share capital of Rs 50 crore or more, or turnover of Rs 250 crore or more, in the preceding financial year. The Companies (Appointment and Remuneration of Managerial Personnel) Rules, 2014 extend the requirement to certain other companies with large bank borrowings or turnover. The report in Form MR-3 is annexed to the board's report with the AOC-4. Private companies below these thresholds are outside the mandate, but a voluntary secretarial audit is increasingly used before fundraising, acquisitions and IPO-readiness reviews.

## Penalties for Late or Missed ROC Filings

The Companies (Amendment) Act, 2020 replaced most imprisonment exposure for filing lapses with monetary penalties — but the amounts compound daily:

| Provision | Default | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| Section 92(5) | Annual return (MGT-7) not filed | Rs 50,000 on the company and each officer in default; continuing default Rs 100 per day, capped at Rs 3,00,000 for the company and Rs 1,00,000 for an officer |
| Section 117 | Filable resolutions and agreements not filed in MGT-14 | Rs 1,00,000 on the company and each officer; continuing default Rs 500 per day, capped at Rs 25,00,000 |
| Section 137(3) | Financial statements (AOC-4) not filed | Fine of Rs 50,000 up to Rs 3,00,000 on the company, and likewise on officers in default |
| Schedule J additional fees | Delayed annual filings | Additional fee of Rs 100 per day of continuing delay |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When is AOC-4 due?

Within 30 days of the AGM; where no AGM is required, as for one person companies, within 30 days of board approval.

### When is the MGT-7 annual return due?

Within 60 days of the AGM. Small companies and one person companies file the simplified MGT-7A. Missing the date attracts additional fees of Rs 100 per day.

### Which companies need a secretarial audit?

All listed companies and public companies with paid-up capital of Rs 50 crore or turnover of Rs 250 crore or more, plus certain companies with large bank borrowings or turnover under the rules.

### What is the penalty for late annual filings?

Sections 92(5), 117 and 137 impose fines on the company and officers, plus Rs 100 per day additional fee on annual forms. Five years of defaults also disqualifies directors.

### Do LLPs file the same ROC forms?

No. LLPs file Form 8, the statement of account and solvency, by 30 October and Form 11, the annual return, by 30 May, along with DIR-3 KYC for designated partners.
