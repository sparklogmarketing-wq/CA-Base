> Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) are the IFRS-converged accounting standards notified under section 133 of the Companies Act 2013. They apply to listed companies and companies with a net worth of Rs 250 crore or more, replacing the older Accounting Standards (AS) with a fair-value, principles-based reporting framework.

## What Are Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS)?

India operates two frameworks. The Accounting Standards (AS) — such as AS 2, AS 10 and AS 19 — are the older, historical-cost based standards issued under the Companies (Accounting Standards) Rules, 2006 and still applied by non-Ind AS companies and non-corporates. Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS), notified under the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015, are converged with IFRS through a small set of carve-outs and carve-ins adapted to Indian law and conditions. The Central Government notifies the standards on the recommendation of the National Advisory Committee on Accounting Standards, and audit quality under either framework is examined within the statutory audit framework, with oversight bodies such as the {audit_regulator} reviewing specified classes of companies.

## Ind AS vs AS: Key Differences That Change Reported Numbers

Transition is not a relabelling exercise — Ind AS changes measurement, presentation and disclosure. The comparison below shows where the two frameworks diverge most.

| Area | AS framework | Ind AS (IFRS-converged) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue recognition | AS 7 and AS 9 — risks-and-rewards transfer | Ind AS 115 — five-step model based on performance obligations and control |
| Financial instruments | AS 11 and AS 13 — largely historical cost | Ind AS 109 and Ind AS 113 — fair value measurement and expected credit loss impairment |
| Leases | AS 19 — operating leases off balance sheet | Ind AS 116 — right-of-use asset and lease liability recognised by lessees |
| Deferred tax | AS 22 — timing-difference approach | Ind AS 12 — balance-sheet approach on temporary differences |
| Consolidation | AS 21 — control through majority voting rights | Ind AS 110 — control including de facto control and structured entities |
| Presentation | Profit and loss account | Ind AS 1 — single statement with other comprehensive income |

## Ind AS Applicability: Thresholds and Roadmap

Applicability turns on listing status and net worth (as defined in the rules), with a phased roadmap for regulated entities. Once a company crosses the mandatory threshold, Ind AS applies from the first accounting period prescribed — and voluntary adopters cannot revert.

| Entity | Framework | Ind AS mandatory from |
|---|---|---|
| Listed companies (and those in process of listing) | Ind AS | FY 2016-17 |
| Unlisted companies with net worth of Rs 250 crore or more | Ind AS | FY 2016-17 |
| Companies with net worth below Rs 250 crore | AS by default | Voluntary opt-in permitted (irrevocable) |
| Banks, NBFCs and insurers with net worth of Rs 250 crore or more | Ind AS | FY 2018-19 |
| Banks, NBFCs and insurers with net worth between Rs 100 crore and Rs 250 crore | Ind AS | FY 2019-20 |
| Regulated entities with net worth below Rs 100 crore | AS | Continue with AS |

The rules are organised into divisions: Division I applies to listed companies, Division II to other companies and Division III to NBFCs. Subsidiaries, associates and joint ventures of an Ind AS adopter must prepare Ind AS financial statements from the financial year in which the parent does, so that consolidated reporting under section 129 remains consistent — a point foreign groups monitoring [foreign subsidiary reporting](/foreign-subsidiary-tax-planning) should note.

## Which Companies Follow Ind AS Today

In practice, the Ind AS population covers every listed company, large unlisted companies above the Rs 250 crore net worth threshold, most banks and insurers of scale, NBFCs in the phased roadmap, and the Indian subsidiaries, associates and joint ventures of any Ind AS parent — Indian or foreign. Companies below the thresholds, most LLPs and non-corporate entities continue with the AS framework or ICAI-issued standards. If your entity is approaching a mandatory trigger — a listing plan, a funding round that lifts net worth, or acquisition by an Ind AS group — transition planning should start a full year ahead.

## Transition and First-Time Adoption: Ind AS 101

Ind AS 101 governs the move. Management fixes a date of transition — the beginning of the earliest comparative period presented — and prepares an opening Ind AS balance sheet at that date, applying mandatory exceptions and elective exemptions such as deemed-cost elections for property, plant and equipment. The first Ind AS financial statements carry reconciliations of equity and profit from previous GAAP, and the Companies Act requires a third balance sheet as at the transition date in that first year. Because fair-value remeasurements flow through opening reserves, the transition balance sheet also resets metrics used in debt covenants, management incentive plans and [financial modeling and valuation](/financial-model-pitch-deck-valuation) work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between Ind AS and IFRS?

Ind AS is converged with, but not identical to, IFRS. India retained a limited set of carve-outs and carve-ins to suit domestic law, regulation and business conditions.

### Is Ind AS mandatory for private limited companies?

Where net worth is Rs 250 crore or more, or the company is a subsidiary, associate or joint venture of an Ind AS adopter. Smaller private companies may adopt voluntarily.

### Can a company revert from Ind AS to AS?

No. Voluntary adoption is irrevocable, and companies continue applying Ind AS even if net worth later falls below the threshold.

### What is Ind AS 101?

The first-time adoption standard. It prescribes the opening Ind AS balance sheet at the transition date, optional and mandatory exemptions, and reconciliations to previous GAAP.

### Which division of the Ind AS rules applies to my company?

Division I for listed companies, Division II for other companies and Division III for NBFCs. The divisions differ mainly in presentation and certain recognition details.
