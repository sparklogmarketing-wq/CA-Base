## GST 2.0: The September 2025 Rate Restructure

The most consequential reform since launch took effect on 22 September 2025: the slab structure consolidated into two principal rates — 5% and 18% — with a 40% demerit rate for specified sin and luxury goods, collapsing the earlier 12% and 28% bands. The transition burden landed on businesses, not the law: HSN-to-rate re-mapping, price-list and MRP revisions, treatment of advances received at old rates, ITC held in stock at higher rates, and credit notes spanning the changeover date. Businesses that treated it as an ERP project, not a press-release item, transitioned cleanly. ## E-Invoicing Expansion and Digitisation

E-invoicing has moved up-market steadily, with the mandate now covering taxpayers above Rs 5 crore aggregate turnover and expanding across document types. The direction of travel is clear: machine-readable invoices, tighter two-way matching with returns, and less manual intervention in ITC availment. Our [GST e-invoicing and e-way bill services](/gst-e-invoicing-eway-bill) cover readiness assessment, IRP integration and reconciliation.

## Amnesty and Adjudication Reform

Two structural changes have reshaped the demand landscape. First, a conditional amnesty under section 128 allowed waiver of interest and penalty on specified demand orders relating to financial years 2017-18 to 2019-20, where tax and interest were paid within the notified window — a one-time de-risking route for legacy disputes. Second, from FY 2024-25, sections 73 and 74 were consolidated into a single adjudication provision, section 74A, standardising notice formats, timelines and penalty regimes across fraud and non-fraud cases. Both reward early case review — see our [GST advisory and notices](/gst-advisory-notices) team before positions harden.

## How Businesses Should Respond to GST Changes

The playbook is unchanged even as the rules move: monitor Council and {indirect_tax_authority} releases monthly, run an impact assessment against your invoice masters and ERP tax codes, quantify transitions (advances, stock, ITC), train invoice-raising teams, and document positions taken where interpretation is open. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often do GST rules change in India?

Material changes arrive through Finance Acts, GST Council decisions and CBIC notifications — typically several each quarter across rates, returns, ITC and invoicing. Review your compliance calendar at least quarterly and before major sales events.

### What did the September 2025 GST 2.0 reform change?

It restructured the slab system to two principal rates of 5% and 18%, with a 40% demerit rate for specified goods. Businesses had to re-map HSN codes, revise price lists and address transitions for advances, ITC and stock.

### Who is required to generate e-invoices?

E-invoicing is mandatory for taxpayers with aggregate turnover above Rs 5 crore, covering invoices and credit notes through the IRP. Covered document types and taxpayer categories have expanded periodically.

### What is the section 128 amnesty for old GST demands?

A time-bound waiver of interest and penalty on certain demand orders relating to earlier financial years (2017-18 to 2019-20), where tax and interest were paid within the notified window. Eligibility depended on the order stage and payment timing.

### What is section 74A of the CGST Act?

A consolidated adjudication provision effective from FY 2024-25 that replaced the separate sections 73 and 74 tracks, applying a single framework of notices, timelines and penalties to demand cases with and without fraud.
