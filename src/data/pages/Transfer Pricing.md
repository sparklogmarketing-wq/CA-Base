> Transfer pricing services in India help companies price transactions with associated enterprises at arm's length under Sections 92 to 92F of the Income-tax Act, 1961 — covering documentation, Form 3CEB certification, master file and CbCR filings, advance pricing agreements and defence of transfer pricing adjustments proposed by Indian tax authorities under Chapter X.

## What Are Transfer Pricing Services in India?

Any cross-border group operating in {country} faces one recurring question from the tax authorities: is the price right? Transfer pricing services in India answer that question end to end — designing intercompany pricing policy, documenting the arm's length analysis, certifying it in Form 3CEB, filing the group-level master file and CbCR obligations, and defending positions through assessments, dispute resolution panels and, where certainty justifies it, advance pricing agreements. The objective is not just compliance; it is a documented position that survives scrutiny years later.

## Who Must Comply: Sections 92 to 92F Explained

Chapter X of the Income-tax Act, 1961 applies to international transactions between associated enterprises as defined in Section 92A — entities participating, directly or indirectly, in the management, control or capital of one another. Section 92B sweeps in services, goods, intangibles, loans, guarantees, cost sharing, business restructuring and even the issue of shares to a non-resident related party. Deemed international transactions extend the net to arrangements where a unrelated party is involved but the terms are influenced by an associated enterprise. Specified domestic transactions face an analogous arm's length regime at a lower threshold. Two duties follow: compute income at an arm's length price, and maintain contemporaneous documentation under Rule 10D — documentation that must exist before it is demanded, not assembled afterwards.

## Arm's Length Methods Under Rule 10B

Method selection drives everything — comparables, margins and the defence narrative:

| Method | Best suited to |
| --- | --- |
| Comparable uncontrolled price | Royalties, licences, transactions with direct price comparables |
| Resale price | Distributors adding limited value |
| Cost plus | Contract manufacturers, software development captives |
| Transactional net margin method (TNMM) | IT services, GCCs, shared services — India's workhorse method |
| Profit split | Jointly developed intangibles, highly integrated operations |

## Documentation, Form 3CEB and the Three-Tier Regime

Aligned with the {international_policy_body} BEPS framework, Indian compliance runs on three tiers: a local file with OECD-consistent arm's length margin (ALM) documentation, a group master file, and country-by-country reporting. The filing calendar:

| Obligation | Form | Threshold | Due date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accountant's report u/s 92E | Form 3CEB | Every entity with international transactions | 31 October of the assessment year |
| Master file | Form 3CEAA | Group consolidated revenue above Rs 500 crore or international transactions above Rs 100 crore | As prescribed u/s 286 |
| Country-by-country report | Form 3CEAB | Group consolidated revenue above the 750 million euro equivalent | Within 12 months of the reporting year end |

Form 3CEB filing fails quietly when left to October: comparables thin out, intercompany agreements no longer match reality, and margins drift without explanation. Disciplined practices refresh intercompany agreements, run benchmarking searches early and reconcile 3CEB disclosures with the return of income and financial statements.

## Penalties and Transfer Pricing Adjustments

Non-compliance is expensive by design. Failure to maintain documentation attracts a penalty of 2% of the value of international transactions under Section 271AA; failure to furnish information on demand attracts a further 2% under Section 271G. In assessment, the officer substitutes an arm's length price, taxes the adjustment with interest, and under Section 92CE secondary adjustments convert the delta into a deemed advance repayable by the company. Because master file and CbCR data now flow across jurisdictions, an inconsistent Indian position surfaces in other group countries too — the adjustment risk is global, not local.

## Advance Pricing Agreements and Dispute Resolution

Where recurring transactions generate annual controversy, an advance pricing agreement with the {direct_tax_authority} fixes the method and arm's length range for future years — unilateral or bilateral through MAP with the treaty partner. Where disputes already exist, the sequence runs through Draft Assessment Orders and DRP proceedings, with ITAT and beyond for contested matters, and mutual agreement procedure where double taxation persists. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is transfer pricing compliance mandatory in India?

Yes. Any Indian entity transacting with an associated enterprise must determine and document arm's length prices under Sections 92 to 92F, maintain Rule 10D documentation contemporaneously and file the Form 3CEB accountant's report by 31 October of the assessment year.

### What is the due date for Form 3CEB?

Form 3CEB, the accountant's report under Section 92E, is due by 31 October of the assessment year for the preceding accounting year. Benchmarking and documentation must be ready well before, since the report certifies an arm's length analysis that already exists.

### When does the master file and CbCR apply?

Master file in Form 3CEAA applies where consolidated group revenue exceeds Rs 500 crore or international transactions exceed Rs 100 crore. Country-by-country reporting in Form 3CEAB applies to groups with consolidated revenue above the 750 million euro equivalent.

### What is the penalty for not maintaining TP documentation?

A penalty of 2% of the value of international transactions applies under Section 271AA for failure to maintain documentation, with a matching 2% under Section 271G for failure to furnish information. Adjustments, interest and secondary adjustments can follow separately.

### Who needs transfer pricing services?

Indian subsidiaries and captives of foreign groups, exporters billing related parties, companies paying royalties or management fees abroad, startups issuing shares to non-resident investors at related-party direction, and Indian parents with foreign subsidiaries — essentially any cross-border related-party dealing.

### What is an advance pricing agreement?

An APA is a forward-looking agreement with the {direct_tax_authority} fixing the transfer pricing method and arm's length range for covered transactions, typically for five years. Unilateral and bilateral APAs are available, and bilateral APAs coordinate with the treaty partner through MAP.
