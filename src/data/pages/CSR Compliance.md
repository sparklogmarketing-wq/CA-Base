> CSR compliance is the set of obligations under Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013 that applies to companies crossing prescribed net worth, turnover or net profit thresholds: constituting a CSR committee, spending 2 percent of average net profits on Schedule VII activities, reporting through CSR-2 and transferring unspent amounts within statutory deadlines.

## CSR Compliance Requirements Under Section 135

Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013 pulls a company into the CSR framework when it meets any one of three thresholds in the immediately preceding financial year: net worth of Rs 500 crore or more, turnover of Rs 1,000 crore or more, or net profit of Rs 5 crore or more. The board must then constitute a CSR committee of at least three directors, including one independent director; unlisted companies not required to appoint an independent director need two directors on the committee, and a foreign company's Indian business needs two persons including its resident representative. CSR compliance is mandatory — a company cannot opt out by declining to spend; below the thresholds, a committee may still be constituted voluntarily.

## The 2 Percent Rule: Calculating the CSR Obligation

The board-approved CSR obligation is 2 percent of the average net profits of the company for the three preceding financial years, computed under Section 198. A company incorporated for less than three years calculates on the years available. Spend must go to Schedule VII activities, implemented directly or through registered implementing agencies, and the board must adopt and disclose a CSR policy on its website. For large programmes, Rule 8 requires independent impact assessment where the obligation is Rs 10 crore or more and projects exceed Rs 1 crore.

## CSR-1 and CSR-2 Filings for Companies and Implementing Agencies

Two forms do most of the administrative work. Form CSR-1 registers implementing agencies — trusts, societies and Section 8 companies — on the MCA CSR portal, with a valid CSR Registration Number; an agency without CSR-1 registration is not eligible to receive CSR funds from companies. Form CSR-2 is the annual report on CSR, filed with the RoC along with the AOC-4, disclosing the policy, committee composition, amounts obligated and spent, transfers to the Unspent CSR Account and details of implementing agencies. Groups with several entities must consolidate reporting so the right entity files — our [ROC compliance](/roc-compliance) calendar carries both filings.

## Unspent CSR Amounts and Transfers to Schedule VII Funds

Unspent money is where most companies slip, and where the 2020 amendment introduced real penalties:

| Situation | Rule | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Unspent amount on approved ongoing projects | Transfer to a separate Unspent CSR Account within 30 days of financial year end; spend within three financial years | Failing which, 30 percent of the unspent amount must go to a fund specified in Schedule VII within 30 days of the three-year window closing |
| Unspent amount other than ongoing projects | Transfer to a fund specified in Schedule VII | Within six months of the financial year end |
| Continuing default | Penalty on the company: twice the unspent amount or Rs 50 lakh, whichever is less; officers in default: one-tenth of the amount or Rs 2 lakh, whichever is less | Under Section 135(7) |

## Schedule VII Areas and Where CSR Funds Can Go

Schedule VII defines the permitted canvas: eradicating hunger, poverty and malnutrition; preventive healthcare and sanitation; education and employability; gender equality and women empowerment; environment sustainability and conservation; protection of national heritage, art and culture; benefits to armed forces veterans and their dependents; rural and slum area development; and disaster management and relief, including contributions to notified funds. Activities must be undertaken in India; spending that benefits only employees or their families does not qualify.

## Tax Treatment of CSR Expenditure

CSR spending is generally not deductible as a business expense under Section 37 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 — the Finance Act, 2021 inserted an express exclusion. The exception: CSR activities that form part of the company's normal course of business. Donations to eligible funds and institutions can still qualify under Section 80G, subject to conditions. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which companies must spend on CSR?

Companies meeting any one threshold in the preceding financial year: net worth of Rs 500 crore, turnover of Rs 1,000 crore, or net profit of Rs 5 crore or more.

### What is the 2 percent CSR rule?

The CSR committee must ensure spending of 2 percent of average net profits of the preceding three financial years, computed under Section 198 of the Companies Act, 2013.

### What is Form CSR-1 and who needs it?

CSR-1 registers implementing agencies — trusts, societies and Section 8 companies — on the MCA CSR portal. Registered entities become eligible to receive CSR funds from companies.

### What happens to unspent CSR amounts?

Unspent amounts for ongoing projects move to an Unspent CSR Account within 30 days of year-end; other shortfalls go to a Schedule VII fund within six months. Penalties apply for continuing default.

### Is CSR expenditure tax deductible?

Generally no. CSR spending is not deductible as a business expense under Section 37, though donations to eligible institutions can still qualify for Section 80G deductions.

### Is CSR impact assessment mandatory?

Yes for large programmes: companies with a CSR obligation of Rs 10 crore or more need independent impact assessment for projects with outlay of Rs 1 crore or more, through an eligible institution.
