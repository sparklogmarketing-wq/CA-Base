## Tax Audit under Section 44AB

A tax audit in {city} applies where turnover crosses Rs 1 crore, or Rs 10 crore where at least 95% of receipts and payments are digital — a threshold many {city} IT and SaaS businesses cross without realising it. The engagement culminates in Form 3CA or 3CB with the Form 3CD clause-by-clause report, due by September 30 of the assessment year, with the return due by October 31.

Beyond compliance, the 3CD report is where reporting inconsistencies surface: section 43B(h) MSME payment timelines, depreciation claims, loan discrepancies and GST turnover reconciliation. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is statutory audit mandatory for every private company?

Yes. Under the Companies Act 2013, every private company must have its accounts audited annually regardless of turnover. The first auditor is appointed by the board within 30 days of incorporation, and annual audits continue for the life of the company.

### What is the turnover limit for tax audit under section 44AB?

The default threshold is Rs 1 crore of turnover. It rises to Rs 10 crore where at least 95% of receipts and payments are digital. Businesses below these limits may still opt for an audit voluntarily or under presumptive conditions.

### When is the tax audit report due?

The tax audit report in Form 3CA/3CB with Form 3CD is due by September 30 of the following assessment year, and the income tax return itself is due by October 31 for audit cases.

### Can internal audit be outsourced to your firm?

Yes. Section 138 permits internal audit to be conducted by a chartered accountant in practice. Many companies outsource the function to gain independence, sector experience and continuity without adding headcount.

### What records should we keep ready for an audit?

Books of account, bank statements, GST returns, TDS returns, fixed asset and inventory registers, loan agreements and key contracts. We share a tailored checklist after understanding your business and accounting system.
