> Foreign subsidiary tax planning is the structuring discipline that governs how an Indian subsidiary of a foreign parent is set up, taxed and repatriated from — covering entity choice between branch and subsidiary, corporate tax rates, dividend withholding u/s 115A, POEM residency risk and treaty relief.

{brand} advises inbound investors on the full lifecycle — from [subsidiary incorporation](/subsidiary-entity) through annual tax and exit. This guide sets out the planning levers that decide how much of Indian profit actually reaches the foreign shareholder.

## Branch vs Subsidiary Taxation

The first structural decision changes everything downstream:

| Factor | Branch office | Indian subsidiary |
|---|---|---|
| Legal status | Extension of the foreign company | Separate domestic company |
| Tax rate | Foreign company rates (35% plus surcharge and cess) | Domestic company rates; 22% regime u/s 115BAA available |
| Post-tax repatriation | No dividend tax on remitting branch profits | Dividend to parent attracts withholding u/s 115A |
| Sector access | RBI approval route only for specified activities | Broad FDI access under the automatic route |
| Contracting and credibility | Limited; parent bears all liability | Full contracting capacity; limited liability |

A subsidiary wins on rates, sector access and liability ring-fencing; the branch avoids dividend tax on repatriation. Sector permissions and setup mechanics for the branch route sit on our [branch and liaison office](/branch-liaison-office) page.

## Dividend Taxation u/s 115A: The Repatriation Waterfall

Profits reach the parent through layers of tax:

1. **Corporate tax in the subsidiary** — 25.17% effective under the 22% regime with surcharge and cess.
2. **Withholding on dividend** — the default u/s 115A for a foreign company is 20% plus surcharge and cess. Under a DTAA, treaty rates — frequently 5% to 15% — apply with a valid **Tax Residency Certificate (TRC)**, Form 10F and a no-PE declaration.
3. **Credit at home** — the parent claims foreign tax credit under its domestic rules and the treaty; our [DTAA and foreign tax credit guide](/dtaa-foreign-tax-credit) covers the India-side Form 67 mechanics.

Because the treaty rate applies per the parent's residence, choosing the holding jurisdiction before the first dividend is declared is classic foreign subsidiary tax planning — post-hoc treaty shopping is restricted by limitation-of-benefits clauses.

## POEM Rules: When the Foreign Parent Becomes Indian

Under Section 6(3), a foreign company is **resident in India if its Place of Effective Management is in India at any time in the year**. POEM means the place where key management and commercial decisions — strategy, contracts, finance — are, in substance, made. Boards that only rubber-stamp decisions actually taken from India create residency exposure: worldwide income, Advance Tax obligations and ITR filing follow. The safe harbours in the guiding principles — turnover and passive-income tests for active businesses outside India — help, but board minutes, meeting locations and decision records must match reality. Where group functions sit in India, intercompany charges need defensible pricing — see our [transfer pricing](/transfer-pricing) practice.

## Withholding Tax Planning on Cross-Border Flows

Flows from the subsidiary to the parent — royalties, technical service fees, interest, management charges — attract withholding u/s 195 at rates in Section 115A or the DTAA, whichever the non-resident validly claims. Planning levers:

- Maintain current **TRCs, beneficial-owner forms and no-PE declarations** for every flow.
- Obtain **lower-withholding certificates** where treaty rates or expenditure-linked deductions justify them.
- Document arm's-length margins for service fees, since excessive management charges are a recurring assessment trigger.

## Pillar Two and the Global Minimum Tax

The {international_policy_body}-driven Pillar Two framework imposes a 15% minimum effective tax rate on large multinationals, jurisdiction by jurisdiction, through rules enacted in many parent countries. India has engaged with the framework but had not enacted GloBE rules into domestic law at the time of writing. Groups above the revenue threshold should still model India's rate against the 15% floor and their parent's top-up exposure — a now-standard input in foreign subsidiary tax planning.

## Exit and Repatriation

Exits are taxable in India: long-term capital gains on unlisted Indian shares attract the concessional rate (12.5% from FY 2024-25) in the hands of a non-resident seller, subject to treaty overrides. A **buyback** is now treated as a deemed dividend in the shareholder's hands rather than a capital gain — often the worse route. Every outward remittance runs through the AD bank with **Form 15CA/15CB**, and share transfers require the FEMA filings on our [FEMA and RBI reporting](/fema-RBI-reporting) page. Sequencing the exit two to three years ahead preserves both the gain profile and the repatriation path.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the tax rate for a foreign company in India?

A foreign company's Indian income is taxed at a basic rate of 35%, plus the applicable surcharge and 4% health and education cess. A domestically incorporated subsidiary can instead elect the concessional 22% regime u/s 115BAA, roughly 25.17% effective.

### How are dividends to a foreign parent taxed?

The default rate u/s 115A is 20% plus surcharge and cess. Where a DTAA prescribes a lower rate — frequently 5% to 15% — the parent can claim it with a valid Tax Residency Certificate, Form 10F and a no-permanent-establishment declaration.

### When does a foreign company become an Indian tax resident?

If its Place of Effective Management is in India at any time during the year. Where key management and commercial decisions are actually made from India, the foreign company is residable and taxable on worldwide income, regardless of incorporation.

### Are branch losses available to the parent?

Branch losses stay in the branch return and cannot shelter the parent's overseas income; they carry forward within the India filing as domestic rules allow. A subsidiary's losses also remain with the subsidiary — neither structure exports losses home.

### Does Pillar Two apply in India?

India has signed on to the {international_policy_body} framework but had not enacted the GloBE income-inclusion rules into domestic law at the time of writing. Group modelling is still essential where the parent sits in a jurisdiction that has implemented the 15% minimum.
