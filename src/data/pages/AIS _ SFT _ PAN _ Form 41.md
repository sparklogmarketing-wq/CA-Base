> The Annual Information Statement (AIS) is a comprehensive, PAN-linked statement available on the income-tax e-filing portal that consolidates your financial transactions — TDS, TCS, interest, dividends, securities trades and SFT-reported high-value transactions — so you can verify the data before filing your income-tax return, alongside Form 26AS and the Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS).

## What the Annual Information Statement (AIS) Contains

The AIS, maintained by the {direct_tax_authority} on the e-filing portal since November 2021, aggregates information reported by deductors, collectors and reporting entities against your PAN. Typical components include TDS and TCS details, advance tax and refunds, savings account and deposit interest, dividend income, securities and mutual fund transactions, foreign remittances, GST turnover information and high-value transactions reported under the SFT framework. Each line shows the reported value and a modified value after deduplication, and every entry carries feedback options so errors can be flagged.

## Form 26AS vs AIS vs TIS: How They Differ

Form 26AS remains the credit statement for tax deducted or collected; the AIS is the wide-angle view of your financial footprint; and the TIS is the simplified summary your return processing actually works from.

| Statement | What it shows | Where to access | Primary use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Form 26AS | TDS, TCS, advance tax, self-assessment tax, refunds and demands; SFT data also appears since 2020 | TRACES or the e-filing portal | Verifying tax credits before filing |
| Annual Information Statement (AIS) | TDS/TCS plus SFT transactions, interest, dividends, securities and mutual fund trades, foreign remittances and more, with reported and modified values | AIS tab on the e-filing portal | Self-verification and feedback on errors |
| TIS | Category-wise summary with processed and derived values, generated from the AIS | Auto-generated on the portal with the AIS | Shows the values used in return processing |

## SFT Reporting Under Section 285BA: Categories and Thresholds

Section 285BA obliges specified entities — banks, issuers and registrars, brokers, card issuers, property sub-registrars and foreign-exchange dealers, among others — to file a Statement of Financial Transactions (SFT) in Form 61A under Rule 114E, quoting the PAN of the transacting person. Reporting is due by 31 May following the financial year. Commonly reported transactions include:

| Reportable transaction (aggregated per financial year) | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Cash deposits in a savings bank account | Rs 10 lakh or more |
| Cash payments into or deposits in a current account | Rs 1 crore or more |
| Credit card bill payment — cash | Rs 1 lakh or more |
| Credit card bill payment — any other mode | Rs 10 lakh or more |
| Purchase or sale of immovable property | Rs 30 lakh or more |
| Purchase of shares / mutual fund units | Rs 10 lakh or more |
| Purchase of bonds or debentures | Rs 5 lakh or more |
| Purchase of foreign exchange, including drafts and traveller cheques | Rs 10 lakh or more |
| Cash payment against bills for goods or services | Rs 2 lakh or more |

## How SFT Reporting Links to Your PAN

The PAN is the common key across the entire reporting chain: reporting entities file transactions against it, the AIS aggregates them, and the system compares the total with your filed return. Where reported amounts exceed returned income, the department may issue e-campaign nudges and compliance emails, make adjustments in intimation under section 143(1), or select the case for scrutiny — at which point professional [scrutiny and notice support](/income-tax-scrutiny-notices) becomes essential. Quoting an incorrect PAN misroutes data and creates mismatches for the wrong taxpayer, so entities must apply PAN due diligence; individuals should ensure their PAN is linked and correctly quoted in KYC records.

## Reportable Accounts and the Common Reporting Standard

India implements the OECD's Common Reporting Standard through Chapter X-A of the Income-tax Rules (rules 114G to 114H). Reporting financial institutions apply due diligence under rule 114H to identify reportable accounts — financial accounts held by foreign tax residents — and file them in Form 61B, enabling automatic exchange of account information between countries. The reciprocal data on Indian residents' overseas accounts flows back into the AIS framework, which is why undisclosed foreign assets are increasingly visible to the authorities. Note that the earlier Form 61 annual information regime was superseded by Form 61A under the current Rule 114E framework. Non-residents and returning NRIs should review [NRI taxation](/nri-taxation) implications before assuming cross-border data goes unnoticed.

## If Your AIS Is Wrong or Incomplete

Use the feedback facility on each AIS line to report inaccurate or duplicate entries; the reporting entity is then asked to verify and, where needed, revise its filing. Download the AIS and TIS, reconcile them with Form 26AS, Form 16/16A and bank or broker statements, and keep proofs for everything you disclose. If income reported in the AIS was genuinely missed, an updated return under section 139(8A) within 24 months can regularise the position. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it mandatory to check the AIS before filing my return?

Not legally, but it is prudent. Return processing uses this data, and unreported high-value transactions can trigger e-campaign nudges or scrutiny notices.

### What is the difference between AIS and Form 26AS?

Form 26AS focuses on tax credits — TDS, TCS, advance tax and refunds. The AIS is broader, covering SFT-reported transactions, interest, dividends, securities trades and foreign remittances.

### What if a PAN-linked transaction shows a wrong value in my AIS?

Give feedback on the AIS portal so the reporting entity is asked to verify and correct it, and retain documentary proof for the figure you report in your return.

### What is Form 61B?

Reporting financial institutions file Form 61B to report reportable accounts under the Common Reporting Standard, applying due diligence under rule 114H of the Income-tax Rules.

### Can I file an updated return if I missed SFT-reported income?

Yes. Under section 139(8A) you may file an updated return (ITR-U) within 24 months of the end of the relevant assessment year, paying the additional tax prescribed.
