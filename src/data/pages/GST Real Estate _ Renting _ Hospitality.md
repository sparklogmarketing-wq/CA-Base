> GST on real estate applies mainly to under-construction property at 5% (1% for affordable housing) without input tax credit, to commercial rent at 18%, and to hotel accommodation and food services at slab-linked rates, while completed residential units, plotted land and residential rent paid to individuals remain outside the levy.

## Where GST on Real Estate Applies — and Where It Does Not

GST on real estate is a patchwork by design: the levy attaches to construction services embedded in property transactions, not the transfer of finished assets as such. Sale of completed residential units and of land or plots without construction service carries no GST, while anything involving construction attracts the real estate scheme. Overlaying this are renting, works contracts and hospitality, each with its own rate mechanics. Developers and investors also face the direct-tax overlays of [RERA and JDA taxation](/rera-jda-real-estate), kept deliberately separate here.

## Rates on Under-Construction Property

Under-construction residential property sold by a promoter is taxed under the 2019 real estate project scheme; those headline rates continue after the 2025 restructure.

| Transaction | GST rate | Input tax credit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under-construction residential unit (non-affordable), in an RREP | 5% on the whole value | No |
| Affordable residential unit, in an RREP | 1% on the whole value | No |
| Commercial units within a residential real estate project | Generally follow the project's non-affordable entry | No |
| Plot within an RREP sold with construction | Project rate (1% or 5%) | No |
| Plot or completed unit without construction service | Exempt | Not applicable |

An affordable residential unit is one with carpet area up to 60 square metres in metropolitan cities or 90 square metres elsewhere, priced up to Rs 45 lakh. Because no input tax credit is available to the developer under the scheme, the tax becomes a cost flowing into pricing — review procurement so inputs carrying unusable credit are not loaded into the structure. Blocked-credit mechanics sit on our [GST ITC and RCM rules](/gst-itc-rcm) page.

## GST on Commercial and Residential Rent

Renting of commercial property is a taxable supply at 18% in the forward charge, with the landlord claiming eligible input tax credit and issuing GST invoices. The residential side is where confusion lives. Renting a residential dwelling for use as residence is exempt when the landlord is an individual not in the rental business. Where such a dwelling is supplied to a GST-registered person, however, the tenant pays the GST under reverse charge under section 9(5) — the classic case being a company leasing a residential unit for staff accommodation. Registered tenants in {city} should build this deposit into monthly compliance, since the tax is payable in cash before credit is claimed.

## Works Contracts and Developer Arrangements

Works contract GST depends on what the contract builds. Construction services for a real estate project follow the project scheme at 5% or 1%, works contracts for commercial real estate are taxed at 18% with credit to the recipient, and construction services for third parties attract 18%. Joint development arrangements add a layer: supplies by a promoter to the landowner against development rights can fall under reverse charge where the promoter is not registered, with landowner shares settled at actual cost. Document rate and classification positions on composite contracts against current [GST rates and notifications](/gst-rates-notifications) before invoicing.

## Hotels, Restaurants and Hospitality

Hospitality rates were reshaped by the restructure effective 22 September 2025. Accommodation up to Rs 7,500 per night is taxed at 5% without input tax credit; higher-value rooms attract 18% with credit. Restaurant services are generally taxed at a uniform 5% without credit, ending older premises-based variations. Hotel operators should review costing — room-price banding near the Rs 7,500 line and banquet or outdoor catering arrangements — case by case, keeping classification evidence for audits.

## TDS Under Section 194-IA: An Income-Tax Point, Not GST

Property buyers frequently conflate two laws. TDS under section 194-IA of the Income-tax Act, 1961 requires the buyer of immovable property (consideration of Rs 50 lakh or more) from a resident seller to deduct 1% and deposit it through Form 26QB; the seller claims it in the income-tax return. It has nothing to do with GST. Buying an under-construction property from a registered builder typically involves both: 5% GST in instalment invoices and 1% income-tax TDS once payments cross the threshold. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the GST rate on under-construction property?

Under-construction residential property is taxed at 5% without input tax credit, and affordable housing units at 1%, also without credit. Completed residential units and plots without any construction service are exempt.

### What counts as affordable housing for GST?

Affordable residential units are those with carpet area up to 60 square metres in metropolitan cities or 90 square metres elsewhere, priced up to Rs 45 lakh, as defined for the concessional 1% rate.

### Is GST applicable on commercial rent?

Yes. Renting of commercial property is a taxable supply of service at 18%, charged by the landlord in the forward charge. The landlord can take eligible input tax credit, and tenants receive a GST invoice for their own credit.

### When does reverse charge apply on residential rent?

Renting of a residential dwelling for use as residence is normally exempt for individual landlords. Where the tenant is a registered person, the tenant pays GST on the rent under reverse charge under section 9(5).

### What is the GST rate on hotel rooms?

Since 22 September 2025, accommodation priced up to Rs 7,500 per night is taxed at 5% without input tax credit, while higher-value rooms attract 18% with credit. Restaurant services in hotels are generally taxed at 5% without credit.

### Is TDS under section 194-IA part of GST?

No. Section 194-IA of the Income-tax Act requires a 1% TDS by buyers of immovable property above Rs 50 lakh, deposited through Form 26QB. It is a direct-tax obligation and operates independently of GST.
