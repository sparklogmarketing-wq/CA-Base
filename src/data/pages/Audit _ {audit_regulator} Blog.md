> The {audit_regulator} is India's independent audit regulator constituted under section 132 of the Companies Act 2013. NFRA audit oversight covers its investigation of auditor misconduct for listed and large unlisted public companies, its review of audit working papers and quality, and its role in setting and enforcing accounting and auditing standards.

Audit in India now operates under two lenses: the auditor's professional standards and the statutory gaze of the {audit_regulator}. NFRA has moved oversight from peer review towards regulator-led enforcement, and boards must assume the audit file will eventually be read by the regulator. This {brand} column tracks that shift.

## What NFRA Audit Oversight Covers

Five themes define the regulator's operating model:

| Oversight theme | What it involves | Who is affected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Section 132 mandate | Auditors of listed and large unlisted companies | Boards and audit committees |
| Auditor accountability | Investigation of misconduct, penalties, debarment | Audit firms and signing partners |
| Enforcement direction | Regulator-led reviews replacing peer discipline | Larger firms first |
| Quality reviews | Examination of files, scepticism, documentation | Every audit within NFRA's class |
| ICFR linkage | Testing and reporting on internal financial controls | Listed and specified unlisted companies |

## The Section 132 Mandate: Who NFRA Regulates

Section 132 of the Companies Act 2013 created the {audit_regulator} as an independent authority with jurisdiction over the auditors of listed companies, large unlisted public companies and bodies referred by the Central Government. Its functions span recommending standards, monitoring compliance and investigating misconduct. The significance is structural: audit discipline for this class no longer rests solely with the professional institute, and findings can carry penalties up to five times the fees received, plus debarment.

## Auditor Accountability in Practice

NFRA oversight converts auditor accountability into a document trail. Investigations have probed risk assessment planning, evidenced scepticism, group scoping and corroboration of management representations. The working paper file is the centrepiece: an undocumented judgment is treated as not made. For CFOs this reframes the audit itself — evidence for significant estimates and related-party transactions is now also defence preparation. Our [statutory audit](/statutory-audit) practice is built around documentation that survives regulatory review.

## The Direction of NFRA Enforcement

Across successive enforcement cycles the {audit_regulator} has examined audits of high-profile failures regardless of firm size, published its findings, and expected technical positions — revenue recognition, provisioning, going concern — to rest on contemporaneous evidence. The direction is towards fuller public accountability of auditors. Audit committees that only discuss fees and timelines are increasingly out of step.

## Quality Reviews and the Working Paper Standard

NFRA's review approach treats each audit file as evidence of a quality system. Recurring themes include weak documentation over journal entries and estimates, over-reliance on management-produced information, and gaps between risk assessment and procedures. The response is engagement review checkpoints, independence confirmations and monitoring that mirrors the regulator's questions. Treat auditors' demands for more access as quality, not friction.

## ICFR and the Link to Financial Reporting Quality

The auditor's report on internal financial controls over financial reporting has become a natural review ground; reviews trace how control deficiencies were evaluated, aggregated and communicated to those charged with governance. Management's own ICFR discipline — reconciliations, IT general controls, access management — directly shapes audit outcomes, and our [internal audit](/internal-audit) co-sourcing bridges management testing and statutory assurance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which companies fall under NFRA jurisdiction?

NFRA covers the auditors of companies listed in India and unlisted public companies with paid-up capital of Rs 500 crore or more or annual turnover of Rs 1,000 crore or more, plus companies referred by the Central Government.

### How is NFRA different from ICAI?

ICAI is the professional body that qualifies and self-regulates chartered accountants. For the specified class of companies, NFRA acts as the statutory investigator and disciplinary authority for audit misconduct, so the same audit can face both.

### What powers does NFRA have?

NFRA can investigate misconduct without prior reference, summon persons, examine records and working papers, exercise civil-court powers, and impose monetary penalties up to five times the fees received, along with debarment.

### Does NFRA audit companies itself?

No. NFRA oversees the auditors. It reviews audit working papers and quality, investigates alleged professional or other misconduct, and recommends accounting and auditing standards to the Central Government.

### What is ICFR reporting and why does NFRA examine it?

Auditors of listed and specified unlisted companies must opine on the adequacy and operating effectiveness of internal financial controls. Weak ICFR often explains misstatements, so NFRA treats the ICFR audit trail as a core review focus.

### How should audit committees respond to NFRA oversight?

Treat audit documentation, auditor independence and ICFR findings as board-level matters. Review engagement quality indicators, ensure control deficiencies are closed, and expect the audit file to stand on its own under review.
