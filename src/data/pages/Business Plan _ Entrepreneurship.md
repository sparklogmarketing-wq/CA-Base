> A business plan is a written document that sets out what a business will sell, to whom, how it will operate and how it will make money, combining strategy, market analysis and financial projections into a single roadmap that guides founders, banks and investors alike.

## Why a Business Plan Still Matters

A business plan forces hard choices onto paper before capital is committed. It disciplines pricing and cost assumptions, gives co-founders and early hires a shared target, converts fundraising conversations from promises into evidence, and provides the baseline against which actual performance is measured. For lenders, it becomes the backbone of the detailed project report; for equity investors, the first filter on whether the opportunity deserves a meeting.

## Core Components of a Business Plan

Most credible plans follow a recognisable structure. Keep each section short, specific and consistent with the numbers.

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Executive summary | One to two pages capturing the opportunity, traction, ask and return — often the only part read in full. |
| Company overview | Legal structure, promoters, vision and stage — from [company registration](/company-registration) documents through current status. |
| Market analysis | Target segments, size, growth drivers and competition, with sources for every claim. |
| Products and services | What is sold, pricing, and the problem each offering solves. |
| Business model | How the company earns: revenue streams, unit economics and gross-margin logic. |
| Marketing and sales | Channels, customer acquisition cost expectations and the sales process. |
| Operations | Delivery model, supply chain, technology, locations and key hires. |
| Team | Promoters and key people, with roles and relevant track record. |
| Financial projections | Three-to-five-year statements, assumptions and funding requirement. |
| Risk analysis | Key risks — market, regulatory, execution — and mitigation. |
| Appendix | Detailed workings, market data, quotations and regulatory approvals. |

## Business Plan Financial Projections: What to Build

Financial projections are the section scrutinised hardest. Build them driver-based — volume, price, conversion, hiring, capacity — rather than as a growth percentage applied to last year.

- **Profit and loss:** monthly for the first 12 to 24 months, then annual to year five, showing gross margin and operating leverage.
- **Balance sheet:** receivables, inventory and payables days so working capital is funded, not ignored.
- **Cash flow:** the statement that decides survival — show burn, runway and the month the plan turns cash-positive.
- **Break-even and sensitivity:** the volume or price at which the model breaks even, plus best, base and downside cases.
- **Funding requirement and use of funds:** how much, in what instruments, and exactly what each tranche achieves.

Every number should trace back to a written assumption; reviewers trust plans they can interrogate. When projections need to withstand investor or transaction scrutiny, pair the plan with dedicated [financial modeling and valuation](/financial-model-pitch-deck-valuation) support.

## Business Plan vs Financial Model vs Pitch Deck

The three artefacts answer different questions. The business plan is the narrative plus headline numbers. The financial model is the driver-based workbook behind those numbers, where scenarios are tested. The pitch deck is the 10-to-15-slide conversation version for investors. Keep them consistent — a mismatch between deck and model is the fastest way to lose credibility in diligence.

## Tailoring the Plan to Its Audience

Banks and term lenders read for repayment: project cost, means of finance, debt service coverage and collateral coverage, presented in detailed-project-report format. Equity investors read for scalability: market size, unit economics, defensibility and exit. Both respond to honest sensitivity analysis. Startups should also sequence the plan with the compliance and incentive stack — [DPIIT recognition](/startup-registration-dpiit), incentive eligibility and the readiness work that precedes [startup funding and valuation support](/startup-funding-angel-vc).

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Revenue curves that hockey-stick without capacity or funnel logic; working capital treated as an afterthought; assumptions locked in a spreadsheet no one documents; regulatory and tax costs — GST, payroll, professional tax — missing from the cost base; and a plan written once and never revisited. Treat the document as a living operating tool reviewed quarterly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should a business plan be?

Most plans work best at 15 to 25 pages. Use a one-page summary for first conversations and keep detailed workings in the financial model and appendix.

### How far ahead should financial projections go?

Three to five years, with monthly detail for at least the first year and clear, documented assumptions behind revenue, margins and working capital.

### What do investors read first in a business plan?

The executive summary, unit economics and cash runway: whether the model scales, how much is being raised and exactly what the funds will achieve.

### Is a business plan required for a bank loan?

Lenders typically ask for a detailed project report covering project cost, means of finance, revenue assumptions and repayment indicators such as the debt service coverage ratio.

### Can a chartered accountant help with my business plan?

Yes. A CA can pressure-test assumptions, build credible financial projections and align the plan with tax, GST and regulatory implications before investors or banks see it.
