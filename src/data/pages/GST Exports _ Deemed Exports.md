> GST on exports follows the zero-rating principle: goods and services exported from India, or supplied to SEZ units and developers, bear no GST burden in India, either by paying IGST and claiming its refund or by exporting under a LUT and claiming a refund of accumulated input tax credit.

## How GST on Exports Works: The Zero-Rating Principle

GST on exports is built on a simple idea: consumption is taxed where it happens, not where goods are produced. Exports of goods or services, and supplies to SEZ units or developers for authorised operations, are zero-rated under section 16 of the IGST Act. The seller is not expected to absorb the tax; the law offers two recovery routes, and choosing between them is a key cash-flow decision each year. Services exports are zero-rated on the same principle where the place of supply lies outside India or payment is received in convertible foreign exchange.

## Route 1: Export with Payment of Tax (IGST Refund)

Under the IGST route, the exporter charges and pays IGST on the shipping bill. Refund then processes automatically: GSTR-1 export data (Table 6A) is matched with shipping bill data from customs, and the sanctioned refund of IGST paid is disbursed to the designated bank account. The advantages are speed and automation, and the refund is not contingent on realisation evidence at the processing stage. Where the department delays beyond the statutory sixty-day window, interest applies under section 56 of the CGST Act — one more reason to align GSTR-1 and customs data before filing.

## Route 2: Export Without Payment (LUT and ITC Refund)

The more common route today is export under a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) in FORM GST RFD-11, filed online each financial year with no turnover restriction. Exporting under LUT means no IGST is charged, and the exporter claims a refund of accumulated input tax credit arising from exports and deemed exports through FORM GST RFD-01. Key discipline points:

- Refund is available only on the balance of credit after deducting exempt-use and blocked credit under section 17(5), so keep the [GST ITC and RCM rules](/gst-itc-rcm) clean through the year.
- Bank realisation evidence (BRC or FIRC) must be furnished within the prescribed time; without realisation, the claim is treated as provisional and eventually recoverable.
- Sanctioned ITC-route refunds are credited to the electronic credit ledger, not paid in cash, so plan working capital accordingly.

## Route Comparison at a Glance

| Parameter | With payment of tax | Without payment (LUT) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tax charged on invoice | IGST paid | Nil, under LUT in FORM GST RFD-11 |
| Refund claim | Automatic, from GSTR-1 plus shipping bill | FORM GST RFD-01 by the exporter |
| Refund paid into | Bank account | Electronic credit ledger |
| Realisation evidence | Not required at processing stage | BRC/FIRC required within prescribed time |
| Working-capital profile | Cash outflow until refund arrives | No upfront tax; credit locked until refund |

## Deemed Exports Under Section 31

Not every export leaves the country. Section 31 of the CGST Act recognises deemed exports: goods manufactured in India and supplied against an Advance Authorisation, or for production or procurement by an EOU, STP, EHTP or BTP unit. The supplier pays GST at domestic rates, and the recipient claims a refund of the tax paid on such inward supplies, subject to the deemed export policy conditions and documentation from the authorised unit. The benefit is that the supply chain serving exporters enjoys the same cash-flow neutrality as direct exports, even though the goods physically stay in India. ## Merchant Exports at 0.1%

A merchant exporter buys goods from a domestic manufacturer and exports them in its own name. To keep the export chain competitive, such procurement is taxed at a concessional 0.1% — 0.05% each under CGST and SGST, or 0.1% IGST — subject to notification conditions: goods intended for export, exported within the prescribed period, and duty drawback not exceeding the prescribed ceiling. Merchant exporters should also watch the interplay with input credit: the supplier's concessional output tax limits the credit available upstream, which is why pricing between manufacturer and merchant exporter needs joint planning. Both routes feed the same [GST return filing](/gst-return-filing) obligations, including export reporting in GSTR-1.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a zero-rated supply under GST?

Exports of goods or services, and supplies to SEZ units or SEZ developers, are zero-rated under section 16 of the IGST Act. No GST burden remains in India, and exporters can recover input tax through refunds.

### What is a LUT and why file one?

A Letter of Undertaking in FORM GST RFD-11 lets you export without paying IGST and claim a refund of accumulated input tax credit. It is filed online, is valid for a financial year and carries no turnover restriction.

### Which refund route is better: IGST or ITC?

The IGST route refunds tax paid on the shipping bill and processes automatically against GSTR-1 data. The ITC route refunds accumulated credit after realisation evidence. The better route depends on liquidity, ITC depth and documentation readiness.

### What are deemed exports under section 31?

Deemed exports are goods manufactured in India and supplied against Advance Authorisations or to EOU, STP, EHTP and BTP units. The supplier charges tax at domestic rates, and the recipient claims the refund subject to departmental conditions.

### What is the GST rate for merchant exports?

Goods procured by merchant exporters for export are taxed at 0.1% (0.05% each under CGST and SGST, or 0.1% IGST), subject to conditions including export within the prescribed period and limitation on duty drawback claims.

### Do I need a BRC for GST export refunds?

Yes for ITC-route refunds: bank realisation evidence must be furnished within the prescribed time. For the IGST route, shipping bill and GSTR-1 data drive automatic processing, though realisation remains relevant for later verification.
