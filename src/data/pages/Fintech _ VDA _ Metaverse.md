> Virtual digital asset taxation in India taxes crypto and NFT transfers at a flat 30% under Section 115BBH with no loss set-off, imposes 1% TDS under Section 194S, and leaves fintechs to navigate RBI authorisation, payment-gateway GST and the characterisation of metaverse services. Reporting discipline in Form 26AS, AIS and ITR schedules decides audit exposure.

## How Does Virtual Digital Asset Taxation in India Work?

The framework is deliberately one-sided in the exchequer's favour. Gains on transferring a virtual digital asset are taxed at a flat 30% under Section 115BBH, with only the cost of acquisition deductible — no expenses, no indexation, no slab benefits. The proviso goes further: loss from a VDA transfer cannot be set off against any other income, nor carried forward, even against gains on other VDAs. Gifts of VDAs are taxed in the recipient's hands subject to the general gift rules, valued at fair market value.

| Event | Tax treatment |
|---|---|
| Sale or swap of crypto or NFT | 30% on gain under Section 115BBH; only cost of acquisition deductible |
| Gift of a VDA | Taxable at fair market value in recipient's hands, subject to gift-tax exceptions |
| Airdrop or play-to-earn reward | Taxable on receipt under the appropriate head; later transfers follow Section 115BBH |
| Salary paid in crypto | Taxed as salary at fair market value; employer withholds under Section 192 |

On top of the rate sits collection: Section 194S requires the buyer — or the exchange, where it settles the transaction — to deduct 1% of consideration once annual value crosses Rs 10,000 for most persons or Rs 50,000 for specified persons, at the earlier of credit or payment. The deduction surfaces in the seller's Form 26AS and AIS, which is precisely how undisclosed positions now get noticed. Report VDA transfers in the dedicated ITR schedule; our [ITR filing](/itr-filing) practice handles these schedules and the reconciliation behind them.

## What GST Direction Applies to Crypto and VDAs?

Indirect-tax treatment has moved by direction rather than settled statute. A direction issued by the indirect-tax authority brought VDA transactions within the GST net, applying the 28% demerit-rate treatment as it has been interpreted across subsequent clarifications. The unresolved question is characterisation — whether a crypto transaction is supply of goods, supply of services, or merely transfer of an actionable claim — and that uncertainty flows straight into invoicing, valuation and input-credit positions for exchanges. Prudent exchanges document their classification, charge consistently, and reconcile exchange-level data with returns, because the enforcement direction is clear even where the law is contested. The registration and rate context sits in our [GST services](/gst-services) hub.

## How Are Payment Aggregators and Gateways Taxed and Regulated?

Two tracks run in parallel. On the regulatory track, the direction issued by the Reserve Bank requires online payment aggregators and gateways to hold authorisation under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, with net-worth thresholds, escrow-account norms, KYC obligations and technology audits — and application windows that have opened and closed across successive cycles, making timing itself a compliance decision.

On the tax track, the aggregator's own revenue — MDR, settlement fees, processing charges — is a taxable service at 18% GST, while platform businesses selling goods or notified services through their apps carry e-commerce TCS and Section 9(5)-type liabilities described in our [IT, SaaS and e-commerce guide](/it-saas-ecommerce). Merchants should reconcile settlement statements against GST invoices monthly; reconciliation failures are the most common trigger for both credit mismatches and scrutiny notices.

## How Are Metaverse Services Characterised?

Metaverse economics compress several legal categories into one checkout. Land parcels and avatar items issued as NFTs sit inside the VDA regime, while world subscriptions, event tickets and in-world services behave like ordinary digital services — potentially OIDAR supplies with their own registration mechanics. Income character matters as much as rate: an investor's occasional disposal leans toward capital gains under Section 115BBH, while systematic trading looks like business income, changing the book-profit computation and audit profile. Rewards earned in-world — airdrops, play-to-earn yields, staking distributions — are generally taxable on receipt, with cost bases recorded contemporaneously. Keep wallet-level records from day one; reconstructing them after a notice is far more expensive.

## What Funding and Reporting Risks Should Fintechs Watch?

Funding into fintechs and VDA ventures draws ordinary but muscular provisions: share premiums at unexplained rates attract Section 56(2)(viib) scrutiny directions, and unexplained credits flow into the [Section 68](/section-68-unexplained-credits) framework with its source-of-funds demands. Foreign investment follows the FDI and reporting path summarised on our [startup registration and DPIIT](/startup-registration-dpiit) page. Fundamentally, virtual digital asset taxation in India rewards businesses that can evidence every wallet movement — valuations, settlement trails, TDS certificates — before anyone asks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the tax rate on crypto gains in India?

Gains from transferring a virtual digital asset are taxed at a flat 30% under Section 115BBH, with no deduction allowed except the cost of acquisition. The rate applies to both residents and non-residents on India-taxable transfers.

### Can crypto losses be set off against other income?

No. The proviso to Section 115BBH bars set-off of loss from transferring a virtual digital asset against any other income, and the loss cannot be carried forward even against future VDA gains.

### When does 1% TDS under Section 194S apply?

On transfer of a virtual digital asset, the buyer or exchange deducts 1% of consideration, from Rs 10,000 of annual value for most persons and Rs 50,000 for specified persons subject to tax audit, credited at the earlier of credit or payment.

### Is GST levied on cryptocurrency transactions?

A direction issued by the indirect-tax authority brought VDA transactions within GST at the 28% demerit rate as interpreted over time. Classification as goods, services or actionable claims remains contested, so exchanges should document positions carefully.

### How are metaverse purchases and rewards taxed?

Characterisation decides: assets meeting the VDA definition follow Section 115BBH, while service subscriptions follow normal GST and income-tax rules. Airdrops, play-to-earn rewards and salaries in crypto are generally taxable at receipt under their respective heads.

### Do payment aggregators need RBI authorisation?

Yes. The direction issued by the Reserve Bank requires online payment aggregators and gateways to seek authorisation under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, with net-worth conditions, escrow norms and ongoing compliance reviewed across application windows.
