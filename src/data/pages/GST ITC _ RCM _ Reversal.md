> GST input tax credit is the mechanism under the CGST Act, 2017 that allows a registered person to set off GST paid on inputs, input services and capital goods against output tax, subject to section 16 conditions, the section 17(5) blocked-credit list, Rules 37, 42 and 43 reversals and reverse charge obligations.

## How GST Input Tax Credit Works: The Section 16 Conditions

GST input tax credit is the largest working-capital lever in the GST system, but it is claim-driven, not automatic. Section 16(2) lays down cumulative conditions, tightened by the requirement that the supplier's invoice actually reaches your GSTR-2B, and Rule 36(4) caps the credit to what appears in that statement.

| Section | Condition | What to verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 16(2)(a) | Possession of a valid tax invoice or specified document | Invoice number, GSTIN, HSN and tax breakup |
| 16(2)(aa) | Invoice details furnished by the supplier in GSTR-1 or IFF | Invoice appears in your GSTR-2B |
| 16(2)(b) | Actual receipt of goods or services | GRNs and service completion records |
| 16(2)(c) | Tax actually paid by the supplier to the government | Supplier's GSTR-3B compliance |
| 16(2)(d) | Valid return filed by the recipient | Your own GSTR-3B filed on time |

Under section 16(4), the claim must be made by 30 November following the end of the relevant financial year, or by the date of filing the annual return, whichever is earlier. Missed credits are usually lost, so monthly reconciliation beats year-end clean-up.

## Blocked Credits Under Section 17(5)

Even a perfectly documented invoice can carry credit the law disallows. Section 17(5) blocks ITC on, among other items:

- Motor vehicles seating up to 13 persons, except for transport services, passenger training or further supply.
- Food and beverages, outdoor catering, club, health and fitness memberships and beauty treatment, subject to narrow exceptions.
- Works contract services used for constructing immovable property and construction on own account — except plant and machinery, and works contracts received for further supply of construction services.
- Corporate social responsibility expenditure, added expressly by the Finance Act, 2023.
- Membership of a club, personal consumption, gifts and free samples.
- Tax paid under sections 74, 129 and 130.

Blocked credits booked in error must be reversed in the annual return and frequently surface as demands in departmental scrutiny.

## Reversal of ITC: Rules 37, 42 and 43

Three reversal rules do most of the damage in practice:

- **Rule 37** requires reversal, with interest, if a supplier is not paid within 180 days of the invoice date. The credit can be reclaimed once payment is made.
- **Rule 42** reverses credit on inputs and input services attributable to exempt supplies, using the ratio of exempt turnover to total turnover; re-credit is allowed if they later become taxable.
- **Rule 43** spreads capital goods credit reversal for exempt use over 60 equal monthly instalments, following the same proportionate principle.

Re-credit is also available under section 18(4), and credit on capital goods, semi-finished and finished goods must be reversed when a registered person opts for the composition scheme and files FORM GST ITC-03.

## Reverse Charge Mechanism Under GST: Sections 9(3), 9(4) and 9(5)

Under the reverse charge mechanism, the recipient rather than the supplier pays GST. For registered persons the liability applies without any turnover threshold, and the tax must be paid in cash before it is claimed as credit.

| Supply | Provision | Who discharges GST | Typical example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Transport by GTA | 9(3) | Specified registered recipients | Factory-bound consignments moved by road |
| Services by an advocate | 9(3) | Business entity receiving the service | Litigation or advisory fees |
| Services of a director | 9(3) | The company | Sitting fees to independent directors |
| Import of services | 9(4) and IGST Act section 3(1)(d) | Indian recipient | Royalty or management fees from an overseas group |
| Renting of residential dwelling | 9(5) | Registered tenant | Company leasing staff accommodation |

Recipients can claim the RCM amount as credit in the same period, provided the supply is used for business and is not blocked under section 17(5). GTA deserves special attention: transporters who opted for the forward charge take certain supplies outside RCM, and the specified-recipient categories under section 9(3) decide who pays.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the conditions for claiming input tax credit under GST?

Section 16 requires possession of a valid tax invoice, actual receipt of goods or services, the supplier having deposited the tax and filed returns, and the invoice appearing in your GSTR-2B. The claim must be made within the section 16(4) time limit.

### Which credits are blocked under section 17(5)?

Blocked credits include motor vehicles with seating up to 13 persons, food and beverages, club and fitness memberships, works contract services used for constructing immovable property, construction on own account, CSR expenditure, personal consumption, gifts and free samples, and tax paid under sections 74, 129 and 130.

### What is the Rule 37 reversal of ITC?

If a supplier is not paid within 180 days of the invoice date, the corresponding input tax credit must be reversed with interest. Once payment is made, the credit can be reclaimed in the return for the period following the payment.

### Who pays GST under the reverse charge mechanism?

The registered recipient pays. Notified cases under section 9(3) include GTA transport, services by advocates, director services and sponsorship; section 9(4) covers notified procurements from unregistered suppliers; and section 9(5) covers specified supplies such as renting of residential dwellings to registered tenants.
