## Step-by-Step Online Company Incorporation Process

Online company registration is fully digital on the {corporate_affairs_authority}'s MCA portal:

1. **Digital signatures (DSC)** for all proposed directors.
2. **Name approval** through SPICe+ Part A, applying the proposed names against the name-availability rules.
3. **SPICe+ Part B** — incorporation details with e-MoA (INC-33) and e-AoA (INC-34), so no physical charter documents are needed.
4. **Linked forms** — AGILE-PRO-S for EPFO, ESIC, professional tax (where applicable in {state}), bank account opening and, optionally, GSTIN.
5. **Issuance** — Certificate of Incorporation with CIN, along with PAN and TAN.
6. **Commencement** — declaration in INC-20A within 180 days, after subscription money is deposited in the company account.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does company registration take in {city}?

Most straightforward private limited incorporations complete in one to two weeks once documents are ready and the name is approved. Timelines depend on {corporate_affairs_authority} processing, name availability and digital signature issuance.

### What is the minimum capital required to start a private limited company?

There is no minimum paid-up capital requirement under the Companies Act 2013. Founders choose a sensible authorised capital based on funding plans, since stamp duty and government fees vary with authorised capital.

### Can one person register a company alone?

Yes. A one person company (OPC) allows a single member and director, with a nominee. It offers limited liability with simpler compliance, and can convert to a private limited company as the team grows.

### What happens immediately after incorporation?

File INC-20A within 180 days, appoint the first auditor within 30 days, open a current account, deposit subscription money, and complete GST registration if applicable. We run a first-year compliance calendar from day one.

### Is GST registration needed at incorporation?

Not always. Registration is required above Rs 40 lakh turnover for goods or Rs 20 lakh for services, and for inter-state supply, e-commerce selling or voluntary cases. Many startups register voluntarily to claim ITC.
