> DPIIT startup recognition is the certificate issued by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade under the Startup India initiative, confirming that an entity qualifies as a startup. Recognition unlocks self-certification, IPR fee rebates, tender relaxations, funding schemes and eligibility for the Section 80-IAC tax holiday.

{brand} takes founders through startup registration and DPIIT startup recognition together — choosing the right entity, filing the recognition application, and sequencing the tax and funding benefits that follow. Here is the full picture.

## Who Qualifies: Startup India Eligibility Criteria

Recognition is available only to entities meeting all of these conditions:

- Incorporated as a **private limited company, registered partnership firm or LLP** in India — see our [company registration](/company-registration) and [LLP registration](/llp-registration) services
- **Under ten years old** from incorporation or registration
- **Turnover below Rs 100 crore** in any financial year since inception
- **Not formed by splitting up or reconstructing** an existing business
- Working towards **innovation, improvement of products, processes or services**, or a scalable business model with potential for employment or wealth creation

MSMEs and startups are different regimes — many entities hold both [Udyam and DPIIT](/msme-udyam-registration) registrations, each serving different benefits.

## How DPIIT Startup Recognition Works: Process and Documents

The application is free and filed online on the Startup India portal:

1. **Create the profile** — entity details, director or partner information, and PAN.
2. **Upload the documents** — certificate of incorporation or registration, and a brief write-up explaining the problem, the innovative solution, and scalability, supported by a pitch deck, website or video.
3. **Self-certify the conditions** — age, turnover, originality of the entity, and that it is not formed by splitting an existing business.
4. **Receive the recognition certificate** — a permanent DPIIT number is issued, typically within days if the write-up is credible; queries can extend the wait.

The innovation narrative is where applications fail. We draft it against what evaluators actually look for, and maintain the [ROC compliance](/roc-compliance) record that supports the self-certifications.

## The Tax Angle: 80-IAC and the End of Angel Tax

Two tax points founders get wrong:

- **Section 80-IAC** allows a 100% deduction of profits for three consecutive years out of the first ten — but it is a separate Inter-Ministerial Board application with its own conditions and scheme validity. Recognition alone does not grant it; see our detailed [startup tax exemptions guide](/startup-tax-exemptions) before committing a holiday year.
- **Angel tax** u/s 56(2)(viib) — the levy on share premiums above fair market value — was abolished for all investors from FY 2025-26 by the Finance Act 2025. Recognition no longer shields against it because it no longer exists; valuation reports still matter for FEMA and [funding](/startup-funding-angel-vc) documentation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which entities can get DPIIT recognition?

Only a private limited company, a registered partnership firm or an LLP incorporated in India can apply. The entity must be under ten years old, have turnover below the prescribed Rs 100 crore ceiling, and work towards innovation or a scalable model.

### How long does DPIIT recognition take?

The application is online and self-certified; most recognitions arrive within days of filing, though queries on the innovation write-up can extend timelines. A permanent DPIIT number and recognition certificate are issued digitally on approval.

### Does recognition automatically give the 80-IAC tax holiday?

No. The 80-IAC deduction is a separate application to the Inter-Ministerial Board, subject to the scheme's validity and prescribed conditions. DPIIT recognition is the necessary first step, not the exemption itself.

### Is the angel tax still applicable?

Section 56(2)(viib), which taxed share premiums above fair value, was abolished for all investors from FY 2025-26 by the Finance Act 2025. Fundraising documentation still requires valuation reports for FEMA and company law purposes.

### What documents are needed for recognition?

Certificate of incorporation, PAN, director or partner details, a short write-up on the problem, innovation and scalability, and supporting proof such as a pitch deck, website or video. Self-certifications confirm eligibility conditions.
