> DTAA and foreign tax credit rules determine how income taxed in two countries is spared a double levy: section 90 of the Income-tax Act gives treaty relief precedence where beneficial, section 91 grants unilateral relief, and sections 90(4) and 90(5) with Rule 128 require a TRC and Form 67 to claim credit for foreign taxes paid.

## How Do DTAA and Foreign Tax Credit Rules Work Together?

India taxes residents on global income and non-residents on India-sourced income, so cross-border earners get taxed twice by design. The fix has two layers. Where India has a double taxation avoidance agreement with the other country, section 90 lets the taxpayer apply the treaty, and under section 90(2) the more beneficial of the treaty and the Act prevails. Where no treaty exists, section 91 steps in with unilateral relief computed on the doubly taxed income. The working rules differ: treaty relief usually takes the form of concessional withholding rates or allocation of taxing rights, while the foreign tax credit mechanism converts foreign taxes actually paid into a credit against Indian liability.

## Claiming Foreign Tax Credit: Sections 90(4), 90(5) and Rule 128

The credit regime is documentary and unforgiving. Section 90(4) requires a tax residency certificate from the government of the foreign country containing the prescribed particulars; section 90(5) and Rule 128BC add electronic Form 10F where the TRC omits any required detail. Rule 128 sets the substantive conditions: the income must accrue or arise outside India and be offered to tax here, the foreign tax must actually be paid in the source country, and the credit is capped at the Indian tax attributable to that income — excess credit lapses. The claim is made in Form 67, filed before the due date under section 139(1).

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) | Certificate of foreign residence; mandatory under section 90(4) |
| Form 10F | Electronic declaration of treaty particulars missing from the TRC |
| Form 67 | Statement of foreign income and foreign tax for the credit claim |
| Foreign withholding statements | Proof of tax actually deducted and paid abroad |

## Key DTAA Articles: Business Profits, Royalties and Tie-Breakers

Article 7 allocates business profits: a foreign enterprise's profits are taxable only in its residence state unless it carries on business through a permanent establishment in India, in which case only profits attributable to that PE are taxable here — making PE risk analysis the core of inbound structuring. Article 12 fixes source-state rates on royalties and fees for included technical services, commonly 10 to 15 percent depending on the treaty and subsequent protocols. Article 13 taxes capital gains on immovable property in the situs state. Article 4 resolves dual residence through tie-breaker rules: for individuals, the sequence is permanent home, centre of vital interests, habitual abode and nationality; for companies, newer Indian protocols prefer place of effective management. Getting the residence position wrong infects the withholding rate, the [transfer pricing](/transfer-pricing) exposure and the credit arithmetic alike.

## Advance Rulings: Certainty for Cross-Border Investors

Chapter XIX-B provides an advance ruling mechanism: a binding decision on the tax consequences of a proposed transaction. Applications lie before the Board for Advance Rulings — the forum constituted after the erstwhile Authority for Advance Rulings was wound up in 2021 — by non-residents, residents with foreign income or cross-border associated enterprise transactions, and certain wholly-owned subsidiaries of foreign companies. A ruling binds the applicant and the income-tax authorities. Section 196F adds a targeted incentive: income of foreign institutions from notified securities purchased in foreign currency can be exempt where an application for advance ruling is duly made.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the deadline to file Form 67?

Form 67 must be furnished on the e-filing portal before the due date for filing the return of income under section 139(1) for the year in which the foreign income is offered.

### Do I need a tax residency certificate every year?

Yes. Section 90(4) requires a TRC from the foreign government's specified authority containing the prescribed particulars, obtained for the relevant previous year in which the treaty relief is claimed.

### Can the foreign tax credit exceed Indian tax on that income?

No. Rule 128 caps the credit at the Indian tax payable on the doubly taxed income; any excess foreign tax over the Indian liability lapses and cannot be carried forward or refunded.

### Which law prevails on a conflict, the DTAA or the Act?

The more beneficial one. Under section 90(2), where the treaty gives more favourable treatment than the Income-tax Act, the treaty applies to the resident's advantage.

### What is a tie-breaker rule in a DTAA?

It resolves dual residence. For individuals the sequence runs through permanent home, centre of vital interests, habitual abode and nationality; for companies, most treaties now use place of effective management.

### Who can seek an advance ruling in India?

Non-residents, residents with foreign income or transactions with associated enterprises abroad, and certain wholly-owned subsidiaries of foreign companies can apply to the Board for Advance Rulings under Chapter XIX-B.
