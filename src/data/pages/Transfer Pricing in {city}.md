## When Do Transfer Pricing Rules Apply in {city}?

If your {city} company transacts with a non-resident group entity — a parent that cross-charges software development costs, a foreign affiliate paying or receiving royalty, a shared services centre billing group customers — Chapter X of the Income-tax Act, 1961 applies. Sections 92 to 92F require every international transaction with an associated enterprise, and every deemed international transaction, to be carried out at an arm's length price determined using prescribed methods. The net is wide: it captures services, tangible goods, intangibles, loans and guarantees, cost sharing, business restructuring, and even the issue of shares to a non-resident related party.

## What Documentation Does the Law Require?

Transfer pricing documentation under Rule 10D must exist contemporaneously — before the specified date — and must justify why your pricing delivers an arm's length result. {international_policy_body}-consistent practice has converged on a three-tier structure: a local file with the transaction detail and an arm's length margin analysis (ALM documentation), a group master file, and country-by-country reporting. The workhorse of the local file is the benchmarking study, applying one or more prescribed methods:

| Method | Typical use case in {city} |
| --- | --- |
| Comparable uncontrolled price | Royalties, licensing, commodity-style transactions with comparables |

| Cost plus | Contract manufacturing, software development captives |

| Profit split | Jointly developed IP and highly integrated operations |

## Form 3CEB, Master File and CbCR: Forms and Deadlines

The compliance calendar for cross-border groups is unforgiving, and each filing stands on the documentation behind it:

| Form | Purpose | Applicability | Due date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Form 3CEB | Accountant's report u/s 92E on international transactions | Every entity with reportable transactions | 31 October of the assessment year |
| Form 3CEAA | Master file | Consolidated group revenue above Rs 500 crore, or international transactions above Rs 100 crore | Within the time prescribed u/s 286 |
| Form 3CEAB | Country-by-country report | Groups with consolidated revenue above the 750 million euro threshold | Within 12 months of the reporting year end |

An entity in {city} with even one reportable transaction needs Form 3CEB regardless of group size, which is why disciplined Form 3CEB filing starts months before the October deadline — comparables are selected, margins are tested, and gaps are fixed while they can still be fixed.

## What Are the Consequences of Non-Compliance?

The penal consequences are among the harshest in the Act. Failure to maintain documentation attracts a penalty of 2% of the value of international transactions under Section 271AA; failure to furnish information on a notice attracts a matching 2% under Section 271G. Beyond penalties, the assessing officer can adjust your declared price to an arm's length price, tax the delta, levy interest, and trigger secondary adjustments that convert the adjustment into a deemed advance repayable to the company. Undisclosed group arrangements surface easily once data flows through master file and CbCR channels — compliance is no longer optional even for mid-sized subsidiaries.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who needs transfer pricing documentation in {city}?

Any {city} entity transacting with a non-resident associated enterprise — cross-charged software development, royalty, management fees, loans, shared services or share issuances to related non-residents — must maintain documentation and obtain the Form 3CEB accountant's report each year.

### What is the Form 3CEB due date?

Form 3CEB, the accountant's report under Section 92E, is due by 31 October of the assessment year for the preceding accounting year. It must be certified by a chartered accountant and matched to contemporaneous documentation maintained under Rule 10D.

### What are the master file and CbCR thresholds?

Master file in Form 3CEAA applies where consolidated group revenue exceeds Rs 500 crore or international transactions exceed Rs 100 crore. Country-by-country reporting in Form 3CEAB applies where the group's consolidated revenue crosses the equivalent of 750 million euros.

### What is the penalty for TP non-compliance?

Failure to maintain prescribed documentation attracts a penalty of 2% of the value of international transactions under Section 271AA, and a matching 2% penalty under Section 271G for failure to furnish information. Adjustments, interest and further penalties can follow an adverse assessment.

### What TP issues are common for {city} companies?

Captive software and GCC service charges, royalty and brand fees, management support costs, R&D cost sharing, intercompany loans and share issuances to non-resident parents. These recurring transactions need tested benchmarking and consistent margins year after year.

### Can disputes be resolved without litigation?

Yes. Options include Advance Pricing Agreements negotiated with the {direct_tax_authority} for multi-year certainty, mutual agreement procedure under the applicable DTAA, and dispute resolution panel or ITAT representation where adjustments are already proposed.
