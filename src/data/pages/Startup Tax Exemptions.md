> Startup tax exemptions in India bundle the section 80-IAC profit holiday, the abolition of angel tax under section 56(2)(viib), the section 54GB exemption for investors, ESOP tax deferral for employees and relaxed loss carry-forward under section 79 — a package that can save an eligible startup and its backers crores across the funding lifecycle.

## What Startup Tax Exemptions Are Available in India?

| Benefit | Statutory anchor | Core condition |
|---|---|---|
| 100% profit holiday for 3 years | Section 80-IAC | DPIIT-recognised company or LLP within the notified incorporation window |
| Angel tax abolished | Section 56(2)(viib) removed | No premium-based taxation from AY 2026-27 |
| Investor-side gains exemption | Section 54GB | Residential property gain reinvested in eligible startup equity |
| LTCG fund route | Section 54EE | Notified fund investment up to Rs 50 lakh (window lapsed 31 March 2022) |

| Loss carry-forward relaxation | Proviso to section 79 | Shareholder continuity test eased for eligible startups |

The common gateway is DPIIT recognition, covered on our [startup registration and DPIIT recognition](/startup-registration-dpiit) service page.

## Section 80-IAC: The 100% Tax Holiday Explained

Section 80-IAC is the centrepiece of startup tax exemptions in India. An eligible startup deducts 100 percent of its profits for any three consecutive years out of the first ten years from incorporation — the founder chooses the window, ideally the three highest-profit years. Eligibility requires incorporation as a private limited company or an LLP; turnover not exceeding Rs 100 crore in any previous year; non-formation by splitting up an existing business; and demonstrable work towards innovation or employment and wealth creation, certified through DPIIT recognition. Incorporation must fall within the notified eligibility window, which Parliament has extended from time to time — most recently to 31 March 2030 under the Finance Act 2025. A startup running losses in its early years should plan the three-year claim window before profitability arrives, since the choice shapes the [tax audit](/tax-audit) and compliance calendar.

## Angel Tax: Abolished for All Investors from AY 2026-27

Section 56(2)(viib) once taxed share premium above the fair market value determined by the assessing officer when a closely held company raised money from resident investors — the notorious angel tax. Relief first came narrowly: DPIIT-recognised startups were exempted from 2019. The Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 then removed the provision altogether for all classes of investors with effect from assessment year 2026-27 (FY 2025-26). The abolition ends premium-order disputes on domestic rounds, but valuation documentation still matters: pricing records, board minutes and the share valuation report remain the paper trail for future diligence, ESOP grants and exit computations.

## Section 54GB and 54EE: Exemptions for Founders and Investors

Section 54GB lets a resident individual or HUF selling a residential house route the capital gain into equity of an eligible startup — one where the seller holds more than 50 percent and the funds buy new plant, machinery or assets for an eligible business. The gain-based cap is Rs 10 crore, with a five-year lock-in on the shares. It turns a founder's family property sale into tax-free startup capital. Section 54EE allowed long-term gains to be parked in a notified long-term assets fund up to Rs 50 lakh, but the investment window closed on 31 March 2022. For residential-house exemptions on the other side of the balance sheet, see our [capital gains sections 54 and 54F](/capital-gains-section-54-54f) guide.

## ESOP Tax Deferral: Sections 156 and 192(1C)

ESOPs create a taxable perquisite at exercise, and employees of cash-burning startups often faced tax before liquidity. Section 192(1C) fixes this for eligible startups: the employer defers the TDS on the ESOP perquisite and pays it within 14 days after the earliest of the expiry of five years from allotment, the employee ceasing employment, or the employee selling the shares. The startup must maintain the prescribed declaration and reporting discipline — payroll systems need configuring, and the deferral interacts with [individual tax planning](/individual-tax-planning) for each employee.

## Carrying Forward Losses Despite Section 79

Ordinarily, business and capital losses lapse on a change of more than 49 percent in shareholding. The proviso to section 79 relaxes this for eligible startups: accumulated losses carry forward so long as all shareholders who held shares on the last day of the loss year continue to hold shares on the last day of the year in which the loss is set off. Founder dilution across funding rounds therefore does not destroy the loss basket.

## FAQs

### Who qualifies as an eligible startup for section 80-IAC?

A private company or LLP recognised by DPIIT, incorporated within the notified window, with turnover up to Rs 100 crore in any previous year, not formed by splitting up an existing business, and working towards innovation or employment generation.

### Is angel tax still applicable to startups?

No. The Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 abolished the section 56(2)(viib) angel tax for all classes of investors with effect from assessment year 2026-27, ending premium-based taxation of resident-funded rounds.

### How long does the 80-IAC tax holiday last?

One hundred percent deduction of profits for any three consecutive years, chosen by the startup out of its first ten years from incorporation.

### Can LLPs claim the 80-IAC deduction?

Yes. Eligible startups for the 80-IAC holiday include private limited companies and limited liability partnerships that hold DPIIT recognition and meet the other conditions.

### How does the ESOP tax deferral for startups work?

Under section 192(1C), an eligible startup defers the TDS on the ESOP perquisite, paying it within 14 days after the earliest of five years from allotment, the employee leaving, or the employee selling the shares.

### What happens to losses if founders dilute their stake?

For eligible startups, the proviso to section 79 relaxes the shareholding test: losses carry forward so long as all shareholders of the loss year continue to hold shares on the last day of the set-off year.
