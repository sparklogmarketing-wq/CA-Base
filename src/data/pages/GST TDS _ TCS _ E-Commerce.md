> GST TDS and TCS are withholding mechanisms under the CGST Act, 2017: tax deducted at source under section 51 by specified government and notified entities at 2%, and tax collected at source under section 52 by e-commerce operators at 1%, each credited to the counterparty through the GST portal.

## GST TDS and TCS: Two Withholding Levers, One Portal

GST TDS and TCS exist so that tax reaches the government at the point of payment rather than at return filing. They are distinct from income-tax TDS, and one feature is easy to miss: the deducted or collected amount is not lost by the supplier — it flows into their electronic cash ledger and is usable against output liability. Both sit on top of ordinary [GST return filing](/gst-return-filing), and each generates its own return, GSTR-7 or GSTR-8.

## TDS Under Section 51: Who Deducts and How Much

Section 51 requires tax deduction at source at 2% of taxable value — 1% each under CGST and SGST, or 2% where IGST applies. The deductor list is narrow: government departments and establishments, local authorities, government agencies and boards, PSUs and notified persons. Practical rules that decide whether you must deduct:

- The threshold is Rs 2.5 lakh of total value of supply under a single contract; the aggregate matters, not each invoice.
- TDS applies only where the supplier is registered; supplies from unregistered suppliers fall outside it.
- Deduction is at the time of payment or credit to the supplier's account, whichever is earlier.
- The deductor files FORM GSTR-7 by the 10th of the following month, after which a TDS certificate in FORM GSTR-7A is auto-generated for the deductee.

Government vendors should reconcile certificates against the cash ledger monthly; undistributed credit is a working-capital leak.

## TCS Under Section 52 and the Net-Value Basis

Section 52 shifts the withholding duty to e-commerce operators, who collect 1% of the net taxable value of supplies made through their platform — 0.5% each under CGST and SGST, or 1% IGST. Collection happens at the earlier of crediting the seller's account or making payment. The computation basis matters: Section 14 of the CGST (Amendment) Act, 2018 moved TCS to the net value of taxable supplies made through the operator, and the 2024 amendments effective 16 July 2024 further aligned it, so returns, exchanges and cancellations reduce the base. Operators file FORM GSTR-8 by the 10th of the following month; the amount lands in each seller's cash ledger for claim in GSTR-3B.

## Section 9(5): When the E-Commerce Operator Is Liable

Section 9(5) creates a different responsibility: for certain services, the operator is treated as the supplier and pays GST itself, not the underlying provider. Notified categories include passenger transport where the operator does not own the vehicles, house-keeping where it does not own the workers, and accommodation services notified in specified States. Restaurant services through platforms changed with the 2024 amendments: such supplies are now generally deemed to be made by the restaurant itself, so GST-registered restaurants bill GST on their own invoices, and platform-level levy and TCS on those supplies no longer applies. Platforms and restaurants should revisit their invoicing chains against these rules — see our page on [GST issues by sector](/gst-sector-issues).

## Claiming the Credit: GSTR-3B and Reconciliation

For the recipient or seller, withholding under GST is recoverable, not a cost. TDS appears in the electronic cash ledger once the deductor files GSTR-7; TCS once the operator files GSTR-8. Amounts are claimed in [GSTR-3B](/gst-return-filing) and set off against any output tax liability. Quarterly reconciliation between certificates (GSTR-7A for TDS) and the cash ledger catches deductors who filed late and operators whose GSTR-8 computation does not match the seller's payouts.

## How GST Withholding Differs from Income-Tax TDS

| Aspect | GST TDS / TCS | Income-tax TDS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Statute | CGST/SGST/IGST Act, 2017 | Income-tax Act, 1961 |
| Rates | 2% (section 51) or 1% (section 52) | Varies by section, e.g. 1% under 194-IA |
| Applies to | Notified government entities and e-commerce platforms | Broad base of payers, including e-commerce operators under 194-O |
| Credit mechanics | Electronic cash ledger; claimed in GSTR-3B | Form 26AS / AIS; claimed in the ITR |

Income-tax TDS under section 194-O runs in parallel with GST TCS — one does not discharge the other. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who must deduct TDS under GST section 51?

Government departments and establishments, local authorities, government agencies, PSUs and notified persons deduct 2% (1% CGST plus 1% SGST, or 2% IGST) where payment under a single contract exceeds Rs 2.5 lakh in aggregate and the supplier is registered.

### What is the TCS rate for e-commerce operators?

E-commerce operators collect 1% tax at source (0.5% each under CGST and SGST, or 1% IGST) on the net value of taxable supplies made through their platform, at the earlier of crediting or paying the seller.

### How do I file GSTR-7 and GSTR-8?

TDS deductors file GSTR-7 and e-commerce operators file GSTR-8 by the 10th of the following month. Filed details flow into the counterparty's electronic cash ledger, which can be claimed in the GSTR-3B of the relevant period.

### How is GST TDS credit claimed?

Deductions reflect in the deductee's electronic cash ledger once GSTR-7 is filed; a certificate in FORM GSTR-7A is auto-generated. The credit is claimed in GSTR-3B and can be used against any output tax liability.

### Does GST TCS replace income-tax TDS on e-commerce?

No. Income-tax TDS under section 194-O applies separately to e-commerce operators on gross amounts of e-commerce transactions. GST TCS under section 52 is an indirect-tax levy; both may apply to the same platform.

### What changed for restaurants on platforms after 2024?

Following the 2024 amendments effective 16 July 2024, restaurant services supplied through an e-commerce operator are generally treated as supplied by the restaurant itself, so registered restaurants bill GST directly and platform-level TCS on such supplies has ceased.
