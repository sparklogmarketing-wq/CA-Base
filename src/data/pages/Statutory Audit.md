> A statutory audit is the independent examination of a company's or LLP's books of account by a qualified chartered accountant, as mandated by law. Statutory audit services under the Companies Act 2013 verify that financial statements present a true and fair view and comply with applicable accounting standards and disclosure requirements.

## What Are Statutory Audit Services Under the Companies Act 2013?

Statutory audit is a legal requirement, not a management choice. Section 129 of the Companies Act 2013 requires boards to lay audited financial statements before members, and Section 143 governs the auditor's powers and duties. The auditor examines books, vouchers, bank confirmations and disclosures, then issues an independent report on whether the accounts show a true and fair view. Where applicable, the engagement adds CARO 2020 reporting and an internal financial controls assessment. Engagements are structured so the audit feeds cleanly into ROC filings, tax audit and lending requirements.

## Who Must Get a Statutory Audit Done?

| Entity | Statutory audit requirement |
|---|---|
| Private limited company | Mandatory for all, irrespective of turnover or capital (Sections 129 and 139) |
| Public company, OPC and small company | Mandatory; earlier exemptions for one person and small companies withdrawn |
| Limited Liability Partnership | Mandatory where contribution exceeds Rs 25 lakh or turnover exceeds Rs 40 lakh in any financial year (Rule 24, LLP Rules 2009) |
| Trusts, societies and NGOs | Audit per the governing statute, regulation or donor conditions |

## Appointment, Rotation and Tenure of Statutory Auditors

The board must appoint the first auditor within 30 days of incorporation; failing that, members may do so within 90 days. Subsequent auditors are appointed by members at every AGM, with Form ADT-1 filed with the {corporate_affairs_authority} within 15 days. Section 139(2) caps individuals at five consecutive years and firms at ten, with a cooling-off period in specified cases. Section 141 disqualifications â€” indebtedness, certain business relationships and prescribed shareholdings by relatives â€” must be cleared before appointment.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

Failure to appoint an auditor or file audited accounts attracts Section 147 penalties: a fine of Rs 50,000 to Rs 5,00,000 on the company and Rs 10,000 to Rs 1,00,000 on every officer in default. Late AOC-4 and MGT-7 filings add fees of Rs 100 per day. An incomplete audit also blocks dividend declarations, borrowings and tender participation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a statutory audit mandatory for every private limited company?

Yes. Under the Companies Act 2013, every company â€” private, public, one person or small â€” must have its accounts audited by a chartered accountant, irrespective of turnover or paid-up capital. The first auditor must be appointed within 30 days of incorporation.

### Do LLPs need a statutory audit?

Under Rule 24 of the LLP Rules 2009, audit is mandatory where contribution exceeds Rs 25 lakh or annual turnover exceeds Rs 40 lakh. LLPs below these limits may still opt for a voluntary audit.

### What is the difference between a statutory audit and a tax audit?

A statutory audit under the Companies Act 2013 opines on true and fair financial statements. A tax audit under Section 44AB of the Income-tax Act 1961 reports income-tax compliance. One engagement can often cover both.

### How is a statutory auditor appointed and rotated?

The board appoints the first auditor within 30 days; members appoint at the AGM thereafter. Individuals serve five consecutive years and firms ten, followed by rotation and, in specified cases, a five-year cooling-off period.

### What is the deadline for filing audited financial statements?

Companies must hold the AGM within six months of financial year end, file audited accounts in AOC-4 within 30 days of the AGM and the annual return in MGT-7 within 60 days. Late filings attract additional fees.
