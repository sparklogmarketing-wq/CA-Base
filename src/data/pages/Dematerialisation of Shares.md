> Dematerialisation of shares is the conversion of physical share certificates into electronic holdings with a depository — NSDL or CDSL. For private limited companies, Rule 9B of the Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules, 2014 makes dematerialisation of all securities mandatory, with transfers permitted only in demat form.

## Why Rule 9B Makes Dematerialisation of Shares Mandatory

The Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Second Amendment Rules, 2023 inserted Rule 9B, bringing demat of shares for private limited company purposes squarely into law. Every private company — other than small companies and Nidhi companies — must issue securities only in dematerialised form and facilitate dematerialisation of all its existing securities in accordance with the Depositories Act, 1996. The change ends paper certificates for closely held companies: securities now live only in electronic form, issue-side and transfer-side alike. Unlisted public companies have been under the parallel Rule 9A regime since 2018.

## Rule 9B Compliance Timeline for Private Companies

| Obligation | What changed | When |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh issue of securities — bonus, rights, private placement, ESOPs | Must be issued only in dematerialised form | From the December 2023 amendment |
| Transfer of shares | Physical transfer instruments no longer accepted; only demat transfers | From 1 April 2024 |
| Dematerialisation of all existing securities | Company to complete demat of every class of shares and other securities | By 30 September 2024 |
| New incorporations | Securities issued only in demat from the start | On and from the amendment |

## The NSDL and CDSL Demat Process, Step by Step

Both NSDL and CDSL operate identically for unlisted companies; the practical choice follows the depository participant (DP) your registrar and transfer agent (RTA) is empanelled with.

### Step 1 — Board Resolution and Appointing the RTA

The board appoints an RTA, and the company enters into the required agreements with the RTA and the depository (NSDL or CDSL).

### Step 2 — Obtaining the ISIN

The RTA applies to the depository for an International Securities Identification Number (ISIN) for each class of securities — equity shares, preference shares, ESOP pools and any securities issued.

### Step 3 — Shareholder Onboarding

Every shareholder opens a demat account with any DP, completing PAN-based KYC. Joint holders dematerialise through the same account structure in which the shares are held.

### Step 4 — Demat Requests and Confirmation

Shareholders submit a Demat Request Form with the original share certificates. The company and RTA verify requests against the register of members, confirm them in the depository system, and the physical certificates are defaced and destroyed. Future allotments flow directly into shareholders' demat accounts.

## Documents Required for Demat of Shares

| Shareholder KYC documents | Company-level documents |
|---|---|
| PAN card of each holder | Board resolution authorising the demat process |
| Address proof and photographs | MOA, AOA and current shareholding pattern |
| Client master report from the shareholder's DP | Complete list of securities, holders and certificate numbers |
| Demat Request Form per folio | RTA agreement and depository agreement |
| Original share certificates | Details of lien, pledges or disputes on any shares |

Dematerialisation of shares also surfaces messy records — mismatched names, deceased joint holders, old addresses — which must be regularised before the RTA can confirm requests.

## IEPF Transfers: Unclaimed Shares and How to Claim Them

A related obligation sits under Section 124 of the Companies Act, 2013: where a shareholder has not claimed dividends for seven consecutive financial years, the corresponding shares must be transferred to the demat account of the Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF) Authority, and the company reports the transfer in the prescribed forms. Shareholders and legal heirs can reclaim such shares and unclaimed dividends by filing Form IEPF-5 with the IEPF Authority, along with supporting documents and an indemnity. For families holding legacy unlisted shares, resolving IEPF and transmission issues before dematerialisation avoids duplicating work.

## Consequences of Missing Demat Compliance

Non-compliant companies cannot register transfers of physical shares, locking in shareholders — including exiting founders and ESOP holders — and stalling funding rounds, since investors now routinely verify demat status. Contravention attracts penal consequences under Section 450 and the Depositories Act. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is dematerialisation of shares mandatory for private companies?

Yes. Rule 9B requires every private company, other than small and Nidhi companies, to issue securities only in dematerialised form and to dematerialise all existing securities by the prescribed deadline.

### Which depository should a private company choose - NSDL or CDSL?

Both are equivalent under the Depositories Act, 1996. The choice usually follows the depository participant your registrar and transfer agent works with; compliance obligations are identical.

### Can physical shares still be transferred?

No. After the Rule 9B cut-offs, transfers of private company shares must be executed in dematerialised form; physical transfer instruments are not accepted for registration of transfer.

### What is an ISIN and who obtains it?

An ISIN is the unique 12-character identifier for each class of securities. The company obtains it from NSDL or CDSL through its registrar and transfer agent before accepting demat requests.

### How do I recover shares transferred to IEPF?

The shareholder or legal heir files Form IEPF-5 online to claim shares and unclaimed dividends transferred to the IEPF Authority, supported by documents and an indemnity bond.
