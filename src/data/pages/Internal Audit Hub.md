Section 138 of the Companies Act 2013 requires specified companies to appoint an internal auditor; outsourced internal audit delivers risk-based process reviews, fraud red flags and actionable recommendations for the board.

## Who Must Appoint an Internal Auditor under Section 138?

Section 138 of the Companies Act 2013, read with Rule 13 of the Companies (Accounts) Rules 2014, mandates internal audit for larger companies; crossing any single threshold triggers the obligation:

| Company type | Threshold that triggers internal audit (any one is enough) |
|---|---|
| Listed company | Mandatory for all listed companies, regardless of size |
| Unlisted public company | Paid-up capital Rs 50 crore+; or turnover Rs 200 crore+; or outstanding loans, borrowings, debentures or deposits Rs 100 crore+; or outstanding deposits Rs 25 crore+ |
| Private company | Paid-up capital Rs 50 crore+; or turnover Rs 200 crore+; or outstanding loans or borrowings from banks or public financial institutions Rs 100 crore+ |

The board sets the internal auditor's role and responsibilities; the audit committee, where one exists, monitors the function. Even below these thresholds, investors and lenders increasingly expect the function â€” it is cheaper to build controls than to repair breaches.

## Internal Audit vs Statutory Audit

| Aspect | Internal audit | Statutory audit |
|---|---|---|
| Legal basis | Section 138 and Rule 13, Companies Act 2013 | Sections 129, 139 and 143; LLP Rules for LLPs |
| Objective | Improve controls, processes and compliance for management | Independent opinion that financial statements show a true and fair view |
| Appointment | By the Board | By members at the AGM |
| Reporting | Management and audit committee (Section 177(4)) | Members; filed with ROC via AOC-4 |
| Frequency | Continuous or quarterly risk-based cycle | Annual |

Strong internal audit work feeds directly into a faster, cleaner statutory audit of the same books.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which companies need internal audit under Section 138?

All listed companies; unlisted public and private companies exceeding any one threshold â€” paid-up capital of Rs 50 crore, turnover of Rs 200 crore, outstanding loans or borrowings of Rs 100 crore, or deposits of Rs 25 crore as applicable under Rule 13.

### Can internal audit be outsourced?

Yes. Rule 13 of the Companies (Accounts) Rules 2014 permits appointing a chartered accountant or cost accountant, whether in practice or not. Outsourcing to a firm is the most common model and keeps the function independent.

### Who can be appointed as internal auditor?

A chartered accountant, a cost accountant, an employee or officer of the company, or a firm including an LLP — boards can size the function to their risk profile.

### How often should internal audits be conducted?

No fixed statutory frequency. Best practice is a risk-based annual plan with high-risk areas audited quarterly and stable processes annually, so every significant cycle is covered each year.

### What is the link between internal audit and ICFR?

Internal audit testing evidences the directors' internal financial controls reporting under Section 134(5)(e) and the auditor's opinion under Section 143(3)(i), reducing statutory audit friction and remediation risk.
