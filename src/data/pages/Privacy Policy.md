> This privacy policy explains how {brand}, a chartered accountancy firm in {city}, collects, uses, stores and protects the personal information that clients and website visitors share with us, and sets out the rights available under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and other applicable Indian law.

## Information We Collect

We collect only what is necessary to deliver professional services and run this website: identity and contact details submitted through enquiry forms, email or engagement letters; financial statements, tax records, KYC documents and payroll data shared for assignments; and limited technical data such as browser type and pages visited.

## How We Use Your Information

Your information is used to provide and manage engagements, meet statutory obligations such as know-your-client and anti-money-laundering checks, issue invoices and maintain professional records. Processing rests on your consent, our contractual obligations or a legal requirement — never for unrelated commercial purposes.

## Cookies and Analytics

This website uses essential cookies for core functionality and, where enabled, privacy-respecting analytics reporting aggregate usage. Cookies do not identify you personally; you may control or delete them through browser settings without losing access to site content.

## Sharing and Disclosure

We do not sell or rent personal data. Information is shared only with your consent, with professional advisers and service providers bound by confidentiality obligations, or where disclosure is required by law — for example, filings with the {direct_tax_authority}, the {corporate_affairs_authority} or other regulators.

## Data Security and Retention

Client data is held on access-controlled systems with encryption in transit and at rest. Professional records are retained for the periods prescribed by law — generally not less than eight financial years for company books of account — after which they are securely deleted or destroyed.

## Your Rights Under the DPDP Act 2023

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 recognises you as a Data Principal with rights to access a summary of your data, seek correction or erasure, nominate a representative and withdraw consent, subject to our retention duties as a Data Fiduciary. EU visitors receive equivalent protection under our GDPR-aligned practices. Raise grievances through {contact_email}.

## Changes to This Privacy Policy and Governing Law

This policy is governed by the laws of India, and disputes are subject to the exclusive jurisdiction of the courts at {city}, Karnataka. We may update it for legal or operational changes; the current version always appears on this page.

## Contact Us

For any privacy question, data request or grievance, write to {contact_email}; we respond within a reasonable time. See also our [Terms and Conditions](/terms-and-conditions) for the rules governing use of this website.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What personal data does {brand} collect?

Identity and contact details you submit through forms, email or engagement letters, financial and tax records shared for professional assignments, and limited website usage data collected through cookies.

### Is my personal data shared with third parties?

Only where required by law, such as filings with tax authorities or the MCA, or with service providers bound by confidentiality contracts. We never sell or rent personal data.

### What rights do I have under the DPDP Act 2023?

You may access a summary of your data, seek correction or erasure, nominate a representative, withdraw consent and raise grievances by writing to {contact_email}, subject to statutory retention duties.

### How long are client records retained?

Professional records are kept for statutorily prescribed periods, generally not less than eight financial years for company books of account, after which they are securely deleted or destroyed.
