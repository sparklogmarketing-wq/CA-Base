> Tax dispute resolution services cover the full appeal ladder for income tax demands — first appeals before the JCIT(A) at the National Faceless Appeal Centre, second appeals before the ITAT, and further challenges before the High Court and the Supreme Court — together with revision and settlement alternatives.

## What Do Tax Dispute Resolution Services Cover?

A demand can originate from a processing adjustment under section 143(1), a scrutiny assessment, a penalty order or a section 148 reassessment. Professional tax dispute resolution services begin before the first ground of appeal is drafted: merits review of the assessment order, quantification of the cash locked in pre-deposits, an evaluation of stay options, and a decision on whether to appeal, seek revision or negotiate closure. {brand} handles each stage, including cases where credits were taxed under the [section 68 unexplained credits](/section-68-unexplained-credits) provisions.

## The Income Tax Appeal Ladder Explained

| Stage | Forum | Statutory route | Key limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| First appeal | JCIT(A), decided at NFAC (faceless) | Section 246A | File within 30 days of service; pre-deposit of 10% of assessed tax |
| Second appeal | Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) | Section 253 | File within 60 days; pre-deposit of 20% of tax on assessed income |
| High Court | Jurisdictional High Court | Section 260A | File within 120 days; substantial question of law |
| Supreme Court | Supreme Court | SLP under Article 136 | As per Supreme Court procedure |

The first appeal under section 246A is decided by the Joint Commissioner (Appeals) through the National Faceless Appeal Centre — there is no personal hearing by default, only e-hearings on request. The ITAT is the final fact-finding forum; beyond it, litigation moves to questions of law.

## Pre-Deposit Rules You Must Plan For

Appeal rights are now gated by cash. The proviso to section 246A requires payment of 10 percent of the tax on the assessed income before the first appeal is admitted where a return was furnished; higher or other amounts apply where no return was filed or in search-related cases, as prescribed. The proviso to section 253 requires 20 percent of the tax on the assessed income before the ITAT admits an appeal. Where a stay order from the jurisdictional authority is obtained, the pre-deposit requirement is not attracted. Because refunds can be adjusted against outstanding demands under section 245, pre-deposit strategy and refund management should be planned together — not as afterthoughts.

## First Appeal at NFAC: What Changes in Practice

Faceless first appeals reward the written word. The entire record — assessment order, your replies, evidence annexures and grounds of appeal — is decided on paper unless an e-hearing is granted. A tight grounds-of-appeal structure, cross-referenced to the evidence pack, consistently outperforms verbose briefs.

## Revision, DRP and Settlement Alternatives

Appeals are not the only route. Under section 264, the Principal Commissioner can revise orders prejudicial to the assessee, generally on an application within one year — useful where a finding is factually perverse. In international and specified domestic transaction cases, objections to the draft assessment order go to the Dispute Resolution Panel under section 144C within 30 days. The Dispute Resolution Committee under section 245MA offers structured settlement for specified taxpayers with smaller disputes. The erstwhile Income-tax Settlement Commission was discontinued in 2021, with pending matters transferred to an Interim Board — so any adviser still pitching it as a live option is behind the law.

## Vivad se Vishwas 2020: Lessons From a Concluded Scheme

The Direct Tax Vivad se Vishwas Act, 2020 allowed declarants to close pending appeals by paying the disputed tax plus an additional amount (broadly 100 to 125 percent, higher for search cases). The scheme closed in 2021 and is now a lesson, not an option: taxpayers who kept clean documentation and quantified litigation risk early settled cheaply. No open direct-tax amnesty exists today, though successor schemes have been signalled from time to time. The practical takeaway is to keep your facts scheme-ready — reconciled books, documented loans and consistent returns — while pursuing the ordinary remedies. Cross-border disputes add [transfer pricing](/transfer-pricing) MAP and APA routes to the toolkit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the time limit to file a first income tax appeal?

Thirty days from the date the assessment or penalty order is communicated. The JCIT(A) can condone a delay on reasonable cause, but you should not rely on condonation.

### How much pre-deposit is needed for an ITAT appeal?

The proviso to section 253 requires 20 percent of the tax on the assessed income before the ITAT admits the appeal, against 10 percent of assessed tax at the JCIT(A) stage.

### What was the Vivad se Vishwas scheme?

A concluded 2020 direct-tax settlement scheme under which declarants paid disputed tax plus an additional amount to close appeals. It ended in 2021; current remedies are appeals, revision and DRP.

### Can the PCIT revise an assessment order?

Yes. Under section 264, the Principal Commissioner may revise orders prejudicial to the assessee, generally on an application made within one year of the order sought to be revised.

### Should I appeal or explore settlement options?

It depends on the merits, the cash locked in pre-deposits and recovery prospects. A structured merits memo quantifying each ground usually settles the question quickly.

### What is the DRP in income tax?

The Dispute Resolution Panel under section 144C is a collegium of commissioners that reviews the draft assessment order in international and specified domestic transaction cases, on objections filed within 30 days.
