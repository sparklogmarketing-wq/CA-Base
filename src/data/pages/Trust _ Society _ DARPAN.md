> Trust registration in Karnataka — together with society registration under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 — creates a legal non-profit entity. Registered trusts and societies can obtain 12A and 80G registrations, open institutional bank accounts, receive domestic and foreign grants, and claim income-tax exemptions for genuine charitable activity.

## Trust vs Society vs Section 8 Company: Choosing the Structure

Families, educational institutions and charitable groups choose between three vehicles, based on membership model, funding plans and compliance appetite:

| Feature | Public charitable trust | Society | Section 8 company |
|---|---|---|---|
| Governing law | General law of trusts; Indian Trusts Act, 1882 governs private trusts | Societies Registration Act, 1860 and the Karnataka enactment | Companies Act, 2013 |
| Minimum persons | Author or settlor plus at least two trustees | Seven or more members | Two members; no minimum capital |
| Registered by | Sub-registrar, through a registered trust deed | Registrar of Societies | Registrar of Companies |
| Best suited for | Endowments, scholarship funds, temple and family trusts | Membership-driven associations, alumni and cultural bodies | Structured NGOs seeking institutional funding and credibility |

A Section 8 company trades lighter formation requirements for heavier ongoing [ROC compliance](/roc-compliance).

## How Trust Registration Works in Karnataka

The Indian Trusts Act, 1882 codifies private trusts — family settlements and estate-planning vehicles — while public charitable trusts are formed under general law and relevant state enactments. The essentials are the same either way: a lawful object, a named author or settlor, identifiable trustees and beneficiaries, and trust property. The deed records objects, trustee powers, succession and amendment mode, is stamped at Karnataka rates, and is registered with the sub-registrar for the jurisdiction where the property sits. Banks, donors and the tax department expect a registered deed; thereafter come PAN, TAN and the tax registrations below.

## Society Registration Under the Societies Registration Act, 1860

A society is a membership organisation: seven or more persons subscribe to a memorandum stating the objects and adopt rules covering membership, the general body, the governing council, meetings, audit and dissolution. Registration lies with the Registrar of Societies — under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 or the corresponding Karnataka enactment. Compliance is lighter than a company's but not absent: governing-body changes are filed with the Registrar, with annual accounts and general body resolutions maintained. Where commercial-style operations or grant-heavy funding demand it, a [Section 8 vehicle via the startup and DPIIT route](/startup-registration-dpiit) or a [partnership structure](/partnership-proprietorship-registration) for unrelated commercial activity may sit alongside.

## 12A and 80G Registration: Provisional and Permanent

The income-tax incentives make proper registration worthwhile. Under Section 12AB — which replaced the old 12A regime — a new trust or society first obtains provisional registration in Form 10A, valid for three years, then applies for regular registration in Form 10AB within six months before expiry or of commencing activities. Section 80G follows the same provisional-then-permanent route and benefits donors, who deduct contributions subject to prescribed limits. Trusts must apply at least 85 percent of income to their objects, accumulate the balance under Section 11 conditions, and obtain Form 10B audit where total income exceeds the prescribed threshold. ## FCRA and NGO Darpan: Accessing Domestic and Foreign Grants

Two registrations unlock funding. An NGO Darpan ID — issued on the NITI Aayog portal against PAN and registration details — is required by central ministries before grants are processed, so it is obtained immediately after incorporation. Foreign contributions require FCRA registration under the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010, or prior permission for newer organisations; registration ordinarily needs three years of existence and demonstrated activity. FCRA funds flow only through the designated account, renewal falls due every five years, and annual returns in Form FC-4 are mandatory. Violations can cost the registration itself, so treat FCRA as board-level compliance.

## Ongoing Compliance After Registration

Registration is the start, not the end. Each year brings books of account, TDS, professional tax for staff in Karnataka, GST where activities are not exempt, the annual ITR-7, audit forms where applicable, Registrar filings for societies and the full RoC calendar for Section 8 companies. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many people are required to start a trust?

A trust is created by an author or settlor with at least two trustees in the usual case; a society needs a minimum of seven members. Stamp duty and registration requirements differ.

### Is annual filing required for trusts and societies?

Yes. Trusts and societies file annual income-tax returns, typically ITR-7, with Form 10B audits where income exceeds prescribed limits; societies also file governing-body details with the Registrar.

### Can trusts and societies get income-tax exemption?

Yes. Registration under Section 12AB exempts income applied toward charitable or religious purposes under Sections 11 and 12, subject to applying at least 85 percent of income and prescribed audits and filings.

### What is the difference between 12A and 80G?

12A, now granted under Section 12AB, exempts the NGO's own income; 80G gives donors a deduction on contributions made to the NGO. Both follow a provisional-then-permanent registration route.

### What is NGO Darpan and why does it matter?

NGO Darpan is a NITI Aayog portal that issues a unique ID used by central ministries to process grants. Most government funding is unavailable to an NGO without it.

### Can an NGO receive foreign donations?

Only after obtaining FCRA registration or prior permission from the Ministry of Home Affairs, receiving funds through the designated bank account, and filing annual FC-4 returns.
