## Old vs New Tax Regime: Choosing With Numbers, Not Instinct

The regime decision is the single largest lever in personal taxation, and it should be revisited every year.

| Feature | Old regime | New regime (default) |
|---|---|---|
| Slab structure | Higher early bands | Revised, lower rates from FY 2025-26 |
| Standard deduction | Rs 50,000 | Rs 75,000 |
| 80C, 80D, HRA, home-loan interest | Allowed | Largely withdrawn (80CCD(2) employer NPS continues) |
| Section 87A rebate | Up to Rs 12,500 (income up to Rs 5 lakh) | Up to Rs 60,000 (income up to Rs 12 lakh) |
| Suits | Deduction-heavy profiles | Simple, low-deduction profiles |

Salaried taxpayers without business income can switch between regimes each year at the time of filing, so the choice should follow the numbers, not habit.

## Section 87A Rebate: How Far Does It Stretch?

Under the new regime for FY 2025-26, the section 87A rebate makes total income up to Rs 12 lakh effectively tax-free (a rebate of up to Rs 60,000), with marginal relief smoothing the jump just above the threshold. The old regime continues to allow a rebate of Rs 12,500 where total income does not exceed Rs 5 lakh. Note that income taxed at special rates — such as short-term capital gains on listed equity — does not qualify for the rebate, which matters for investors planning redemption timing.

## Deductions That Still Move the Needle

Where the old regime applies, or for corporate benefits that survive under the new regime:

- Section 80C up to Rs 1.5 lakh — EPF, PPF, ELSS, life insurance, home-loan principal, tuition fees
- Section 80D — health insurance premiums for self and family, with higher limits for senior citizens
- Section 80CCD(1B) — an additional Rs 50,000 for NPS contributions (old regime)
- Section 80CCD(2) — employer NPS contributions, allowed in both regimes (14% of salary in the new regime)
- Section 24(b) — home-loan interest up to Rs 2 lakh (old regime)

Our advice runs the other way too: stop over-investing in locked products merely to save tax, especially when the new regime makes those instruments redundant.

## Capital Gains: Rates, Timing and Exemptions

Post the July 2024 amendments, long-term gains on listed equity and equity mutual funds are taxed at 12.5% beyond a Rs 1.25 lakh annual exemption, with short-term equity gains at 20%. Other long-term assets, including immovable property, generally attract 12.5% without indexation, with a transitional option for resident individuals on property acquired before 23 July 2024. Planning levers include harvesting gains within the Rs 1.25 lakh exempt band, staggering redemptions across financial years, and rollover exemptions — see our detailed [capital gains guide on sections 54 and 54F](/capital-gains-section-54-54f), which covers the Rs 10 crore exemption cap and qualifying timelines.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which tax regime is better for salaried employees?

It depends on your deduction profile. The new regime offers lower slabs, a Rs 75,000 standard deduction and an 87A rebate up to Rs 60,000, while the old regime rewards home loans, HRA and 80C/80D investments. We model both against your actual numbers.

### What is the Section 87A rebate limit?

Under the new regime, total income up to Rs 12 lakh is effectively tax-free through a rebate of up to Rs 60,000 (FY 2025-26), with marginal relief just above. The old regime allows a rebate of Rs 12,500 for incomes up to Rs 5 lakh.

### Are 80C and 80D deductions available under the new regime?

Generally no. The new regime withdraws 80C, 80D and most chapter VI-A deductions, though employer NPS contributions under 80CCD(2) and the standard deduction continue. Weigh this before locking long-term products.

### What are the current capital gains tax rates?

Listed equity gains held over 12 months are taxed at 12.5% beyond a Rs 1.25 lakh annual exemption; short-term equity gains at 20%. Other long-term assets generally attract 12.5% without indexation, with transitional options for pre-July 2024 property.

### Do you advise HNIs and business families?

Yes. We structure HUF and family trust holdings, plan gifting and capital gains harvesting, review large receipts against section 68 exposure and coordinate cross-border positions for NRI family members.
