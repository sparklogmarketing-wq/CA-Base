> An unexplained cash credit under section 68 arises when a sum is found credited in the books of an assessee and the assessee offers no satisfactory explanation about its nature and source. The explanation must establish the creditor's identity, creditworthiness and the genuineness of the transaction; failing which the credit is taxed at a punitive rate.

## What Is an Unexplained Cash Credit Under Section 68?

An unexplained cash credit section 68 case usually begins with a data pick-up: a loan received in cash, an entry routed through layered accounts, or an investor whose own returns show no capacity to fund the subscription. Once a sum is credited in the books, the burden of explaining its nature and source rests on the assessee. If the explanation fails, the credit is deemed income of the previous year in which it was credited — and it travels with the harshest effective tax rate in the Act.

## The Sections 68 to 69D Family: What Each Targets

| Section | Deemed income trigger |
|---|---|
| 68 | Cash credits in books unexplained as to nature and source |
| 68A | Credits explained as exempt income without satisfactory proof |
| 69 | Unexplained investments not recorded in the books |
| 69A | Unexplained money, bullion, jewellery or valuable articles |
| 69B | Expenditure understated or unrecorded |
| 69C | Expenditure not explainable as wholly and exclusively for business |
| 69D | Borrowings or hocking not recorded in the books |

The family works as a net: where section 68 does not catch an entry, its siblings usually will. Section 68A matters where the taxpayer pleads agricultural income or other exemptions as the source and cannot prove them.

## The Three-Prong Test: Identity, Creditworthiness, Genuineness

Settled case law — the principle affirmed in the Nemi Chand Kothari litigation — requires the taxpayer to establish all three links: who the creditor is, whether the creditor had the financial capacity to advance the money, and whether the transaction itself is genuine. Identity without capacity fails; capacity without a banking trail fails. A defensible loan file contains the creditor's PAN, income-tax returns and bank statements, a written loan agreement or board resolution, movement of funds through account-payee channels, and matching entries of interest or repayment in both sets of books. Cash-lending claims and accommodations routed through entry operators fail the test almost uniformly — courts look at the creditor's own records, not just confirmations.

## The Punitive Rate: Section 115BBE

Section 115BBE taxes the credit at a flat 60 percent, attracts a 25 percent surcharge, and adds cess — roughly a 78 percent effective outflow. No expense is deductible against the deemed income, and set-off of losses is restricted to prescribed depreciation. The rate design is deliberate deterrence: the department prices the risk of accommodation entries far above any penalty logic. A taxpayer who anticipated a section 68 query at the [tax audit](/tax-audit) stage — with creditors' confirmations banked — rarely ends up paying it.

## Section 69A and Unexplained Money

Section 69A deems income any money, bullion, jewellery or valuable article not recorded in the books, where the owner offers no satisfactory explanation about its nature and source. It reaches beyond accounting entries: cash found in a search or unrecorded jewellery lying outside the books are classic triggers. Its companions — sections 69, 69B, 69C and 69D — cover unrecorded investments, understated expenditure, non-business-justified spending and off-book borrowings respectively.

## Benami Transactions: The Parallel Penal Regime

The Benami Transactions (Prohibition) Act, 1988, as rewritten in 2016, prohibits holding property in a name other than the real beneficial owner. Where a transaction is held benami, the authority can provisionally attach and then confiscate the property, levy a penalty of 25 percent of its fair market value, and launch prosecution carrying rigorous imprisonment of one to seven years plus fine. Genuine arrangements are protected: property held in the name of a spouse or child from the individual's own known sources, or held in a fiduciary capacity such as a trustee or partner, falls outside the prohibition. Benami proceedings are separate from income-tax additions — a single layered transaction can attract both the section 68 inclusion and confiscation exposure.

Prevention here is a filing discipline, not litigation heroics. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What three tests apply to an unexplained cash credit?

The assessee must establish the identity of the creditor, the creditor's capacity to lend (creditworthiness) and the genuineness of the transaction. All three must be proved; a weak link lets the credit be taxed.

### What is the tax rate on unexplained cash credit?

Section 115BBE taxes it at 60 percent plus a 25 percent surcharge and cess — roughly 78 percent effective — with no deduction of expenses and strict limits on setting off losses.

### Can section 68 apply to share premium received?

Yes. For closely held companies, the explanation to section 68 extends to share capital, share premium and share application money where the company fails to explain the source of the investor's funds.

### Which documents prove loan genuineness?

Creditor PAN, ITRs and bank statements, a written loan agreement or board resolution, confirmed banking trail through account payee channels and timely interest or repayment entries in the books.

### How is benami different from a section 68 addition?

Section 68 is an income-tax inclusion with a punitive rate. The Benami Act is a separate penal statute that can confiscate the property itself, impose a 25 percent penalty and lead to imprisonment.

### Can gifts received be taxed as unexplained credits?

Gifts from unrelated persons above Rs 50,000 in value fall under section 56(2)(x), a different provision. Section 68 bites where a sum sits credited in books with no explanation of nature or source.
