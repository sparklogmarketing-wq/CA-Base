> A GST refund is the repayment of tax collected without authority or of unutilised input tax credit — arising from inverted duty structures, zero-rated exports of goods or services, excess tax payment or finalisation of assessment — claimed through Form RFD-01 within two years of the relevant date, with interest payable by the department if sanction exceeds sixty days.

Every GST refund is working capital stuck with the exchequer. Exporters waiting on [refund of IGST paid on exports](/gst-exports), manufacturers absorbing an [inverted duty structure refund](/gst-itc-rcm) mismatch, and businesses with plain excess payment all lose cash for as long as the claim sits unprocessed. {brand} prepares, files and follows GST refund claims through to credit in your ledger.

## When Can You Claim a GST Refund?

Section 54 of the CGST Act, 2017 permits refunds in defined situations, and the two-year window runs from a category-specific relevant date:

| Situation | Typical relevant date (start of the 2-year window) |
|---|---|
| Export of goods by sea or air (with or without IGST payment) | Date the ship or aircraft leaves India |
| Export of services with payment of tax | Date of receipt of convertible foreign exchange (date of invoice where payment is received in advance) |
| Zero-rated supply without payment of tax under LUT | Date of invoice, as applicable to the supply |
| Finalisation of provisional assessment | Date of the final assessment order |
| Order of court or tribunal | Date of the order |

Where several situations apply, the law generally requires the refund of tax or credit to be pursued through the route that first became available — sequencing matters as much as eligibility.

## Export Refunds: The IGST Route vs the LUT Route

Exporters choose between two mechanics. Under the IGST route, IGST is paid on the shipping bill and the refund is auto-transmitted to the portal from customs systems; claims still fail on data mismatches between shipping bills, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B. Under the LUT route, exports are made without payment of tax on a Letter of Undertaking and the refund is claimed against accumulated input tax credit, including an [export of services refund](/gst-exports) for cross-border service providers. Either way, 90 percent of a zero-rated claim is payable provisionally within seven days of acknowledgement, and full documentation — FIRCs, bank realisation certificates, export invoices — determines how quickly the balance follows.

## Inverted Duty Structure Refunds

Where the rate of tax on inputs exceeds the rate on outputs, unutilised credit accumulates structurally. The September 2025 rate rationalisation corrected several inversions, but the reorganisation also created new mismatches in specific sectors, so inverted duty claims remain a live working-capital issue. Rule 89 caps the refundable amount through a formula based on turnover and net ITC, and the claim must be supported by invoice-level computations — an area where approximation costs real money. ## How to Claim a GST Refund: The RFD-01 Process and Timelines

1. **RFD-01:** File the claim online with self-certification and supporting documents — invoices, shipping bills or FIRCs, LUT details, and the rule 89 computation where applicable.
2. **RFD-02:** Acknowledgement is issued; the sixty-day sanction clock runs from this date.
3. **Provisional refund:** For zero-rated supplies, 90 percent is ordered within seven days of acknowledgement where eligible.
4. **Processing:** The officer verifies documents; a notice in FORM RFD-08 precedes any partial or full rejection, and you have the right to reply and be heard.
5. **Sanction:** FORM RFD-05 orders payment; the credit reflects in your cash ledger, and recrediting of rejected amounts to the credit ledger happens through FORM RFD-06.

If sanction crosses sixty days from RFD-02, interest at 6 percent accrues — a useful lever when following up. Note that refunds can be withheld or adjusted against outstanding demands, so open litigation history is reviewed before filing.

## Why Refund Claims Get Rejected or Delayed

- Invoices missing from GSTR-2B, breaking the credit trail behind the claim.
- Export data mismatches among shipping bill, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B.
- Missing FIRC or bank realisation entries for services exported in foreign currency.
- Turnover or formula errors in inverted duty computations.
- Filing close to the GST refund time limit, leaving no room to cure a deficiency memo under RFD-03.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the time limit to claim a GST refund?

Two years from the relevant date, which differs by category — for example, the date the vessel leaves India for exported goods or the date of foreign exchange receipt for exported services. Missing the relevant date extinguishes the claim.

### How long does the department take to sanction a GST refund?

Sixty days from the RFD-02 acknowledgement. If sanction is delayed beyond sixty days, interest at 6 percent accrues on the refund amount under section 56 — we track this and pursue delayed credits.

### What is provisional refund under GST?

For claims arising from zero-rated supplies of goods or services, 90 percent of the claimed amount is provisionally paid within seven days of acknowledgement, with the balance released after verification — provided there is no fraud history.

### Can I claim refund of unutilised ITC due to inverted duty structure?

Yes, under section 54(3) where input tax rates exceed the output rate. Rule 89 caps the refund through a formula tied to turnover and net ITC, so the computation must be done precisely.

### Why was my refund application marked deficient?

A deficiency memo in FORM RFD-03 issues when documents, declarations or export particulars are incomplete. The claim is treated as never filed and must be refiled — losing time against the two-year limit if you are close to it.
