## Udyam Classification: Reading the Revised Limits

Udyam classification rests on two dials — investment in plant and machinery or equipment, and annual turnover — and both dials were moved upward in the most recent revision, effective April 2025, with export turnover excluded from the turnover test:

| Category | Investment ceiling | Annual turnover ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | Rs 2.5 crore | Rs 10 crore |
| Small | Rs 25 crore | Rs 50 crore |
| Medium | Rs 125 crore | Rs 250 crore |

The direction is deliberate: keep growing firms inside the MSME tent longer, and stop export success from stripping benefits. Practical consequences follow the tag — payment protection and interest on delayed payment under the MSMED Act for micro and small units, priority-sector credit for lenders, and eligibility gates for subsidy and procurement programmes. Businesses should re-test their category after each audited year and file updates on the Udyam portal rather than discovering a stale classification mid-scheme.

## Section 43B(h): Payment Discipline Comes to Large Buyers

Clause (h) inserted in section 43B changed the arithmetic of paying micro and small suppliers late. Where a buyer on regular books owes a micro or small enterprise, the expenditure is allowable only on actual payment if settlement slips beyond the credit period permitted under the MSMED Act — capped at 45 days even where a longer term is agreed. The result: accrued-but-unpaid MSME payables are pushed out of the profit and loss deduction until the year of payment, raising the effective cost of stretching working capital. Larger counterparties have responded by shortening MSME payment runs and reviewing procurement terms; MSME suppliers, for their part, should register on Udyam and document credit periods in writing to anchor the protection. Assessees under the presumptive scheme are outside clause (h), so the rule lands squarely on regular-book buyers — precisely the segment our [presumptive and corporate tax planning](/presumptive-corporate-tax) work benchmarks.

## DPIIT Recognition: What the Benefits Actually Are

DPIIT recognition converts a young company into a recognised startup with a defined benefit stack: the income-tax holiday under section 80-IAC for eligible recognised startups, subject to its incorporation-window and certification conditions; self-certification for specified labour and environment compliances; relaxations in public procurement on prior experience and earnest-money requirements; and access to the {funding_program} for prototype-to-market funding. None of these apply automatically — recognition is granted on the entity's profile, and the 80-IAC claim in particular turns on incorporation dates and turnover gates, so the application should be planned rather than rushed. ## Credit Access and Presumptive Taxation Choices

Formal credit has widened along two tracks. First, guarantee-backed lending: collateral-free credit under CGTMSE has carried progressively higher eligible ceilings, making guarantee cover a genuine substitute for security deposits in small business lending, while the pandemic-era ECLGS window has since run its course. Second, tax computation itself: presumptive taxation under section 44AD — up to Rs 3 crore where digital receipts and payments are at least 95 percent, else Rs 2 crore — and section 44ADA for specified professionals at Rs 75 lakh (Rs 50 lakh below the digital threshold) trade book-keeping burden for a deemed income floor. The choice is strategic: presumptive suits lean operations with thin expense bases; regular books suit loss-making growth phases, high-depreciation asset stacks and businesses that need audited statements for credit. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the current Udyam classification limits?

After the most recent revision, effective from April 2025: micro units up to Rs 2.5 crore investment and Rs 10 crore turnover; small up to Rs 25 crore and Rs 50 crore; medium up to Rs 125 crore and Rs 250 crore. Export turnover is excluded from the turnover test.

### How does section 43B(h) affect payments to MSMEs?

Buyers deducting expenditure owed to micro and small suppliers can claim it only on actual payment if payment slips beyond the MSMED timelines, capped at 45 days. Accrued but unpaid balances face disallowance until settled.

### Does section 43B(h) apply to presumptive taxpayers?

No. Assessees opting for presumptive taxation under sections 44AD and 44ADA are outside the mischief of clause (h). It bites on regular-book buyers, which is where payment-run discipline and supplier terms need review.

### What are the benefits of DPIIT recognition?

Recognised startups access the section 80-IAC tax holiday subject to its incorporation-window conditions, self-certification under labour and environment laws, procurement relaxations, and eligibility for Startup India Seed Fund Scheme support.

### Is collateral-free credit genuinely available to MSMEs?

Yes, through guarantee-cover schemes such as CGTMSE, whose eligible ceiling has been enhanced over time. The pandemic-era ECLGS window has since run its course, so lenders now weigh Udyam status and cash flows more directly.

### When should a small business exit presumptive taxation?

Consider regular books when turnover nears the presumptive ceiling, losses or heavy depreciation make regular computation favourable, working-capital cycles need interest deductibility, or lenders and investors require audited statements.
