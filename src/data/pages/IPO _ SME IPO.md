For smaller companies, an SME IPO on NSE Emerge or BSE SME offers a public listing at a scale the mainboard does not.

## Mainboard or SME Platform: Choosing the Listing Track

SEBI ICDR Regulations route issuers by post-issue capital and readiness:

| Parameter | Mainboard | SME platform (NSE Emerge / BSE SME) |
|---|---|---|
| Post-issue face value capital | Above Rs 25 crore | Up to Rs 25 crore (option up to Rs 100 crore where criteria permit) |
| Profitability test | Rule 19(2)(b) SCRR tracks, or 75% QIB book-building route | Operating profit of at least Rs 1 crore in at least two of the preceding three years |
| Track record | Tested through the eligibility tracks | At least three years |
| Underwriting and market making | Standard merchant banker obligations | 100% underwriting and mandatory market making for three years |
| Promoter lock-in | 20% contribution for 3 years; excess for 18 months | 20% contribution for 3 years; lighter lock-in on the excess |
| Non-promoter pre-issue lock-in | 6 months | 1 year |
| Disclosure and governance | Full ICDR and LODR regime | Condensed prospectus and relaxed LODR norms |

The SME track is a genuine listing, with migration to the mainboard available once mainboard eligibility and exchange-specified conditions are met.

## Eligibility Criteria: The Profitability Tracks

Mainboard issuers must satisfy at least one of the Rule 19(2)(b) tracks: net tangible assets of at least Rs 3 crore in each of the preceding three full years, average operating profit of at least Rs 15 crore across those three years with profit in each, or net worth of at least Rs 1 crore in each year. Issuers meeting none can still list if 75% of the book-built issue is mandatorily allocated to qualified institutional buyers. SME issuers work to the platform criteria summarised above — the three-year track record and the operating profit threshold. Test eligibility early: financial-year gaps are fixed by audited years, not drafting.

## The DRHP Process: A Realistic Timeline

- **Readiness (several months)** — restated three-year financials, secretarial clean-up, [ROC compliance services](/roc-compliance) history verified, related-party transactions documented, share capital reconciled and shares dematerialised through [dematerialisation of shares](/dematerialisation-shares).
- **Draft red herring prospectus (DRHP)** — prepared with the merchant banker and filed with SEBI; observations are typically issued within about 30 days of a compliant draft, with responses and re-filings in between. The Companies Act side runs in parallel: the prospectus is filed with the Registrar in LEAP-1, the information memorandum in PAS-2, and PAS-3 returns record each allotment.
- **RHP and price band** — filed and marketed before the issue opens.
- **Book building and anchor book** — the issue runs for the prescribed working days, anchors allocated one day before opening.
- **Allotment and listing** — credits and trading begin on the compressed T+3 timeline after the issue closes.

From board decision to listing, six to twelve months is realistic, driven mostly by how clean the books and filings already are.

## Life After Listing: Ongoing Obligations

A listing changes the compliance calendar permanently. Quarterly and annual results, governance structures such as the audit committee, shareholding and pledge disclosures, related-party approvals and LODR exchange filings all become recurring deadlines. Design these processes before listing, which is why our [finance controller and MIS reporting](/finance-controller-mis) team takes over the reporting calendar on listing day. ## Do IPO Grades Still Matter?

SEBI's mandatory IPO grading regime — credit rating agencies scoring issues one to five — was withdrawn in 2014; no modern offer document carries an IPO grade. What still matters is a voluntary credit rating, which supports lender negotiations and institutional confidence during and after the issue.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between a mainboard and an SME IPO?

An SME IPO on NSE Emerge or BSE SME suits companies with post-issue face value capital up to Rs 25 crore, with lighter disclosure and governance norms. The mainboard suits larger issues, with stricter eligibility tracks, wider investor participation and heavier ongoing compliance.

### What are the minimum criteria for an SME IPO?

Post-issue face value capital up to Rs 25 crore, a track record of at least three years, and operating profit of at least Rs 1 crore in at least two of the preceding three financial years under the tightened SEBI framework, along with the exchange-specified net worth and documentation requirements.

### How long does the DRHP-to-listing process take?

Preparation — restated financials, due diligence and prospectus drafting — usually takes several months. SEBI then issues observations, typically within about 30 days of a satisfactory draft, after which the price band, book building, allotment and listing follow within weeks.

### What is locked in after an IPO?

The minimum promoter contribution of 20 per cent of post-issue capital is locked in for three years. Excess promoter shareholding is locked in for 18 months on the mainboard, and non-promoter pre-issue shareholders face a six-month lock-in on the mainboard and one year on SME platforms.

### Is IPO grading still mandatory in India?

No. SEBI withdrew the mandatory IPO grading regime in 2014. Voluntary credit ratings from registered agencies still help with lender negotiations and institutional credibility, but the old one-to-five IPO grade is no longer part of the offer document.
