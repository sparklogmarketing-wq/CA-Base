> Corporate restructuring services help businesses reorganise ownership, entities and operations — through mergers, demergers, slump sales, share transfers or NCLT-sanctioned schemes — with tax efficiency and statutory compliance built in. ## Share Purchase, Slump Sale or Demerger: Comparing the Routes

The first structural decision is what actually changes hands, because the Income-tax Act 1961 treats each route very differently:

| Route | What transfers | Tax treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Share purchase | Shares of the target company; the business stays inside the entity | Capital gains under Section 45 for selling shareholders; the company's losses and approvals stay intact, subject to change-in-ownership conditions |
| Slump sale (Sections 2(42C), 50B) | An undertaking as a going concern for a lump-sum consideration | Capital gains computed as consideration minus net worth; no allocation to individual assets |
| Demerger (Section 2(19AA)) | A specified undertaking moves to a resulting company | Tax-neutral for the demerged company under Section 47(viid) where conditions are met, with corresponding neutrality for shareholders and the resulting company |
| Amalgamation | The whole undertaking merges into a transferee company | Neutral under Section 47(vi) where the Section 2(1B) amalgamation conditions are satisfied |

A demerger earns neutrality only on strict conditions: all property and liabilities relatable to the undertaking transfer, consideration is by shares issued to at least three-fourths in value of the demerged company's shareholders, and the undertaking remains capable of separate operation after the split. Where neutrality fails, the transfer is taxed like any other disposal — which is why the structure is tested against the statute before drafting begins, never after.

## The NCLT Scheme Route: Sections 230 to 232

Composite arrangements — merger, demerger, creditor restructuring or capital reduction involving multiple classes of stakeholders — generally proceed as a scheme of compromise or arrangement under Sections 230 to 232 of the Companies Act 2013, sanctioned by the {company_law_tribunal}. The sequence is a board-approved scheme, registered valuer's report, meetings of creditors and shareholders, objections from regulators, and a final sanction order followed by filings with {corporate_affairs_authority}. Eligible small companies and holding-subsidiary combinations can use the fast track merger under Section 233, sanctioned by the Regional Director without a tribunal application — quicker, but only where the eligibility conditions and objection windows are cleanly met.

## Accounting for Amalgamation: Ind AS 103 vs Pooling of Interests

Post-merger accounting shapes reported results for years. Under Ind AS 103, the acquirer applies the acquisition method: identifiable assets and liabilities are recognised at fair value, goodwill or a bargain purchase gain is recorded, and results consolidate from the acquisition date. The legacy pooling-of-interests approach — assets at book value, no goodwill, comparative restatement — survives only for amalgamations in the nature of merger under the Indian GAAP framework for entities not applying Ind AS. The choice is not free: fair-value uplifts create fresh depreciation and deferred tax positions, which feed straight into future [tax audit](/tax-audit) and reporting work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between a slump sale and a demerger?

A slump sale transfers an undertaking as a going concern for a lump sum, triggering capital gains under Section 50B on the seller. A demerger under Section 2(19AA) splits the undertaking into a resulting company and can be tax-neutral under Section 47(viid) if the conditions are met.

### What is a fast-track merger under Section 233?

Section 233 lets eligible small companies, and holding companies merging wholly-owned subsidiaries, complete a merger through the Regional Director without an NCLT application. It is faster and cheaper, but eligibility conditions and objection windows apply strictly.

### Do we need a valuation report for an NCLT scheme?

Yes. A scheme of arrangement involving share exchange must be supported by a valuation report from a registered valuer, and the scheme must be fair and reasonable from the perspective of shareholders, creditors and public shareholders where applicable.

### How long does an NCLT merger typically take?

Realistically many months to over a year, depending on tribunal workload, creditor and shareholder objections, and regulatory approvals. Meeting-notice drafting, filings and affidavit quality materially affect how smoothly the scheme moves through.

### When is a slump sale better than selling assets individually?

When the undertaking is sold as a going concern for a lump sum, Section 50B taxes gains on net worth — avoiding section 50C stamp-value uplift on immovable property and depreciation recapture complications that arise when assets are sold one by one.

### Which MCA approvals feature in a restructuring?

Fast-track mergers under Section 233 are approved by the Regional Director through Form RD-1, while NCLT schemes proceed by petition with valuations and affidavits. Supporting filings include INC-23 for an inter-state registered office shift, INC-27 for converting between company classes, INC-18 and INC-12 for section 8 conversions and licences, INC-24 for name change approvals, and Form ADJ appeals against orders of the Registrar or the tribunal.
