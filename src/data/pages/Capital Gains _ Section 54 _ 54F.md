> The section 54 capital gains exemption lets resident taxpayers shelter long-term capital gains by reinvesting sale proceeds: section 54 covers gains on selling a residential house, section 54F covers gains on other long-term assets, and both route the money into a new residential house within strict statutory windows, capped at Rs 10 crore.

## What Is the Section 54 Capital Gains Exemption?

Section 54 applies to an individual or HUF that sells a residential house held as a long-term capital asset and reinvests the gain in another residential house in India. The new house must be purchased within one year before or two years after the date of transfer, or constructed within three years. Only one new house qualifies, though a taxpayer can claim two houses where the capital gain does not exceed Rs 2 crore — an option available once in a lifetime. The Finance Act caps the exemption at Rs 10 crore of capital gain, and the new house cannot be transferred for three years or the exemption is clawed back.

## Section 54F vs 54EC vs 54GB: How They Compare

| Aspect | Section 54 | Section 54F | Section 54EC | Section 54GB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who can claim | Individual or HUF | Individual or HUF | Any taxpayer | Individual or HUF |
| Original asset sold | Residential house | Any long-term asset except a residential house | Any long-term capital asset | Residential house |
| Reinvestment | New residential house | New residential house | Notified bonds (NHAI, REC, PFC, IRFC) | Equity of an eligible startup |
| Investment window | 1 year before / 2 years after; construction 3 years | Same as section 54 | 6 months from transfer | 1 year before / 2 years after transfer |
| Cap | Rs 10 crore | Rs 10 crore | Rs 50 lakh | Rs 10 crore |
| Lock-in | 3 years | 3 years | 5 years | 5 years |

Section 54F has a critical detail: the exemption is computed on the *net consideration* reinvested, not the gain — invest part and the exemption is proportionate. It also requires that you do not own more than one residential house on the transfer date. Section 54GB requires the startup to be an eligible business as defined for the section 80-IAC holiday, with the seller holding more than 50 percent of its share capital; our [startup registration and DPIIT recognition](/startup-registration-dpiit) page explains that qualification.

## Holding Periods: When Does an Asset Become Long-Term?

| Asset | Long-term if held for at least |
|---|---|
| Listed shares and equity mutual fund units | 12 months |
| Immovable property (land or building) | 24 months |
| Unlisted shares | 24 months |
| Debt funds, gold, jewellery and other assets | 36 months |

These thresholds decide whether the concessional long-term regime — and the exemptions above — apply at all.

## LTCG Rates After the July 2024 Reforms

For transfers on or after 23 July 2024, the LTCG rate is 12.5 percent without indexation across asset classes, with listed-equity gains enjoying an annual exemption of Rs 1.25 lakh before the rate applies. Short-term gains on listed equity are taxed at 20 percent. For land or buildings acquired before 23 July 2024, resident individuals and HUFs may pay the lower of 20 percent with indexation or 12.5 percent without it — a calculation worth running both ways before signing a sale deed. ## The Capital Gains Account Scheme: Buying Time Legally

Exemption does not wait for the right property. Any unutilised capital gain must be deposited before the due date for filing the return under section 139(1) in a Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS) account — Type A (savings) or Type B (term deposit) — and withdrawn only for the qualifying investment. Money left idle beyond the prescribed construction or purchase period becomes taxable. CGAS discipline is where most self-prepared claims fail; our [income tax services](/income-tax) team reviews the full chain of title-to-reinvestment before the [ITR](/itr-filing) is filed.

## Common Mistakes That Void the Exemption

The recurring failures are predictable: buying the new house in a family member's name instead of the seller's; ignoring the Rs 10 crore cap while claiming the full gain; missing the CGAS deposit deadline; selling the new asset within the lock-in; and claiming sections 54 and 54F on the same asset. NRIs face an added layer of repatriation and withholding rules covered in our [NRI taxation](/nri-taxation) services. Each of these is avoidable with transaction structuring done at signing, not at filing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I claim both section 54 and section 54EC together?

Yes. A gain from selling a residential house can be routed partly into a new house under section 54 and partly into notified bonds under section 54EC, within each section's own cap and window.

### What happens if I miss the capital gains account scheme deposit?

If the unutilised gain is not deposited in a CGAS account before the ITR due date under section 139(1), the exemption fails and the gain is taxed in the year of transfer.

### Does the Rs 10 crore cap apply per sale?

No. It is an aggregate ceiling on deductions under sections 54, 54B, 54D, 54EC (partly), 54F and 54GB for the relevant assessment year, across all qualifying claims.

### Is indexation still available on property sales?

For transfers on or after 23 July 2024, LTCG is 12.5 percent without indexation. For land or buildings acquired before that date, resident individuals and HUFs may pay the lower of 20 percent with indexation or 12.5 percent without.

### Can I claim 54F if I already own two houses?

No. Section 54F requires that you do not own more than one residential house on the date of transfer of the original asset, apart from other reinvestment conditions.

### What is the deadline to invest in 54EC bonds?

Six months from the date of transfer, subject to the Rs 50 lakh aggregate limit. The bonds carry a five-year lock-in, and selling them earlier revives the exemption.
