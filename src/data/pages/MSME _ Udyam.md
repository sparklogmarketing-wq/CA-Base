## Revised MSME Classification: Investment and Turnover Limits

Classification drives every benefit. Following the 2025 revision, the same criteria apply to manufacturing and service enterprises:

| Category | Investment in plant, machinery or equipment | Annual turnover |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | Up to Rs 2.5 crore | Up to Rs 10 crore |
| Small | Up to Rs 25 crore | Up to Rs 100 crore |
| Medium | Up to Rs 125 crore | Up to Rs 500 crore |

Both limits must be satisfied within the category, and micro and small status is what activates the payment-protection regime described below.

## What Udyam Registration Unlocks

- **Credit access** — priority-sector treatment for bank lending, collateral-free cover under CGTMSE schemes, and discounting of supplier bills through TReDS platforms.
- **Government procurement** — a prescribed share of central government purchases is reserved for micro and small enterprises, with exemptions from earnest-money and turnover conditions in many tenders.
- **Payment protection** — buyers must pay micro and small suppliers within fifteen days, or up to forty-five days where agreed in writing.
- **Cost and tax reliefs** — interest subvention and state-level subsidies where notified, and easier [GST registration](/gst-registration) and compliance positioning for smaller enterprises.

For a proprietorship, Udyam is usually the first formal identity document — see how we pair it with [partnership firm and proprietorship registration](/partnership-proprietorship-registration).

## The 45-Day Rule: Section 43B(h) and MSME Samadhaan

The payment-protection regime bites buyers, and every MSME should understand both sides:

- **Section 43B(h)** denies the buyer a deduction for purchases from micro and small suppliers unless payment is made within the MSMED timelines — fifteen days, or forty-five days by agreement. Payment timing has become a direct tax cost.
- **MSME Form 1** requires specified companies and LLPs to file a half-yearly return of amounts pending with micro and small suppliers beyond forty-five days.
- **MSME Samadhaan** is the online window where suppliers apply to the MSE Facilitation Council for recovery, with compound interest at three times the notified bank rate on delayed amounts.

## How MSME Udyam Registration Works

The process is online and requires no uploads:

1. Aadhaar and PAN validation of the proprietor, partner or director.
2. Enterprise details — name, location, bank account, and NIC activity codes.
3. Self-declared investment and turnover figures, pulled from ITR and GST records where linked.
4. Instant e-certificate with a permanent Udyam Registration Number.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Udyam registration free?

Yes. Registration on the official government portal is free and needs no document uploads — only the PAN, Aadhaar OTP and basic business details. Fees arise only when a professional manages classification, updates and downstream compliance for you.

### Can one PAN hold multiple Udyam registrations?

No. Only one Udyam registration is permitted per PAN, and it must cover all activities of the enterprise, manufacturing as well as services. Updating details as turnover grows is done through the same registration.

### What happens if a buyer delays payment to a micro supplier?

The buyer loses the deduction u/s 43B(h) until payment is made, the supplier can seek compound interest at three times the notified bank rate, and MSE Facilitation Councils can adjudicate claims through the MSME Samadhaan route.

### Who must file MSME Form 1?

Specified companies and LLPs whose supplier dues remain unpaid beyond forty-five days file a half-yearly return of delayed payments to micro and small enterprises, by the due dates notified for the April-September and October-March halves.

### Does Udyam registration expire?

No. The Udyam certificate carries lifetime validity for the enterprise and its PAN. However, classification is self-declared on investment and turnover, so figures must be updated as the business grows to stay accurate.
