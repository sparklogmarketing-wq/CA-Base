> An audit trail in accounting software is a system-generated, non-disablable edit log that records every transaction and every subsequent change to the books of account, with the date of each change. Under the proviso to Rule 3 of the Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014, it is mandatory for every company from FY 2023-24.

## Why the MCA Introduced the Audit Trail Rule

Books of account maintained in electronic mode can be altered without leaving a trace, which weakens reliance on digital records during audits, investigations, due diligence and valuations. To close this gap, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs amended the Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014 to require audit-trail functionality in the accounting software used by companies. The requirement applies from FY 2023-24 — that is, for financial years commencing on or after 1 April 2023 — and covers books of account kept in electronic mode under section 128 of the Companies Act 2013.

## What the Audit Trail in Accounting Software Must Record

The proviso to Rule 3 sets out four specific expectations. The table below translates each requirement into practice.

| Statutory requirement | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Audit log of each transaction | Every entry created in the books carries a system-generated log that remains in the records. |
| Edit log of every change | Any modification — including prior-period corrections — is logged along with the date on which the change was made. |
| Audit trail cannot be disabled | The feature must stay active whenever the software is used; switching it off is not permitted. |
| Preservation with the books | The trail is retained as part of the electronic books for the statutory retention period and verified by the auditor. |

## How Auditors Report Under Rule 11(g)

From FY 2023-24, clause (g) of Rule 11 of the Companies (Accounts) Rules requires the statutory auditor to state in the audit report whether the audit trail has been preserved by the company as per the statutory requirements for record retention. Where the feature was absent, disabled mid-year, or logs were not preserved, the auditor can qualify or comment in the report — a visible red flag for lenders and investors. The company also risks consequences for non-compliance with section 128, which mandates books that give a true and fair view. ## Who Must Comply — and Who Is Not Directly Covered

All companies registered under the Companies Act 2013 must comply, regardless of turnover, paid-up capital or whether they are small companies or one-person companies. LLPs, partnership firms, proprietors and professionals are not directly covered by this mandate. Even so, banks, investors and acquirers increasingly expect clean edit logs everywhere, and groups often standardise so that non-corporate entities using software with an audit trail feed reliable data into consolidated reporting.

## How to Choose Accounting Software With a Compliant Audit Trail

Selecting accounting software is now a statutory decision, not just an operational one. Use the criteria below when evaluating options, and ask vendors to confirm audit-trail behaviour in writing.

| Selection criterion | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Native, non-disablable audit trail | The edit log must be built in — not an optional add-on that administrators can bypass. |
| Tamper-evident, exportable logs | Logs should be exportable for auditors and ideally stored separately from day-to-day users. |
| Restricted user rights | Named logins, maker-checker controls and limited admin rights reduce unauthorised edits. |
| Statutory readiness | GST invoicing and e-invoicing, TDS returns and e-way bill integrations cut compliance rework. |
| Scalability | Multi-branch, multi-GSTIN and consolidated reporting should survive business growth. |
| Backup, security and uptime | Encrypted backups and access controls protect records you must keep for years. |
| Reporting and MIS quality | Clean ledgers and flexible reports feed decisions, not just compliance. |

## Implementation Checklist for Companies

- Obtain written vendor confirmation that the audit trail cannot be disabled in your version.
- Enable the audit trail from the start of the financial year — or immediately if the year is running.
- Restrict administrator rights and map every user to a named login.
- Post a test entry, edit it, and verify the edit log captures the change with its date.
- Take periodic backups; electronic books must be retained for at least the eight preceding financial years.
- Brief the statutory auditor and produce edit logs for verification under Rule 11(g).
- Fold the check into your annual [ROC compliance](/roc-compliance) calendar so it is never missed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the audit trail mandatory for all companies?

Yes. From FY 2023-24, every company using accounting software must use software with a non-disablable audit trail, irrespective of size, paid-up capital or turnover.

### Does the audit trail rule apply to LLPs and proprietorships?

Not directly. The mandate sits in the Companies (Accounts) Rules, so it binds companies. LLPs, firms and individuals are still encouraged to maintain audit trails as good governance.

### What happens if the audit trail was not enabled?

The statutory auditor may qualify or comment in the audit report under Rule 11(g), and the company risks consequences for non-compliance with section 128 of the Companies Act, 2013.

### How long must audit trail records be preserved?

Books of account in electronic mode must be preserved for not less than eight financial years preceding the year in question, and the audit trail forms part of these records.

### Can the audit trail be switched off at any time?

No. The rule expressly requires accounting software that ensures the audit trail cannot be disabled while the software is in use.
