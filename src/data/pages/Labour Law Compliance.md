## Shops and Establishments Registration in {state}

Every commercial establishment — offices, retail stores, clinics, warehouses and IT firms — must register under the {state} Shops and Commercial Establishments Act within the prescribed time from commencing business. The registration records the employer, nature of business and employee strength, must be displayed at the premises, and requires amendment on changes such as a new branch or a change of management. Establishments also maintain records of working hours, weekly holidays, leave and wages, which inspectors can call for at any time.

## Contract Labour Compliance under CLRA

The Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act 1970 applies where an establishment or contractor engages 20 or more contract workers. The obligations split three ways:

| Party | Obligation under CLRA |
|---|---|
| Principal employer | Register the establishment; ensure the contractor holds a valid licence; verify wage payment and benefit contributions |
| Contractor | Obtain and renew the licence; pay wages; maintain registers and issue employment cards |
| Establishment records | Return filings, wage registers and proof of statutory contributions by the contractor |

Gaps here — an expired contractor licence, unverified PF remittance by a manpower agency — surface as principal-employer liability during inspections and disputes, which is why labour law compliance services build verification into the monthly cycle.

## Wages, Bonus and Statutory Records

- **Minimum wages** — employers must pay at least the rates notified for the scheduled employment and skill level, revised periodically with dearness allowance
- **Payment of Bonus Act 1965** — employees drawing up to Rs 21,000 a month with 30 working days in the year are eligible; bonus runs from 8.33% to 20% of annual salary, computed on salary subject to the statutory calculation ceiling
- **Registers and records** — wage registers, leave records, attendance and overtime records under the applicable Acts and rules, maintained in the prescribed format

## The Four Labour Codes: Status and Preparation

India's labour law is being consolidated into four codes: the **Code on Wages 2019**, and the **Industrial Relations Code 2020**, the **Code on Social Security 2020** and the **Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code 2020**. All four stand enacted by Parliament; they take effect from dates to be notified by the government, and until then the existing statutes continue to apply.

For employers, the codes will rationalise registrations and returns and widen the uniform definition of wages — which changes the computation base for PF, gratuity and bonus. We track the transition and re-map registers, contracts and payroll configurations as notifications issue.

## POSH Compliance for Employers

Under the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act 2013, workplaces with 10 or more employees must constitute an Internal Committee with the prescribed composition, adopt and display a written policy, conduct periodic awareness programmes, maintain confidential records of complaints and their disposal, and file an annual report. Non-compliance attracts monetary penalty and, on repeated default, licence consequences.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which establishments need shops and establishments registration?

Nearly every commercial premises — offices, retail outlets, clinics and IT firms — must register under the {state} Shops and Commercial Establishments Act within the prescribed time from commencing business, and keep the certificate displayed at the premises.

### When is CLRA registration required?

Where 20 or more contract workers are engaged, the principal employer must register the establishment and the contractor must hold a licence. Engaging contract labour without these exposes the business to penalties and work stoppage.

### What is the status of the four labour codes?

The Code on Wages 2019 and the three 2020 codes stand enacted; they apply from dates to be notified. Until then, existing statutes — minimum wages, bonus, CLRA and allied Acts — continue to govern employers.

### Who is eligible for bonus under the Payment of Bonus Act?

Employees drawing up to Rs 21,000 a month who have worked 30 days in the year. Bonus runs from 8.33 percent to 20 percent of annual salary, computed on salary subject to the statutory calculation ceiling.

### Is POSH compliance mandatory for small offices?

Workplaces with 10 or more employees must constitute an Internal Committee; smaller workplaces fall under the Local Committee. Every employer must maintain a written policy, display it and file the annual return.
