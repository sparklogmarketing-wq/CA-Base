> Public sector audit services cover statutory and supplementary audits of government companies and PSUs, branch audits of PSU units, and audits of local bodies and autonomous institutions, conducted under the framework of the Companies Act 2013 and the C&AG (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act 1971 by empanelled chartered accountant firms.

## What Do Public Sector Audit Services Cover?

Public sector audit is a distinct discipline: the auditor is answerable both to the shareholders under company law and to Parliament and state legislatures through the C&AG. Our public sector audit services cover:

- Statutory audit of government companies as C&AG-appointed auditors under Section 139(5), working within C&AG directions issued under Section 143(5)
- Branch audits of PSU units and depots under Section 143(8)
- Audits of corporations, boards and local bodies under the C&AG (DPC) Act 1971 framework
- Supporting reviews and compliance certifications for autonomous bodies, universities and co-operative institutions
- Long Form Audit Reports (LFAR) and management letters with prioritised recommendations

Each engagement is planned against the entity's accounting framework — Ind AS, AS or the double-entry requirements of the governing statute — and its sector-specific regulations.

## Who Appoints the Auditor for a Government Company?

For a government company, the C&AG — not the shareholders — appoints the statutory auditor under Section 139(5) of the Companies Act 2013. Appointment flows through the C&AG's empanelment process, which evaluates firm experience, manpower and past performance. The appointed firm audits according to C&AG directives on scope, sampling and reporting, and must preserve workpapers for C&AG inspection. The C&AG retains the right to conduct a supplementary audit under Section 143(6) and to comment on the accounts or exercise test audit powers under Section 143(7) — so the quality bar for psu audit work is set higher than an ordinary statutory audit, and deficiencies surface at the national level.

## How PSU Audits Differ from Private Company Audits

| Aspect | Public sector audit | Private company audit |
|---|---|---|
| Appointment | By the C&AG (Section 139(5)) or under the C&AG (DPC) Act 1971 | By members at the AGM (Section 139) |
| Governing framework | Companies Act 2013 plus C&AG directives and DPC Act | Companies Act 2013 and auditing standards |
| Additional reporting | LFAR, C&AG-format certificates, compliance with government orders | Audit report, CARO 2020 where applicable |
| Review mechanism | Supplementary and test audit by the C&AG; comments laid before the legislature | Regulator and peer review on a sample basis |
| Follow-up | paras tracked by administrative ministries and legislative committees | Management and audit committee tracking |

Effective public sector audit work depends on disciplined execution: engagement teams briefed on C&AG reporting formats, sampling as directed, documented vouching, and reports written so that every para carries evidence, criterion, cause and effect. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who appoints the auditor of a government company?

The Comptroller and Auditor General of India (C&AG) appoints the statutory auditor of a government company under Section 139(5) of the Companies Act 2013, generally from chartered accountant firms empanelled with it. The appointed firm audits under C&AG directions.

### What is a supplementary audit?

Under Section 143(6), the C&AG may conduct a supplementary audit of a government company's accounts after the statutory audit to test the correctness and sufficiency of the work performed, and comments on the accounts under Section 143(7).

### What is a PSU branch audit?

Many government companies operate through units and depots. Under Section 143(8) read with Section 143(5), the C&AG may direct that a branch be audited by an appointed chartered accountant firm, whose report feeds the company's main audit.

### Do PSUs need internal audit?

Listed PSUs must comply with Section 138 thresholds like any listed company, and good-governance practice expects a structured internal audit across PSU operations. C&AG supplementary audits routinely test whether internal controls and internal audit findings were acted upon.

### What reports does a public sector audit produce?

Besides the statutory audit report under Section 143, PSU engagements typically require a Long Form Audit Report (LFAR), compliance certificates, and responses to C&AG directives — with results feeding C&AG comments on the accounts.
