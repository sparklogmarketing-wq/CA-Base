## Why Foreign Companies Invest in {country}

{country} combines a large consumer market, a deep technology talent pool and steadily liberalised FDI rules. Most sectors accept 100% foreign ownership under the automatic route. The practical challenge is not permission but process: FEMA reporting, sectoral conditions and post-incorporation registrations carry strict timelines, and errors are expensive to unwind — which is where a compliance-first entry plan pays for itself.

## Choose the Right Entry Structure

The structure determines your approvals, tax profile and operating freedom. Our detailed comparison of [FDI in companies and LLPs](/fdi-in-company-llp) covers the ownership rules; the summary below shows how the main routes differ.

| Structure | Legal position | Approval route | Best suited for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wholly-owned subsidiary | Separate private limited company in {country}; parent holds 100% | Automatic route in most sectors | Full operations, hiring, long-term market presence |
| Joint venture | {country} company with a local partner | Automatic, subject to sectoral caps | Market access requiring local relationships |
| Branch office | Extension of the foreign parent, not a separate company | RBI via AD Category-I bank | Trading, research and representative work by established parents |
| Liaison office | Representational only; cannot earn revenue in {country} | RBI via AD Category-I bank | Market study and brand presence before committing capital |

For most groups, the [wholly-owned subsidiary](/subsidiary-entity) is the default: clean liability separation, unrestricted commercial activity and eligibility for domestic-company tax rates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best structure to invest in {country}?

Most foreign companies choose a wholly-owned private limited subsidiary because it offers limited liability, 100% FDI under the automatic route in most sectors and a clean tax profile. Branch and liaison offices suit limited or representational objectives and need RBI approval through an AD Category-I bank.

### How long does {country} market entry take?

A wholly-owned subsidiary typically incorporates in two to four weeks once parent documents are attested and digital signatures are ready. Branch and liaison offices depend on AD bank and RBI processing. Budget extra time for post-incorporation registrations such as PAN, GST and bank accounts.

### Can a foreign company own 100% of a subsidiary in {country}?

Yes, in most sectors FDI up to 100% is permitted under the automatic route with no prior approval. A few sectors carry caps or require Government approval, and investments from land-bordering countries face additional scrutiny under Press Note 3. We confirm the position for your sector before filing.

### What are the first reporting deadlines after FDI comes in?

Report the inward remittance to the AD bank, file Form FC-GPR within 30 days of allotment of shares, and file the annual FLA return by July 15 each year. Missing these timelines invites FEMA compounding or penalty consequences.

### Do branch and liaison offices pay tax in {country}?

Branch offices are taxed on Indian-source profits at rates applicable to foreign companies. A liaison office confined to representational activities earns no taxable income, but must still file returns and an annual performance report with its AD bank.
