## International Tax Updates India Track Under BEPS 2.0

The OECD's Two-Pillar Solution has redefined the agenda. Pillar One proposes reallocating a share of residual profit of the very largest multinationals to market jurisdictions, reducing reliance on traditional permanent establishment rules. Pillar Two introduces a 15 percent global minimum tax for groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more, operating through the income inclusion rule, the undertaxed profits rule and domestic minimum top-up taxes.

India has engaged closely with the framework but has not yet legislated Pillar Two into domestic law; in the interim, India's focus remains on APA and MAP capacity, robust transfer pricing enforcement, and recent rollbacks of unilateral measures such as the equalisation levies, which reduce friction with treaty partners. The practical takeaway: the pace of international tax updates India now tracks means even mid-sized groups with regional parents should model Pillar Two exposure one hop up the ownership chain.

## MLI Treaty Updates and Relief on Cross-Border Income

India is a signatory to the Multilateral Instrument, and its covered tax agreements have been modified to embed the BEPS minimum standards: a principal purpose test, upgraded limitation-on-benefits provisions and anti-fragmentation rules for permanent establishments. In practice, benefit claims increasingly require demonstrable commercial substance — board presence, employees and risk-bearing capacity — not just a paper intermediary. Where double taxation still arises, relief depends on correctly pairing treaty relief with the foreign tax credit regime; our [DTAA and foreign tax credit guide](/dtaa-foreign-tax-credit) sets out the mechanics, and the [MLI-driven approach to foreign subsidiary structures](/foreign-subsidiary-tax-planning) explains how repatriation policy should adapt.

## Master File and CbCR Thresholds for Indian Groups

India follows the three-tier documentation architecture recommended under BEPS Action 13: local file, master file and country-by-country reporting. Master file and CbCR obligations are threshold-driven, so a group's documentation calendar should be reset whenever revenue or transaction values cross the limits:

| Documentation layer | Applies when (broadly) | Reporting vehicle |
|---|---|---|
| Local file | International transactions above prescribed limits; standard Rule 10D documentation | Supports the accountant's report in Form 3CEB |
| Master file | Group consolidated revenue above Rs 250 crore and aggregate international transactions above Rs 50 crore | Form 3CEAA |
| CbCR | International group's consolidated revenue of Rs 5,500 crore or more | Form 3CEAD |

Thresholds are tested against the preceding accounting year, and Indian constituents of foreign groups may carry notification and filing duties even when the parent files elsewhere. ## FEMA Liberalisation: ODI Rules, LRS and GIFT IFSC Funding

Exchange control has moved decisively toward liberalisation. The Overseas Investment regime comprehensively overhauled the 2004-era rules, replacing a list-based framework with a principles-based distinction between ODI, overseas portfolio investment and financial commitments — with simpler disinvestment, strategic-sector carve-outs and successive relaxations, including investment in IFSC-regulated vehicles. On the personal side, the LRS remittance limit stands at USD 250,000 per resident individual per financial year, with prescribed tax-collection rates applying to specified categories and education remittances treated more favourably — a trend visible in rising outbound volumes. Meanwhile, GIFT IFSC has matured into a credible funding jurisdiction, with IFSCA-regulated fund management entities and treasury units anchoring group structures that keep capital flows onshore yet globally compatible. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is BEPS 2.0 in simple terms?

BEPS 2.0 is the OECD's Two-Pillar Solution. Pillar One reallocates taxing rights over the largest multinationals to market jurisdictions; Pillar Two imposes a 15 percent global minimum tax on groups with revenue of EUR 750 million or more. India has not yet enacted Pillar Two domestically.

### What are the master file and CbCR thresholds in India?

Master file applies where group consolidated revenue exceeds Rs 250 crore and international transactions exceed Rs 50 crore, filed in Form 3CEAA. CbCR applies where the group's consolidated revenue is Rs 5,500 crore or more, filed in Form 3CEAD.

### How has the MLI changed India's tax treaties?

Through the MLI, India has adopted the principal purpose test, revised limitation-on-benefits rules and anti-fragmentation permanent establishment provisions in covered tax agreements, making treaty shopping harder and aligning treaty benefits with genuine business substance.

### How much can a resident individual remit abroad under LRS?

Resident individuals may remit up to USD 250,000 per financial year under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme for permitted current and capital account transactions. Prescribed tax-collection rates apply to specified remittance categories, with education-related remittances treated more favourably.

### What changed with the FEMA ODI rules?

The Overseas Investment framework replaced the 2004-era regulations with a principles-based regime distinguishing ODI, OPI and financial commitments. Successive relaxations have widened permitted destinations, simplified disinvestment and enabled investment in IFSC-regulated funds.

### Why are Indian groups using GIFT IFSC for cross-border funding?

GIFT IFSC offers an Indian-jurisdiction base for fund management entities, treasuries and holding structures, combining domestic regulatory comfort with offshore-style flexibility for raising and deploying cross-border capital, subject to IFSCA and FEMA conditions.
