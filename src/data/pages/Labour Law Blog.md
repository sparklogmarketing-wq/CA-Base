Indian employment regulation is undergoing its largest reset in decades: twenty-nine statutes folding into four codes, gig workers entering social security, filings moving online. The employer risk now is stale payroll structures and unreconciled portals — the focus of this {brand} column.

## The Four Labour Codes: One Direction, Phased Rollout

The Code on Wages 2019, the Industrial Relations Code 2020, the Code on Social Security 2020 and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code 2020 together replace twenty-nine central enactments. Provisions commence as the Centre and states notify rules, with phased implementation. Recent rule-framing cycles show wider registration, simpler returns and a digital-records posture. Treat the codes as the baseline for HR policy and payroll structure.

## The Wage Code: A Single Definition of Wages

The wage code's key idea: one definition of wages across statutes, with exclusions capped so basic wages cannot fall below roughly half of total remuneration. Because provident fund and gratuity key off this base, compliant structures carry a higher statutory cost, and the fifteen-days'-wages gratuity computation runs on a larger base. ## Social Security for Gig and Platform Workers

The Code on Social Security brought gig and platform workers into statute, contemplating registration, scheme-based benefits and aggregator contributions on turnover, capped as a share of payouts. States are legislating too, and e-Shram registration provides the identity infrastructure. ## POSH Compliance: Beyond the Policy Document

The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 obliges every workplace with ten or more employees to constitute an Internal Committee, conduct time-bound inquiries and file annual reports. Scrutiny has sharpened: what is tested is whether committees were functional, members trained and complaints resolved in time — not merely whether a policy exists. ## PF, ESI and the Digital Compliance Shift

PF and ESI administration is portal-first: UAN-linked records, Aadhaar validation, electronic challan-cum-return filings and online claims, with data-led inspections. Every payroll run must reconcile with the portal: counts, wage months and contribution splits must match, because mismatches trigger scrutiny. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the four labour codes in India?

The Code on Wages 2019, the Industrial Relations Code 2020, the Code on Social Security 2020 and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code 2020. Together they consolidate twenty-nine central labour laws, effective as rules are notified.

### How does the wage code change employer costs?

A single definition of wages caps exclusions so basic wages form a substantial share of pay. Provident fund and gratuity accrue on this broader base, raising cost where structures were basic-light.

### Are gig workers covered by social security in India?

The Code on Social Security recognises gig and platform workers, contemplating aggregator contributions alongside registration-based benefits and e-Shram-linked coverage. Implementation is expanding but not yet uniform.

### Which establishments need an Internal Committee under POSH?

Every workplace with ten or more employees must constitute an Internal Committee, complete time-bound inquiries and file an annual report. Scrutiny of committee functioning has increased.

### What has digitisation changed in EPF and ESI compliance?

Electronic challan-cum-return filings, UAN-linked records, Aadhaar validation and online claims are the norm. Inspections draw on digital data, so payroll ledgers must reconcile with portal records.

### Do the labour codes apply to establishments in Karnataka?

Labour is on the Concurrent List, so the codes need corresponding state rules for day-to-day enforcement. Karnataka employers should track both Centre notifications and state rule-making.
