> A tax audit under section 44AB of the Income-tax Act 1961 is the independent examination and certification of the books of account of a taxpayer carrying on business or profession, conducted by a chartered accountant. The audit results in prescribed forms being e-filed, confirming that reported turnover, deductions and taxes paid are properly supported and computed.

## Who Needs a Tax Audit under Section 44AB?

Section 44AB requires specified taxpayers to get their accounts audited by a chartered accountant. Applicability depends on turnover or gross receipts and, for businesses, on the share of digital transactions:

| Assessee category | Threshold | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Business (default limit) | Turnover above Rs 1 crore | Applies to all persons carrying on business |
| Business (enhanced limit) | Turnover above Rs 10 crore | Cash receipts and cash payments must not exceed 5% of total gross receipts and total payments respectively — the tax audit limit 10 crore applies only when the 95% digital test is met |
| Profession | Gross receipts above Rs 50 lakh | Doctors, chartered accountants, architects, consultants and other specified professionals |

Special situations — such as a business eligible for presumptive taxation under Sections 44AD or 44ADA that opts out, or declares income below the presumptive level — can still trigger audit. We assess applicability against your actual transaction mix every year before the test bites.

## Which Forms Apply: 3CA-3CD or 3CB-3CD?

| Situation | Applicable form |
|---|---|
| Accounts already audited under any other law (for example, a company under a Companies Act audit) | Form 3CA plus Form 3CD |
| No other audit required under any law | Form 3CB plus Form 3CD |

Form 3CD is the substantive statement of particulars; 3CA and 3CB are the covering certificates signed by the auditor. Forms are e-filed through the income-tax e-filing portal and verified with a UDIN, and the assessee must accept the submission from their login.

## What Does Form 3CD Examine?

Form 3CD runs through dozens of prescribed clauses. Typical areas include: turnover computation and its reconciliation with GST returns; depreciation and additional depreciation; disallowances under Section 40(a)(i), Section 40A(2) and Section 40A(3) cash payments; related-party transactions; loans accepted or repaid in cash under Sections 269SS and 269T; amounts disallowed under Section 43B; and payments to micro and small suppliers. Late-fee and interest particulars, TDS defaults and dividends distributed also feature. Careful pre-year-end review of these clauses is far cheaper than rectifying disclosures after filing.

## Tax Audit Due Dates and E-Filing

| Compliance | Due date |
|---|---|
| Tax audit report (Form 3CA-3CD or 3CB-3CD) | 30 September of the assessment year — one month before the return due date |
| Return of income for audit cases | 31 October of the assessment year |
| Belated or revised return | 31 December of the assessment year |

Because the tax audit due date falls a month before the ITR deadline, audit planning must start well before year end. Our [ITR filing](/itr-filing) team files the audit-case return using the submitted report, and discrepancies between GST turnover and books are reconciled before submission.

## Consequences of Not Filing or Late Filing

Non-filing or a defective tax audit report has real teeth:

- **Penalty under Section 271B** — the lower of 0.5% of turnover or gross receipts, or Rs 1,50,000. The penalty can be waived only for reasonable cause under Section 273B, which tribunals construe narrowly.
- **Best judgment assessment under Section 144** — the assessing officer may estimate income where the return is not filed or books are not audited as required.
- **Interest and loss restrictions** — tax with interest accrues, and filing after the due date strips carry-forward of business losses and depreciation.
- **Scrutiny exposure** — a late or amended 3CD invites queries; our [scrutiny and notice support](/income-tax-scrutiny-notices) team handles follow-ups where they arise.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the tax audit limit?

For business, the default limit is turnover above Rs 1 crore, raised to Rs 10 crore where at least 95% of receipts and payments are digital. For profession, the limit is gross receipts above Rs 50 lakh.

### What is the due date for filing the tax audit report?

One month before the due date of the return of income — 30 September of the assessment year for most audit cases. The ITR itself is due by 31 October.

### When do Forms 3CA-3CD apply versus 3CB-3CD?

Form 3CA-3CD applies where accounts are already audited under another law, such as the Companies Act. Form 3CB-3CD applies where no other statutory audit applies, as with most sole proprietors and partnership firms.

### What is the penalty for not filing a tax audit report?

Under Section 271B, the penalty is the lower of 0.5% of turnover or gross receipts, or Rs 1,50,000. A best judgment assessment under Section 144 may follow, with interest on unpaid tax.

### Can statutory audit and tax audit be combined?

Yes. For companies already under a Companies Act audit, the same engagement supports Form 3CD. The tax auditor e-files 3CA-3CD alongside the statutory audit report, reducing duplication and cost.

### Do I need a tax audit if I use presumptive taxation?

Generally no, while turnover stays within Section 44AD limits and income is declared at the presumptive rate. Taxpayers who opt out of presumptive taxation in certain years may still require an audit — we confirm applicability for your facts.
